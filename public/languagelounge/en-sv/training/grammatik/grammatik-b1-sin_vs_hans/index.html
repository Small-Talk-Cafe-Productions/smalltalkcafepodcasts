Grundregeln: 'sin / sitt / sina' syftar på satsens subjekt

Core rule: 'sin/sitt/sina' refers back to the subject of the SAME clause

📌 Grammatiknotering

When a third-person subject (han, hon, den, det, man, någon, de, or any noun) owns the object within the same clause, Swedish MUST use the reflexive possessive 'sin' (en), 'sitt' (ett), or 'sina' (plural). Using 'hans/hennes/deras' changes the meaning to 'someone else's'. ⚠️ Using 'hans', 'hennes', or 'deras' to refer to the subject's own possession (Anna tappade hennes mobil), which tells a Swedish listener the phone belongs to another woman.

Exempel

Erik älskar sin fru, men Peter dansar ofta med hans fru på fester.

Erik loves his (own) wife, but Peter often dances with his (Erik's) wife at parties.

Böjning efter det ägda ordet: sin (en), sitt (ett), sina (plural)

Agreement with the possessed noun, NOT the owner

📌 Grammatiknotering

'Sin/sitt/sina' agrees in gender and number with the thing owned, regardless of whether the subject is singular ('hon') or plural ('de'): 'sin bil' (en-word), 'sitt hus' (ett-word), 'sina vänner' (plural). By contrast, 'hans/hennes/deras' never inflect. ⚠️ Choosing 'sina' just because the owners are plural (De sålde sina hus when they only own one house; it must be 'De sålde sitt hus').

Exempel

De sålde sitt gamla hus, sin båt och alla sina möbler innan flytten.

They sold their old house, their boat, and all their furniture before the move.

Förbud mot 'sin/sitt/sina' som del av subjektet!

'sin/sitt/sina' can NEVER be part of the clause's subject

📌 Grammatiknotering

Because 'sin' must refer back to the subject of its own clause, it can never stand inside the subject phrase itself—even in compound subjects with 'och' (e.g., 'Hon och hennes bror'). In subjects, ALWAYS use 'hans/hennes/dess/deras'. ⚠️ Using 'sin' in a coordinated subject (Johan och sin fru kom på besök — must be 'Johan och hans fru') or as the subject of an 'att'-clause (Hon sa att sin bror var sjuk — must be 'att hennes bror').

Exempel

Lisa besökte oss igår, och hennes man följde också med på middagen.

Lisa visited us yesterday, and her husband also came along to dinner.

Satsgränsen: Huvudsats vs. bisats

Clause boundaries: 'sin' does not cross into a new finite subordinate clause

📌 Grammatiknotering

A finite subordinate clause (introduced by 'att', 'eftersom', 'när', 'som', etc.) has its own grammatical subject. Therefore, if the possessive inside the subordinate clause refers back to the main clause's subject—and is NOT the subject of the subordinate clause—check who the local subject is! ⚠️ Using 'sin' inside a subordinate clause to refer to the main-clause subject (Hon hoppas att vi kan passa sin hund — must be 'hennes hund' because 'vi' is the subject of the 'att'-clause).

Exempel

Eva vet att hennes chef uppskattar sitt arbete på avdelningen.

Eva knows that her (Eva's) boss appreciates his/her own (the boss's) work in the department.

'Sin' i infinitivfraser och objekt med infinitiv

Using 'sin' vs 'hans/hennes' in infinitive constructions

📌 Grammatiknotering

An infinitive phrase has an implied (logical) subject. 'Sin' refers to the logical subject of the infinitive. In 'Jag såg honom städa sitt rum', 'honom' is the logical subject of 'städa', so 'sitt' refers to him! ⚠️ Misidentifying who performs the infinitive action, leading to ambiguity between the main subject and the object.

Exempel

Vi hörde grannen ropa på sin hund i parken sent i går kväll.

We heard the neighbor calling his/her (own) dog in the park late last night.

'Sin/sitt/sina' med opersonligt 'man', 'någon' och 'ingen'

Mandatory 'sin/sitt/sina' with indefinite pronouns 'man, någon, var och en'

📌 Grammatiknotering

When the subject is an indefinite third-person pronoun such as 'man' (one), 'någon' (someone), 'ingen' (no one), 'alla' (everyone), or 'var och en' (each one), the possessive MUST be 'sin/sitt/sina' (there is no 'mans' possessive in this role). ⚠️ Using 'ens' as a direct object possessive when 'man' is the subject (Man måste ta hand om ens hälsa — must be 'sin hälsa'; 'ens' is only used when 'man' is NOT the subject of that clause).

Exempel

Man måste alltid ta ansvar för sina egna handlingar och beslut.

One must always take responsibility for one's own actions and decisions.

Objekt vs. subjekt i samma sats (Dubbeltydighet)

Disambiguating subject vs. object ownership within the same clause

📌 Grammatiknotering

When a clause contains both a third-person subject and a third-person object (e.g., 'Han gav Olle...'), 'sin' strictly refers to the subject ('Han'), while 'hans' refers to the object ('Olle') or a third party. ⚠️ Assuming 'hennes' is ambiguous like English 'her'; in Swedish, the choice between 'sin' and 'hennes' completely resolves who owns the car.

Exempel

Advokaten lämnade tillbaka klienten hans originaldokument efter mötet.

The lawyer returned to the client his (the client's) original documents after the meeting.

Icke-mänskliga ägare: 'sin/sitt/sina' vs. 'dess'

Inanimate and animal possessives: 'sin/sitt/sina' vs. 'dess'

📌 Grammatiknotering

For animals, organizations, cities, or things (en/ett-words), 'sin/sitt/sina' is used when they are the subject of the clause. When they are NOT the subject, formal Swedish uses 'dess' (its). ⚠️ Using 'dess' when the inanimate noun is the subject of the clause (Företaget ökade dess vinst instead of 'sin vinst').

Exempel

Stockholm är känt för sin vackra skärgård, och dess historia sträcker sig långt tillbaka.

Stockholm is famous for its beautiful archipelago, and its history stretches far back.

Jämförelsefraser med 'som' och 'än'

Elliptical comparisons with 'som' (as) and 'än' (than)

📌 Grammatiknotering

In comparisons like 'Hon är lika gammal som sin bror' vs 'Hon är äldre än hans bror', if the phrase after 'som/än' is felt as a reduced clause where the noun is the subject ('än hennes bror [är]'), 'hans/hennes' is traditional, though 'sin' is also very common when no verb follows. ⚠️ Using 'sin' when a finite verb explicitly follows in the comparison clause (än sin far gjorde — since 'far' is the subject of 'gjorde', it must be 'hans far').

Exempel

Han tjänar idag betydligt mer pengar än vad hans far gjorde i samma ålder.

He earns significantly more money today than what his father did at the same age.

Förstärkning med 'egen / eget / egna'

Combining 'sin/hans' with 'egen, eget, egna' (own)

📌 Grammatiknotering

To emphasize ownership ('his very own'), Swedish places 'egen' (en), 'eget' (ett), or 'egna' (plural) after 'sin/hans/min'. Crucially, after a possessive, 'egen' inflects STRONG (egen/eget/egna), NOT weak (-a), and the noun remains indefinite! ⚠️ Using weak '-a' in singular after a possessive (sin egna bil or sitt egna hus in standard written Swedish instead of 'sin egen bil' / 'sitt eget hus').

Exempel

Hon startade sitt eget arkitektkontor och anställde sina egna medarbetare.

She started her own architecture firm and hired her own employees.
