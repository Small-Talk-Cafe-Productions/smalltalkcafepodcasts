Verben mit Dativ: Einführung

Verbs that Take Dative: Introduction

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In German, some verbs require their object to be in the dative case. This means the person or thing receiving the action (the indirect object) will have dative endings for articles, pronouns, and sometimes nouns. These verbs answer the question 'Wem?' (To whom?).

Beispielsatz

Ich helfe meinem Freund.

I help my friend.

Die Dativ-Frage: Wem?

The Dative Question: To whom?

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To identify the dative object in a sentence, you ask the question 'Wem?' (To whom?). The answer to this question will always be in the dative case. This question helps differentiate the dative object from the nominative subject ('Wer?') or the accusative direct object ('Wen oder Was?').

Beispielsatz

Wem hilfst du? – Ich helfe meiner Schwester.

Whom do you help? – I help my sister.

Dativ Personalpronomen

Dative Personal Pronouns

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When the dative object is a personal pronoun, it changes its form significantly. These forms are essential to memorize as they are used very frequently. The dative personal pronouns are: mir, dir, ihm, ihr, uns, euch, ihnen, Ihnen.

Beispielsatz

Kannst du mir helfen?

Can you help me?

Dativ Bestimmte Artikel

Dative Definite Articles

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Definite articles ('the') also change their form in the dative case, depending on the gender and number of the noun. They are: dem (masculine), der (feminine), dem (neuter), den (plural). A common mnemonic for the endings is 'M-R-M-N'.

Beispielsatz

Ich antworte dem Mann und der Frau.

I answer the man and the woman.

Dativ Unbestimmte Artikel & Kein-Artikel

Dative Indefinite Articles & 'Kein' Article

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Indefinite articles ('a/an') and the 'kein' article ('no/not a') also take specific dative endings. They are: einem (masculine/neuter), einer (feminine), keinen (plural). The 'kein' article follows the same pattern as 'ein' but also has a plural form.

Beispielsatz

Ich gratuliere einem Freund zum Geburtstag.

I congratulate a friend on his birthday.

Dativ Nomenendungen: Plural -n

Dative Noun Endings: Plural -n

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A crucial rule for dative plural nouns is that they receive an additional '-n' ending, unless they already end in -s or -n. This applies after 'den' or 'keinen' and also after dative prepositions. It's a consistent rule that helps identify plural dative nouns.

Beispielsatz

Ich helfe den Studenten bei der Prüfungsvorbereitung.

I help the students with their exam preparation.

Dativ Verb: helfen (to help)

Dative Verb: helfen

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'helfen' always takes a dative object. The person being helped is in the dative case. It's one of the most common dative verbs and serves as an excellent example to remember the dative requirement.

Beispielsatz

Ich helfe dir gern bei deinen Hausaufgaben.

I gladly help you with your homework.

Dativ Verb: danken (to thank)

Dative Verb: danken

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'danken' always takes a dative object. The person being thanked is in the dative case. You can also specify for what you are thanking, usually with 'für + Akkusativ'.

Beispielsatz

Ich danke Ihnen für Ihre Geduld.

I thank you (formal) for your patience.

Dativ Verb: gehören (to belong to)

Dative Verb: gehören

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'gehören' always takes a dative object. The person or entity to whom something belongs is in the dative case. It's important not to confuse this with 'haben' (to have), as their sentence structures differ significantly.

Beispielsatz

Das Auto gehört meinem Vater.

The car belongs to my father.

Dativ Verben: schmecken & gefallen (sensory)

Dative Verbs: to taste good to & to please/like (sensory)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Verbs like 'schmecken' (to taste good to) and 'gefallen' (to please, to like) take a dative object. The person who likes or enjoys something is in the dative case, while the thing liked is the nominative subject. This structure is often reversed compared to English.

Beispielsatz

Die Pizza schmeckt mir sehr gut. Das Bild gefällt ihr nicht.

The pizza tastes very good to me. She doesn't like the picture (The picture doesn't please her).

Dativ Verben: antworten & glauben (communication)

Dative Verbs: to answer & to believe (communication)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Verbs related to communication like 'antworten' (to answer) and 'glauben' (to believe) also take a dative object. The person being answered or believed is in the dative case. This applies whether you are answering a question or believing a person.

Beispielsatz

Ich antworte dem Kunden sofort. Glaubst du mir?

I answer the customer immediately. Do you believe me?

Dativ Verben: gratulieren & vertrauen (social)

Dative Verbs: to congratulate & to trust (social)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Verbs expressing social interactions such as 'gratulieren' (to congratulate) and 'vertrauen' (to trust) require a dative object. The person being congratulated or trusted is in the dative case. These verbs highlight the indirect nature of the action towards a person.

Beispielsatz

Wir gratulieren dem Brautpaar zur Hochzeit. Ich vertraue meinem besten Freund.

We congratulate the bridal couple on their wedding. I trust my best friend.

Dativ Verben: weh tun & fehlen (states)

Dative Verbs: to hurt & to miss/be missing (states)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Verbs describing physical or emotional states, like 'weh tun' (to hurt) and 'fehlen' (to miss, to be missing), take a dative object. The person experiencing the pain or absence is in the dative. The subject is often the thing causing the state.

Beispielsatz

Mein Kopf tut mir weh. Du fehlst mir sehr.

My head hurts (to me). I miss you very much (You are missing to me).

Dativ Verben: folgen, passen, zuhören

Dative Verbs: to follow, to fit/suit, to listen to

📌 Grammatiknotiz

More common dative verbs include 'folgen' (to follow), 'passen' (to fit, to suit), and 'zuhören' (to listen to). The person or thing being followed, suited, or listened to is in the dative case. These verbs emphasize the direction or recipient of the action.

Beispielsatz

Folgen Sie mir bitte. Die Hose passt mir nicht. Hörst du mir zu?

Please follow me. The pants don't fit me. Are you listening to me?

Verben mit Dativ und Akkusativ

Verbs with Dative and Accusative Objects

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some verbs take both a dative object (the recipient, usually a person) and an accusative object (the thing being given, told, or shown). The dative object answers 'Wem?', and the accusative object answers 'Wen oder Was?'. The dative object typically comes before the accusative object if both are nouns.

Beispielsatz

Ich gebe dem Kind einen Apfel. Er erzählt uns eine Geschichte.

I give the child an apple. He tells us a story.

Dativ Verben vs. Dativ Präpositionen

Dative Verbs vs. Dative Prepositions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

It's important to distinguish between verbs that inherently take a dative object (like 'helfen') and verbs that take a dative object because they are followed by a preposition that always takes dative (e.g., 'sprechen mit dem Freund'). In the latter case, the dative is governed by the preposition, not the verb itself.

Beispielsatz

Ich helfe dem Mann. (Dativ-Verb) vs. Ich spreche mit dem Mann. (Dativ-Präposition)

I help the man. (Dative verb) vs. I speak with the man. (Dative preposition)

Häufiger Fehler: Akkusativ statt Dativ

Common Error: Accusative instead of Dative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A very common mistake is to use the accusative case when a dative verb requires dative. This often happens because in English, the indirect object might appear as a direct object, or the verb structure is different. Always check if the verb is a dative verb and memorize its case requirement.

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Ich helfe den Mann. Richtig: Ich helfe dem Mann.

Wrong: I help the man (accusative). Right: I help the man (dative).

Häufiger Fehler: gehören vs. haben

Common Error: gehören vs. haben

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Learners often confuse 'gehören' (to belong to) with 'haben' (to have). While both express possession, 'gehören' uses the dative case for the owner, and the item owned is the nominative subject. 'Haben' uses the nominative for the owner and the accusative for the item owned. They are not interchangeable.

Beispielsatz

Das Buch gehört mir. (I own the book) vs. Ich habe das Buch. (I have the book)

The book belongs to me. vs. I have the book.

Weitere Dativ Verben (Liste)

Additional Dative Verbs (List)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Beyond the most common ones, many other verbs also take a dative object. It's best to learn these verbs with their required case. Examples include: begegnen (to meet by chance), passieren (to happen to), raten (to advise), verzeihen (to forgive), zustimmen (to agree with).

Beispielsatz

Ich bin gestern einem alten Freund begegnet. Was ist dir passiert?

I met an old friend yesterday. What happened to you?
