Weiterführende Relativsätze mit 'was' (Bezug auf den ganzen Satz)

Continuing Relative Clauses with 'was' (Referencing Whole Clause)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a relative clause refers back to the entire preceding statement (rather than a specific noun), it is introduced by 'was'. A comma is strictly mandatory. ⚠️ Using 'das' or 'welches' to refer back to an entire sentence (correct: 'was', NOT 'das').

Beispielsatz

Er kam ohne Entschuldigung eine Stunde zu spät, [was] mich außerordentlich ärgerte.

He arrived an hour late without an apology, [which] annoyed me extraordinarily.

Pronominaladverbien als Konnektoren ('worüber', 'womit', 'wovon')

Pronominal Adverbs as Continuing Connectors ('worüber', 'womit')

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When the verb in the continuing clause requires a preposition, 'wo(r)-' + preposition is used instead of 'was': 'worüber' (über was), 'womit' (mit was), 'wovon' (von was). ⚠️ Using preposition + 'was' ('über was er sich freute' is colloquial; formal German requires 'worüber').

Beispielsatz

Sie bot ihm unerwartet die Partnerschaft an, [worüber] er sich riesig freute.

She unexpectedly offered him partnership, [about which] he was tremendously happy.

'woraufhin' (Unmittelbare zeitliche und logische Folge)

'woraufhin' (Whereupon / In Response to Which)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Introduces a consequence or an immediate reaction directly triggered by the event in the main clause. Verb moves to clause end. ⚠️ Confusing 'woraufhin' (consecutive reaction) with 'worauf' (waiting for something: worauf er wartete).

Beispielsatz

Der Chef kritisierte die Pläne scharf, [woraufhin] der Abteilungsleiter kündigte.

The boss sharply criticized the plans, [whereupon] the department head resigned.

'weswegen / weshalb' (Relativische Begründung)

'weswegen / weshalb' (Which Is Why / For Which Reason)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Refers back to a preceding cause and introduces the motivated result as a subordinate clause (verb at end). ⚠️ Using main clause word order after 'weshalb' when it introduces a subordinate clause (finite verb must be at the end).

Beispielsatz

Der Zug hatte einen technischen Defekt, [weswegen] wir zwei Stunden warten mussten.

The train had a technical defect, [which is why] we had to wait two hours.

'wohingegen / während' (Adversative Gegenüberstellung)

'wohingegen / während' (Whereas / While - Contrast)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Highlights a direct, sharp contrast between two parallel facts. Subordinating conjunction; verb at clause end. ⚠️ Confusing temporal 'während' (while time passes) with purely contrastive 'wohingegen' (whereas).

Beispielsatz

In Großstädten steigen die Mieten rasant, [wohingegen] sie auf dem Land oft stagnieren.

In major cities rents are rising rapidly, [whereas] in rural areas they often stagnate.

'inwiefern / inwieweit' (Grad oder Art und Weise der Aussage)

'inwiefern / inwieweit' (To What Extent / How Far)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Introduces an indirect question or continuing clause elaborating on extent, mechanism, or validity. ⚠️ Using 'wie weit' as two separate words when functioning as a subordinating connector.

Beispielsatz

Der Bericht legt dar, [inwiefern] die neue Technologie den CO2-Ausstoß senken kann.

The report explains [to what extent] the new technology can reduce CO2 emissions.

Konsekutivsatz mit 'sodass / so dass' (Folge)

Consecutive Clause with 'sodass' (Consequence / Result)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Introduces an actual consequence of the main clause. The verb moves to the end of the clause. ⚠️ Placing 'sodass' at the beginning of a whole sentence; 'sodass' must follow a main clause introducing the cause.

Beispielsatz

Es schneite die ganze Nacht hindurch, [sodass] der Straßenverkehr völlig zusammenbrach.

It snowed all night long, [so that] road traffic broke down completely.

Graduierendes 'so + Adjektiv / Adverb ..., dass'

Graduating 'so + Adjective / Adverb ..., that'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The degree adverb 'so' sits in the main clause before an adjective/adverb, followed by a 'dass'-clause indicating the dramatic extent. ⚠️ Forgetting the comma before 'dass' in 'so ... dass' constructions.

Beispielsatz

Er war [so] müde, [dass] er während des Vortrags einschlief.

He was [so] tired [that] he fell asleep during the lecture.

Relativsatz nach Indefinitpronomen: 'alles / nichts / etwas / vieles, was'

Relative Clauses After Indefinite Pronouns ('alles, was...')

📌 Grammatiknotiz

After indefinite neuter pronouns (alles, nichts, etwas, vieles, einiges), the relative pronoun is ALWAYS 'was' (NEVER 'das'). ⚠️ Using 'das' after 'alles' ('alles, das ich weiß' is an error; it MUST be 'alles, was ich weiß').

Beispielsatz

Das ist [alles, was] ich über diesen Vorfall weiß.

That is [everything that] I know about this incident.

Relativsatz nach substantivierten Superlativen ('das Beste, was')

Relative Clause After Nominalized Superlative ('das Beste, was')

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Similarly, nominalized neuter superlatives (das Beste, das Schönste, das Erste, das Einzige) require 'was' as relative pronoun. ⚠️ Using 'das' instead of 'was' after neuter superlatives ('das Beste, das ich je gesehen habe' is wrong; correct is 'was').

Beispielsatz

Das ist [das Schönste, was] mir je passiert ist.

That is [the most beautiful thing that] has ever happened to me.

Genitiv-Relativpronomen: 'dessen' (mask./neutr.) und 'deren' (fem./pl.)

Genitive Relative Pronouns: 'dessen' and 'deren' (Whose)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'dessen' refers to masculine/neuter antecedents; 'deren' refers to feminine and plural. Unlike English 'whose', German nouns following 'dessen/deren' DO NOT drop their article entirely, but stand WITHOUT article. ⚠️ Adding an article after 'dessen' ('dessen die Forschung' is completely ungrammatical).

Beispielsatz

Der Wissenschaftler, [dessen] Forschung mit dem Nobelpreis ausgezeichnet wurde, hält heute einen Vortrag.

The scientist [whose] research was awarded the Nobel Prize is giving a lecture today.

Relativpronomen mit Präpositionen ('mit denen', 'worauf')

Relative Pronouns Combined with Prepositions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The preposition determines the case of the relative pronoun. Note Dative plural: 'mit denen' (with whom / which). ⚠️ Using 'mit welcher' in colloquial speech; standard relative pronouns (der/die/das/denen) are preferred.

Beispielsatz

Die Freunde, [mit denen] wir in den Urlaub gefahren sind, wohnen in Hamburg.

The friends [with whom] we went on vacation live in Hamburg.

'je nachdem, ob / wie / wann' (Bedingte Abhängigkeit)

'je nachdem, ob / wie / wann' (Depending on Whether / How)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Introduces an indirect conditional dependency clause. Equivalent to 'it depends on whether/how'. ⚠️ Treating 'je nachdem' as an independent clause; it requires a subordinate clause with verb at end.

Beispielsatz

Wir grillen heute Abend im Garten, [je nachdem, wie] das Wetter wird.

We will barbecue in the garden tonight, [depending on how] the weather turns out.

Apposition vs. Weiterführender Relativsatz

Apposition vs. Continuing Relative Clause

📌 Grammatiknotiz

An apposition condenses a relative clause into an uninflected or case-congruent noun phrase set off by commas: 'Herr Schmidt, der Leiter der Abteilung,...'. ⚠️ Failing to match the grammatical case of the apposition with its antecedent noun.

Beispielsatz

Dr. Weber, [der renommierte Leiter des Instituts], eröffnete die Konferenz.

Dr. Weber, [the renowned director of the institute], opened the conference.

Zeichensetzung bei weiterführenden Nebensätzen

Punctuation Rules for Continuing Subordinate Clauses

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Continuing relative clauses and adverbial clauses MUST be preceded by a comma. If inserted into the main clause, they are enclosed by commas on both sides. ⚠️ Missing the closing comma when a relative clause is embedded in the middle of a main sentence.

Beispielsatz

Die Entscheidung, [über die tagelang hitzig gestritten worden war], fiel einstimmig aus.

The decision, [over which people had argued heatedly for days], was unanimous.

Stilistische Eleganz: Relativsätze nicht überhäufen

Stylistic Elegance: Avoiding Relative Clause Cascades

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Chaining multiple relative clauses inside each other ('Die Frau, die den Hund sah, der die Katze jagte, die...') destroys clarity. Break complex thoughts into separate main sentences. ⚠️ Creating Russian-doll nested sentences that force readers to re-read multiple times.

Beispielsatz

Verschachtelt (Schlechter Stil): Das Gesetz, das der Bundestag, der tagelang debattierte, beschloss, ist umstritten.

Nested (Poor style): The law which parliament which debated for days enacted is controversial.
