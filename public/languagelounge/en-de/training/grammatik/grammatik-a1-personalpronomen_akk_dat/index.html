Was sind Personalpronomen? Akkusativ & Dativ

What are Personal Pronouns? Accusative & Dative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Personalpronomen (personal pronouns) replace nouns to avoid repetition. In German, they change their form depending on their grammatical function in a sentence, which is called 'case'. The two main cases for objects are Akkusativ (direct object) and Dativ (indirect object).

Beispielsatz

Ich sehe den Mann. Ich sehe ihn.

I see the man. I see him.

Personalpronomen im Akkusativ (Übersicht)

Personal Pronouns in Accusative (Overview)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Akkusativ case is used for the direct object of a verb – the person or thing that directly receives the action. It answers the question 'Wen?' (Whom?) or 'Was?' (What?). The forms are: ich -> mich, du -> dich, er -> ihn, sie -> sie, es -> es, wir -> uns, ihr -> euch, sie/Sie -> sie/Sie.

Beispielsatz

Kannst du mich hören?

Can you hear me?

Akkusativ: ich -> mich, du -> dich

Accusative: I -> me, you -> you

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the first and second person singular, the personal pronouns change from Nominative 'ich' to 'mich' and 'du' to 'dich' in the Accusative case. These forms are used when 'I' or 'you' are the direct object of a verb or follow an Accusative preposition.

Beispielsatz

Der Lehrer fragt mich. Ich verstehe dich.

The teacher asks me. I understand you.

Akkusativ: er -> ihn, sie -> sie, es -> es

Accusative: he -> him, she -> her, it -> it

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the third person singular, 'er' (he) changes to 'ihn' (him) in the Accusative. 'Sie' (she) remains 'sie' (her), and 'es' (it) remains 'es' (it). Remember to match the gender of the noun the pronoun replaces.

Beispielsatz

Wo ist der Kaffee? Ich trinke ihn gern. Wo ist die Milch? Ich mag sie. Wo ist das Kind? Ich sehe es.

Where is the coffee? I like to drink it. Where is the milk? I like it. Where is the child? I see it.

Akkusativ: wir -> uns, ihr -> euch

Accusative: we -> us, you (plural) -> you (plural)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the first and second person plural, 'wir' (we) becomes 'uns' (us) and 'ihr' (you plural informal) becomes 'euch' (you plural informal) in the Accusative case. These forms are identical to their Dative counterparts, so context is key.

Beispielsatz

Besucht ihr uns morgen? Ja, wir besuchen euch.

Are you visiting us tomorrow? Yes, we are visiting you.

Akkusativ: sie -> sie, Sie -> Sie

Accusative: they -> them, you (formal) -> you (formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the third person plural 'sie' (they) and the formal 'Sie' (you), the Accusative forms remain 'sie' and 'Sie' respectively. These forms are identical to their Nominative counterparts, so pay attention to the verb conjugation and sentence structure to determine the case.

Beispielsatz

Wo sind die Kinder? Ich hole sie ab. Herr Müller, ich rufe Sie später an.

Where are the children? I'll pick them up. Mr. Müller, I'll call you later.

Personalpronomen im Dativ (Übersicht)

Personal Pronouns in Dative (Overview)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Dativ case is used for the indirect object of a verb – the person or thing that benefits from or is affected by the action, but is not directly acted upon. It answers the question 'Wem?' (To whom?/For whom?). The forms are: ich -> mir, du -> dir, er -> ihm, sie -> ihr, es -> ihm, wir -> uns, ihr -> euch, sie/Sie -> ihnen/Ihnen.

Beispielsatz

Ich gebe dir ein Geschenk.

I give you a present.

Dativ: ich -> mir, du -> dir

Dative: I -> to me, you -> to you

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the first and second person singular, 'ich' (I) changes to 'mir' (to me/for me) and 'du' (you) changes to 'dir' (to you/for you) in the Dative case. These are crucial forms to learn as they are frequently used with Dative verbs and prepositions.

Beispielsatz

Kannst du mir helfen? Das gehört dir.

Can you help me? That belongs to you.

Dativ: er -> ihm, sie -> ihr, es -> ihm

Dative: he -> to him, she -> to her, it -> to it

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the third person singular, 'er' (he) becomes 'ihm' (to him), 'sie' (she) becomes 'ihr' (to her), and 'es' (it) becomes 'ihm' (to it). Note that 'er' and 'es' both become 'ihm' in Dative. Always match the gender of the noun being replaced.

Beispielsatz

Ich gebe ihm den Ball. Ich gebe ihr die Tasche. Ich gebe dem Kind das Spielzeug, ich gebe ihm das Spielzeug.

I give him the ball. I give her the bag. I give the child the toy, I give it the toy.

Dativ: wir -> uns, ihr -> euch

Dative: we -> to us, you (plural) -> to you (plural)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the first and second person plural, 'wir' (we) becomes 'uns' (to us/for us) and 'ihr' (you plural informal) becomes 'euch' (to you plural informal) in the Dative case. These forms are identical to their Accusative counterparts. The context of the verb or preposition will indicate the case.

Beispielsatz

Der Kellner bringt uns die Rechnung. Ich schicke euch eine E-Mail.

The waiter brings us the bill. I send you (plural) an email.

Dativ: sie -> ihnen, Sie -> Ihnen

Dative: they -> to them, you (formal) -> to you (formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the third person plural 'sie' (they) and the formal 'Sie' (you), the Dative forms are 'ihnen' (to them/for them) and 'Ihnen' (to you/for you). Note the '-en' ending for Dative plural. The formal 'Ihnen' is always capitalized.

Beispielsatz

Ich erzähle ihnen eine Geschichte. Herr Schmidt, ich danke Ihnen.

I tell them a story. Mr. Schmidt, I thank you.

Verben mit Akkusativ-Ergänzung

Verbs requiring an Accusative object

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Many common German verbs take a direct object in the Accusative case. These verbs describe an action that directly affects someone or something. Examples include 'haben' (to have), 'sehen' (to see), 'hören' (to hear), 'fragen' (to ask), 'brauchen' (to need), 'lieben' (to love), 'kennen' (to know).

Beispielsatz

Ich habe einen Hund. Ich habe ihn. Siehst du mich?

I have a dog. I have him. Do you see me?

Verben mit Dativ-Ergänzung

Verbs requiring a Dative object

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some verbs in German always take an indirect object in the Dative case. These verbs often express an action done to or for someone, rather than directly on them. Examples include 'helfen' (to help), 'danken' (to thank), 'gehören' (to belong to), 'gefallen' (to please), 'antworten' (to answer), 'gratulieren' (to congratulate).

Beispielsatz

Ich helfe dir. Das Buch gehört mir. Die Musik gefällt ihm.

I help you. The book belongs to me. The music pleases him.

Präpositionen mit Akkusativ

Prepositions requiring Accusative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Certain prepositions always require their object to be in the Accusative case. When a personal pronoun follows one of these prepositions, it must be in its Accusative form. Common Accusative prepositions include: 'durch' (through), 'für' (for), 'gegen' (against), 'ohne' (without), 'um' (around/at a time).

Beispielsatz

Der Brief ist für mich. Ohne dich gehe ich nicht.

The letter is for me. Without you, I won't go.

Präpositionen mit Dativ

Prepositions requiring Dative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Other prepositions always require their object to be in the Dative case. When a personal pronoun follows one of these prepositions, it must be in its Dative form. Common Dative prepositions include: 'aus' (out of/from), 'bei' (at/with), 'mit' (with), 'nach' (after/to), 'seit' (since), 'von' (from/of), 'zu' (to).

Beispielsatz

Ich komme aus ihm. Ich wohne bei dir. Ich fahre mit ihr.

I come from it (e.g., the house). I live with you. I drive with her.

Verben mit Akkusativ- und Dativ-Ergänzung

Verbs with both Accusative and Dative objects

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some verbs, like 'geben' (to give), 'schenken' (to give a gift), 'schicken' (to send), 'erzählen' (to tell), take both an Accusative (direct) and a Dative (indirect) object. The Dative object is usually the person receiving something, and the Accusative object is the thing being received. When both objects are personal pronouns, the Dative pronoun always comes before the Accusative pronoun.

Beispielsatz

Ich gebe dir das Buch. Ich gebe es dir. Ich gebe es ihm.

I give you the book. I give it to you. I give it to him.

Wortstellung: Pronomen und Nomen im Satz

Word Order: Pronouns and Nouns in a Sentence

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The general rule for word order with objects is: if both objects are pronouns, the Dative pronoun comes before the Accusative pronoun (e.g., 'Ich gebe ihm es'). If one object is a pronoun and the other is a noun, the pronoun usually comes first, regardless of case (e.g., 'Ich gebe es dem Mann', 'Ich gebe ihm das Buch'). If both are nouns, the Accusative noun often comes before the Dative noun, but this can be flexible.

Beispielsatz

Ich gebe ihm das Buch. Ich gebe es dem Mann. Ich gebe es ihm.

I give him the book. I give it to the man. I give it to him.
