Das Verb "lassen" - Überblick

The verb "lassen" - Overview

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Lassen' is a versatile German verb with three primary meanings: 1. to let/allow (like 'erlauben'), 2. to leave (behind) (like 'zurücklassen'), and 3. to have something done (a causative meaning). Its specific meaning often depends on the grammatical context and accompanying elements.

Beispielsatz

Er lässt sein Auto reparieren.

He has his car repaired.

lassen (Präsens)

lassen (Present Tense)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Lassen' is an irregular (strong) verb in the present tense. It undergoes a stem vowel change from 'a' to 'ä' in the 'du' and 'er/sie/es' forms. The other forms are regular. This vowel change is crucial for correct conjugation.

Beispielsatz

Ich lasse dich nicht allein.

I won't leave you alone.

lassen (Präteritum)

lassen (Simple Past)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the simple past (Präteritum), 'lassen' is a strong verb and changes its stem vowel from 'a' to 'ie'. The endings are typical for strong verbs in the Präteritum (e.g., ich ließ, du ließest, er/sie/es ließ). This form is often used in written German.

Beispielsatz

Sie ließ das Fenster offen.

She left the window open.

lassen + Infinitiv (erlauben)

lassen + Infinitive (to let/allow)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When 'lassen' is followed by an infinitive, it often means 'to allow' or 'to permit' someone to do something. The subject of 'lassen' is the one giving permission, and the object (if present) is the one being allowed to act. This implies non-intervention or granting consent.

Beispielsatz

Meine Eltern lassen mich lange aufbleiben.

My parents let me stay up late.

lassen + Akkusativ (zurücklassen)

lassen + Accusative (to leave behind)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In this usage, 'lassen' means to intentionally leave an object somewhere, not take it with you, or abandon it. It is followed by a direct object in the accusative case. This meaning focuses on the state of an object being left in a certain place or condition.

Beispielsatz

Ich lasse meine Jacke im Auto.

I'm leaving my jacket in the car.

lassen + Objekt + Infinitiv (veranlassen)

lassen + Object + Infinitive (to have something done)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This is a causative use. The subject doesn't perform the action themselves but arranges for someone else to do it. The object of 'lassen' is the thing being acted upon, and the infinitive describes the action. This construction often has a passive meaning for the object, implying 'to get/have something done'.

Beispielsatz

Ich lasse mein Haar schneiden.

I have my hair cut (by someone else).

Perfekt von lassen (veranlassen/erlauben) - Ersatzinfinitiv

Perfect Tense of lassen (to have done/allow) - Infinitive Replacement

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When 'lassen' is used with another infinitive (meaning 'to have something done' or 'to let/allow'), its past participle form in Perfekt/Plusquamperfekt is 'lassen' (not 'gelassen'). This is known as the 'Ersatzinfinitiv' (infinitive replacement) and occurs with other verbs like modal verbs. The auxiliary verb is 'haben'.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe mein Auto reparieren lassen.

I had my car repaired.

Perfekt von lassen (zurücklassen/erlauben) - ohne Infinitiv

Perfect Tense of lassen (to leave behind/allow) - without Infinitive

📌 Grammatiknotiz

If 'lassen' is used without another infinitive (e.g., meaning 'to leave behind' or 'to allow' in a simple context), its past participle form in Perfekt/Plusquamperfekt is 'gelassen'. The Ersatzinfinitiv rule only applies when 'lassen' is followed by another infinitive. The auxiliary verb is 'haben'.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe meine Schlüssel zu Hause gelassen.

I left my keys at home.

sich lassen + Infinitiv (Möglichkeit)

sich lassen + Infinitive (can be done, possibility)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The reflexive construction 'sich lassen + infinitive' expresses that something is possible or easy to do, often implying that it happens by itself or without much effort. It functions similarly to a passive construction with 'können' (e.g., 'Es kann gemacht werden'). The subject of 'sich lassen' is the object of the action.

Beispielsatz

Das Problem lässt sich leicht lösen.

The problem can be easily solved.

lassen vs. verlassen

lassen vs. verlassen (to leave)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

While both verbs relate to 'leaving,' their usage differs significantly. 'Lassen' primarily means 'to leave an object behind,' 'to allow,' or 'to have something done.' 'Verlassen' means 'to leave a place or a person,' implying departure or abandonment. They are not interchangeable.

Beispielsatz

Ich lasse mein Handy hier, aber ich verlasse das Haus.

I'm leaving my phone here, but I'm leaving the house.

lassen (Imperativ)

lassen (Imperative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The imperative forms of 'lassen' are used to give commands or make requests. The forms are: 'Lass!' (du), 'Lasst!' (ihr), 'Lassen Sie!' (Sie), and 'Lassen wir!' (wir). These forms are often used to suggest an action or to tell someone to leave something as it is.

Beispielsatz

Lass mich in Ruhe!

Leave me alone!

lassen (Konjunktiv II)

lassen (Subjunctive II)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Konjunktiv II of 'lassen' is 'ließe' and is used to express hypothetical situations, wishes, or polite requests. It follows the same stem change as the Präteritum ('a' to 'ie') and adds the Konjunktiv II endings. It can also be formed with 'würde lassen'.

Beispielsatz

Ich ließe das Auto reparieren, wenn ich Geld hätte.

I would have the car repaired if I had money.

lassen mit Modalverben

lassen with Modal Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When 'lassen' combines with a modal verb in the Perfekt or Plusquamperfekt, both 'lassen' and the main infinitive remain in their infinitive forms. This creates a 'double infinitive' construction (e.g., 'Ich habe es machen lassen können'). The finite verb is 'haben', and it stands at the end of the clause.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe das Auto reparieren lassen müssen.

I had to have the car repaired.

Ich lasse mich vs. Ich lasse mir

I let myself vs. I have something done for myself

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Ich lasse mich' (reflexive accusative) means 'I allow myself' or 'I let myself be (done something to)'. 'Ich lasse mir' (reflexive dative) means 'I have something done for myself,' where the action benefits or affects the subject. The case of the reflexive pronoun changes the meaning significantly.

Beispielsatz

Ich lasse mich nicht provozieren. (Akk) vs. Ich lasse mir die Haare schneiden. (Dat)

I don't let myself be provoked. vs. I have my hair cut (for myself).

lassen vs. erlauben / dürfen

lassen vs. to allow / to be allowed

📌 Grammatiknotiz

While 'lassen' can mean 'to allow,' it often implies a less formal or more passive permission (e.g., not preventing something). 'Erlauben' is a more direct and formal act of granting permission. 'Dürfen' expresses that one has permission or is allowed to do something. Choose the verb based on the nuance of permission.

Beispielsatz

Ich lasse dich gehen. (I don't stop you) vs. Ich erlaube dir zu gehen. (I formally permit you) vs. Du darfst gehen. (You have permission)

I let you go. vs. I allow you to go. vs. You are allowed to go.

lassen in festen Ausdrücken

lassen in fixed expressions / idioms

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Lassen' appears in many common German idioms and fixed expressions, often carrying one of its core meanings but in a specific, non-literal context. Recognizing these expressions is key to understanding natural German and avoiding literal, incorrect translations. These phrases are best learned as whole units.

Beispielsatz

Das lässt sich machen.

That can be done. (Lit. That lets itself be done.)
