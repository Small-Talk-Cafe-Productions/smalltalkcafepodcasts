Das Präsens (Gegenwart)

The Present Tense

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The German present tense is used to describe actions happening now, habitual actions, future events with a time expression, and general truths. It is the most common tense in everyday German conversation and writing, making it fundamental for A1 learners. Unlike English, it often covers both simple present and present continuous meanings.

Beispielsatz

Ich lerne Deutsch.

I am learning German. / I learn German.

Regelmäßige Konjugation (schwache Verben)

Regular Conjugation (weak verbs)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Most German verbs are regular. To conjugate them in the present tense, you remove the infinitive ending '-en' (or '-n') to find the verb stem. Then, you add specific personal endings to this stem, depending on the subject pronoun. This pattern is consistent and predictable for a large number of verbs.

Beispielsatz

machen -> mach-

to make -> make-

ich-Form (1. Person Singular)

I-form (1st Person Singular)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the 'ich' form, you add the ending '-e' to the verb stem. This applies to all regular verbs and many irregular verbs as well. It's the most straightforward singular ending and a good starting point for learning conjugations.

Beispielsatz

Ich mache.

I make / I am making.

du-Form (2. Person Singular)

You-form (2nd Person Singular, informal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the 'du' form, you add the ending '-st' to the verb stem. This ending is characteristic for informal singular address. Pay close attention to verbs whose stems end in -s, -ß, -z, or -x, as they have a slight variation.

Beispielsatz

Du machst.

You make / You are making.

er/sie/es-Form (3. Person Singular)

He/she/it-form (3rd Person Singular)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the 'er', 'sie' (she), and 'es' forms, you add the ending '-t' to the verb stem. This ending is identical to the 'ihr' form. Remember that 'sie' can mean 'she' (singular) or 'they' (plural), so context is important for singular vs. plural meaning.

Beispielsatz

Er macht.

He makes / He is making.

wir-Form (1. Person Plural)

We-form (1st Person Plural)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the 'wir' form, you add the ending '-en' to the verb stem. This ending is identical to the infinitive form of most verbs. It's one of the easiest forms to remember because it often matches the base form of the verb.

Beispielsatz

Wir machen.

We make / We are making.

ihr-Form (2. Person Plural)

You-all form (2nd Person Plural, informal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the 'ihr' form, you add the ending '-t' to the verb stem. This ending is identical to the 'er/sie/es' form. 'Ihr' is used when addressing a group of people informally. It's crucial to distinguish it from 'du' (singular informal) and 'Sie' (singular/plural formal).

Beispielsatz

Ihr macht.

You all make / You all are making.

sie/Sie-Form (3. Person Plural / Formal Singular & Plural)

They-form (3rd Person Plural) / You-form (Formal Singular & Plural)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For 'sie' (they) and 'Sie' (formal You, singular or plural), you add the ending '-en' to the verb stem. This ending is identical to the infinitive form and the 'wir' form. The capitalization of 'Sie' is the only way to distinguish it from 'sie' (she) and 'sie' (they) in writing, but context helps in speech.

Beispielsatz

Sie (they) machen. / Sie (formal You) machen.

They make. / You (formal) make.

Unregelmäßiges Verb: sein (to be)

Irregular Verb: to be

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'sein' is highly irregular and one of the most important verbs in German. Its conjugation does not follow the regular pattern at all, so you must memorize all its forms. It is used to express existence, identity, and location, similar to 'to be' in English.

Beispielsatz

Ich bin Student. Du bist müde. Er ist Arzt. Wir sind Freunde. Ihr seid hier. Sie sind nett.

I am a student. You are tired. He is a doctor. We are friends. You all are here. They/You are nice.

Unregelmäßiges Verb: haben (to have)

Irregular Verb: to have

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'haben' is also irregular, especially in the 'du' and 'er/sie/es' forms, where the stem changes. It's another fundamental verb used to express possession and as an auxiliary verb for perfect tenses. Memorizing its forms is crucial for A1 learners.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe Hunger. Du hast Zeit. Er hat ein Auto. Wir haben Spaß. Ihr habt Durst. Sie haben Glück.

I am hungry. You have time. He has a car. We are having fun. You all are thirsty. They/You are lucky.

Verben mit Stammerweiterung (-t, -d, -m, -n)

Verbs with stem extension (stems ending in -t, -d, -m, -n)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

If a verb stem ends in -t, -d, -m, or -n (and is preceded by another consonant), an '-e-' is inserted before the '-t' or '-st' endings to make pronunciation easier. This affects the 'du', 'er/sie/es', and 'ihr' forms. For example, 'arbeiten' becomes 'du arbeitest', not 'du arbeitst'.

Beispielsatz

arbeiten -> du arbeitest, er arbeitet, ihr arbeitet

to work -> you work, he works, you all work

Verben mit Stammerweiterung (-s, -ß, -z, -x)

Verbs with stem extension (stems ending in -s, -ß, -z, -x)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

If a verb stem ends in -s, -ß, -z, or -x, the 'du' ending '-st' is shortened to just '-t'. This prevents a double 's' sound and makes the word easier to pronounce. The 'er/sie/es' and 'ihr' forms already end in '-t', so they remain unchanged.

Beispielsatz

tanzen -> du tanzt

to dance -> you dance

Starke Verben: Vokalwechsel (e -> i/ie)

Strong Verbs: Vowel Change (e -> i/ie)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some strong (irregular) verbs change their stem vowel from 'e' to 'i' or 'ie' in the 'du' and 'er/sie/es' forms. All other forms (ich, wir, ihr, sie/Sie) follow the regular pattern. This is a common irregularity you need to memorize for each verb.

Beispielsatz

sprechen -> ich spreche, du sprichst, er spricht

to speak -> I speak, you speak, he speaks

Starke Verben: Vokalwechsel (a -> ä)

Strong Verbs: Vowel Change (a -> ä)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Another common stem vowel change in strong verbs is from 'a' to 'ä'. This change also occurs only in the 'du' and 'er/sie/es' forms. The other forms (ich, wir, ihr, sie/Sie) retain the original 'a' vowel. This umlaut indicates an irregularity.

Beispielsatz

fahren -> ich fahre, du fährst, er fährt

to drive -> I drive, you drive, he drives

Verben mit Stammerweiterung (-el, -er)

Verbs with stem extension (stems ending in -el, -er)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For verbs whose stem ends in '-el' or '-er', the 'e' before the 'l' or 'r' is often dropped in the 'ich' form. For example, 'klingeln' (to ring) becomes 'ich klingle' instead of 'ich klingele'. This makes the pronunciation smoother. All other forms follow the regular pattern.

Beispielsatz

klingeln -> ich klingle

to ring -> I ring

Trennbare Verben (Separable Verbs)

Separable Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the present tense, the prefix of a separable verb moves to the very end of the sentence, while the main verb conjugates normally in the second position. This is a key feature of German sentence structure. The prefix carries meaning and often changes the verb's original sense.

Beispielsatz

anrufen -> Ich rufe dich an.

to call (on the phone) -> I call you (on the phone).

Modalverben (General Irregularity)

Modal Verbs (General Irregularity)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Modal verbs (like können, wollen, müssen, dürfen, sollen, mögen) are highly irregular in the present tense. They often have a stem vowel change in the singular forms (ich, du, er/sie/es) and do not take the typical '-t' ending for 'er/sie/es'. The 'ich' and 'er/sie/es' forms are often identical. Plural forms (wir, ihr, sie/Sie) are more regular.

Beispielsatz

können -> ich kann, du kannst, er kann, wir können, ihr könnt, sie können

to be able to -> I can, you can, he can, we can, you all can, they can
