Ja-/Nein-Fragen: Grundstruktur (Verb an Position 1)

Yes/No Questions: Basic Structure (Verb in first position)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To form a Yes/No question in German, place the conjugated verb at the very beginning of the sentence (position 1). The subject then follows immediately after the verb. The sentence ends with a question mark and typically has a rising intonation in spoken language. ⚠️ Learners often place the subject first, like in a statement, or use a question word (W-Frage) unnecessarily. Remember, no 'W-word' for Yes/No questions.

Beispielsatz

Kommst du aus Deutschland?

Are you from Germany?

Antworten: Ja (Affirmative Antwort)

Answers: Yes (Affirmative Answer)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To give an affirmative answer to a Yes/No question, start with 'Ja' (Yes). You can then follow it with a complete sentence that confirms the information, or a shorter answer repeating the subject and verb. This is used when the question is positive. ⚠️ Sometimes learners forget to include the verb or use an incorrect verb form after 'Ja'.

Beispielsatz

Ja, ich komme aus Deutschland.

Yes, I come from Germany.

Antworten: Nein (Negative Antwort mit 'nicht')

Answers: No (Negative Answer with 'nicht')

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To give a negative answer to a positive Yes/No question, start with 'Nein' (No). Follow it with a complete sentence that negates the information using 'nicht'. 'Nicht' is used to negate verbs, adjectives, adverbs, proper nouns, and nouns with definite articles. ⚠️ Incorrect placement of 'nicht'. It usually comes at the end of the sentence or before the negated element (adjective/adverb).

Beispielsatz

Nein, ich komme nicht aus Deutschland.

No, I don't come from Germany.

Antworten: Nein (Negative Antwort mit 'kein')

Answers: No (Negative Answer with 'kein')

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When negating an indefinite noun (a noun with 'ein/eine' or no article), use 'kein' (no/not a). 'Kein' declines like an indefinite article and must match the gender and case of the noun it negates. It replaces 'ein/eine'. ⚠️ Using 'nicht ein' instead of 'kein' for indefinite nouns. Remember 'kein' is the correct choice here.

Beispielsatz

Hast du ein Auto? - Nein, ich habe kein Auto.

Do you have a car? - No, I don't have a car.

Antworten: Doch (Affirmative Antwort auf negative Frage)

Answers: Yes, indeed / On the contrary (Affirmative Answer to a Negative Question)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Use 'Doch' (Yes, indeed / On the contrary) when you want to give an affirmative answer to a negative Yes/No question. It directly contradicts the negative premise of the question. 'Doch' is a strong affirmation. ⚠️ Using 'Ja' instead of 'Doch' when answering a negative question affirmatively. 'Ja' would confirm the negative statement, which is usually not the intention.

Beispielsatz

Kommst du nicht aus Deutschland? - Doch, ich komme aus Deutschland.

Don't you come from Germany? - Yes, I do come from Germany.

Kurzantworten: Ja / Nein / Doch

Short Answers: Yes / No / On the contrary

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For quick responses, you can use 'Ja', 'Nein', or 'Doch' followed by the subject pronoun and the conjugated verb. This avoids repeating the entire sentence but still provides clear context. This is very common in everyday conversation. ⚠️ Omitting the verb in short answers, e.g., 'Ja, ich.' This is incorrect; the verb must be included.

Beispielsatz

Sprichst du Deutsch? - Ja, ich spreche.

Do you speak German? - Yes, I do.

Ja-/Nein-Fragen mit 'sein' (to be)

Yes/No Questions with 'sein' (to be)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'sein' (to be) is irregular and very common in Yes/No questions. Conjugate 'sein' according to the subject and place it at the beginning of the sentence. It's often used to ask about identity, state, or location. ⚠️ Incorrect conjugation of 'sein' (e.g., 'Bist du Herr Müller?' when addressing formally).

Beispielsatz

Sind Sie Herr Müller?

Are you Mr. Müller?

Ja-/Nein-Fragen mit 'haben' (to have)

Yes/No Questions with 'haben' (to have)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'haben' (to have) is also irregular and frequently used. Conjugate 'haben' according to the subject and place it at the beginning. It's used to ask about possession or certain states (e.g., 'Hunger haben'). ⚠️ Confusing 'haben' with 'sein' for states like hunger/thirst. In German, you 'have' hunger/thirst.

Beispielsatz

Hast du Geschwister?

Do you have siblings?

Ja-/Nein-Fragen mit Modalverben (können, müssen, wollen)

Yes/No Questions with Modal Verbs (can, must, want)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When forming Yes/No questions with modal verbs (like 'können', 'müssen', 'wollen'), the conjugated modal verb goes to position 1. The main verb, in its infinitive form, goes to the very end of the sentence. This is a common structure for expressing ability, necessity, or desire. ⚠️ Forgetting to put the main verb at the end of the sentence in its infinitive form, or conjugating both verbs.

Beispielsatz

Kannst du schwimmen?

Can you swim?

Ja-/Nein-Fragen mit trennbaren Verben (separable verbs)

Yes/No Questions with separable verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For separable verbs (e.g., 'anrufen', 'einkaufen'), the conjugated verb stem goes to position 1, and the separable prefix goes to the very end of the sentence. This split structure is crucial for correct sentence formation with these verbs. ⚠️ Keeping the prefix attached to the verb in position 1, or placing the prefix in the middle of the sentence.

Beispielsatz

Rufst du deine Mutter an?

Are you calling your mother?

Ja-/Nein-Fragen mit Dativobjekt

Yes/No Questions with Dative Object

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some verbs require a dative object (e.g., 'helfen', 'gehören'). When forming a Yes/No question, the verb is still at position 1, and the dative object (often a pronoun or noun with dative article) follows in its correct case. Remember the dative pronouns (mir, dir, ihm, ihr, uns, euch, ihnen, Ihnen). ⚠️ Using an accusative pronoun instead of a dative pronoun (e.g., 'Hilfst du mich?' instead of 'Hilfst du mir?').

Beispielsatz

Hilfst du mir?

Are you helping me?

Ja-/Nein-Fragen mit Akkusativobjekt

Yes/No Questions with Accusative Object

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Many verbs take an accusative object (e.g., 'sehen', 'kaufen', 'lieben'). In Yes/No questions, the verb is at position 1, and the accusative object (often a pronoun or noun with accusative article) follows. Remember the accusative pronouns (mich, dich, ihn, sie, es, uns, euch, sie, Sie). ⚠️ Confusing masculine nominative 'der' with masculine accusative 'den' for nouns, or using nominative pronouns instead of accusative ones.

Beispielsatz

Siehst du den Mann?

Do you see the man?

Ja-/Nein-Fragen mit Präpositionalobjekt

Yes/No Questions with Prepositional Object

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some verbs are always used with a specific preposition and a specific case (e.g., 'warten auf + Akkusativ', 'sprechen mit + Dativ'). In Yes/No questions, the verb is at position 1, and the prepositional phrase follows, maintaining the correct case for the object of the preposition. ⚠️ Using the wrong preposition or the wrong case after the preposition. These combinations must be learned by heart.

Beispielsatz

Wartest du auf den Bus?

Are you waiting for the bus?

Ja-/Nein-Fragen mit Adverbien der Zeit/Ort/Art

Yes/No Questions with Adverbs of Time/Place/Manner

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Adverbs of time, place, or manner can be included in Yes/No questions. They typically follow the subject and any objects, or they can sometimes be placed after the verb if the subject is a pronoun. The basic verb-subject structure remains unchanged. ⚠️ Placing the adverb before the verb or subject, breaking the V1-S order.

Beispielsatz

Kommst du heute Abend?

Are you coming this evening?

Intonation von Ja-/Nein-Fragen

Intonation of Yes/No Questions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In spoken German, Yes/No questions are typically characterized by a rising intonation at the end of the sentence. This rising pitch signals to the listener that a question is being asked and expects a 'Ja', 'Nein', or 'Doch' response. This is crucial for natural communication. ⚠️ Using a falling intonation, which would make the question sound like a statement or a 'W-Frage'.

Beispielsatz

Hast du Hunger? (↗)

Are you hungry? (with rising intonation)

Zusammenfassung: Ja, Nein, Doch im Überblick

Summary: Ja, Nein, Doch at a Glance

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This card summarizes when to use 'Ja', 'Nein', and 'Doch'. Use 'Ja' for affirmative answers to positive questions. Use 'Nein' for negative answers to positive questions. Use 'Doch' for affirmative answers to negative questions, contradicting the negative premise. ⚠️ Misusing 'Ja' instead of 'Doch' when contradicting a negative question, leading to confusion.

Beispielsatz

Kommst du? - Ja. / Kommst du nicht? - Doch. / Kommst du? - Nein.

Are you coming? - Yes. / Aren't you coming? - Yes, I am. / Are you coming? - No.
