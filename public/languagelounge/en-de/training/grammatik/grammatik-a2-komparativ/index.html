Komparativ: Grundform (+er)

Comparative: Basic Form (+er)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To form the basic comparative of most adjectives, you simply add '-er' to the positive form. This form is used when comparing two things or people, indicating that one possesses a quality to a greater degree than the other. The adjective itself does not change its stem unless it falls under specific exceptions.

Beispielsatz

Das Auto ist schnell. -> Das Auto ist schneller.

The car is fast. -> The car is faster.

Komparativ: Umlaut bei a, o, u

Comparative: Umlaut for a, o, u

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Many monosyllabic adjectives, and some polysyllabic ones, that contain the vowels 'a', 'o', or 'u' in their stem will take an umlaut (ä, ö, ü) in the comparative form. This change occurs before the '-er' ending is added. Adjectives with 'e' or 'i' do not take an umlaut.

Beispielsatz

alt -> älter; jung -> jünger; kurz -> kürzer; groß -> größer

old -> older; young -> younger; short -> shorter; big -> bigger

Komparativ: Adjektive auf -el, -er

Comparative: Adjectives ending in -el, -er

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Adjectives ending in '-el' or '-er' drop the 'e' before adding the comparative ending '-er'. This rule helps maintain pronunciation and avoids awkward vowel clusters. This applies to both the positive and superlative forms.

Beispielsatz

dunkel -> dunkler; teuer -> teurer

dark -> darker; expensive -> more expensive

Unregelmäßige Komparative (gut, viel, gern)

Irregular Comparatives (good, much, gladly)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some common adjectives have completely irregular comparative forms that must be memorized. These do not follow the standard '-er' rule or umlaut pattern. They are frequently used and essential for natural-sounding German.

Beispielsatz

gut -> besser; viel -> mehr; gern -> lieber

good -> better; much -> more; gladly -> rather/more gladly

Unregelmäßige Komparative (nah, hoch)

Irregular Comparatives (near, high)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Two other important irregular comparatives are 'nah' and 'hoch'. 'Nah' takes an umlaut and changes its stem, while 'hoch' drops the 'ch' and adds an umlaut. These changes are unique and must be learned individually.

Beispielsatz

nah -> näher; hoch -> höher

near -> nearer; high -> higher

Komparativ als Adverb

Comparative as an Adverb

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a comparative adjective modifies a verb, it functions as an adverb and remains undeclined. It directly uses the '-er' form (or irregular form) without any additional endings. This is the simplest way to use the comparative, as it doesn't involve case or gender.

Beispielsatz

Er spricht Deutsch gut. -> Er spricht Deutsch besser.

He speaks German well. -> He speaks German better.

Komparativ vor Nomen (Deklination)

Comparative before Nouns (Declension)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a comparative adjective stands before a noun, it must be declined according to the noun's gender, number, and case, just like any other adjective. The '-er' ending of the comparative form is followed by the appropriate adjective ending (e.g., -e, -en, -em, -es). This is a crucial point for correct usage.

Beispielsatz

Ein schnelleres Auto ist teurer.

A faster car is more expensive.

Vergleich mit 'als' (than)

Comparison with 'als' (than)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To express inequality in a comparison (X is more/less Y than Z), you use the comparative form of the adjective followed by 'als'. 'Als' introduces the second part of the comparison. The noun or pronoun following 'als' usually takes the same case as the noun or pronoun it is being compared to.

Beispielsatz

Mein Bruder ist größer als ich.

My brother is taller than I (am).

Gleichheit ausdrücken mit 'so... wie' (as...as)

Expressing Equality with 'so... wie' (as...as)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To express that two things or people are equal in a certain quality, you use the structure 'so + positive adjective + wie'. The adjective remains in its positive (base) form, without '-er' or umlaut. 'Wie' introduces the second part of the comparison, which is equal to the first.

Beispielsatz

Er ist so groß wie ich.

He is as tall as I (am).

Ungleichheit ausdrücken mit 'nicht so... wie' (not as...as)

Expressing Inequality with 'nicht so... wie' (not as...as)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To express that something is less than or not equal to something else, you use the structure 'nicht so + positive adjective + wie'. This construction indicates a lack of equality, meaning one item possesses the quality to a lesser degree than the other. The adjective remains in its positive form.

Beispielsatz

Sie ist nicht so schnell wie ihr Bruder.

She is not as fast as her brother.

Steigerung mit 'immer + Komparativ'

Expressing Continuous Increase ('getting more and more')

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To describe a continuous development or an ongoing increase in a quality, you use 'immer' followed by the comparative form of the adjective. This construction emphasizes that something is becoming progressively more of a certain quality over time. The adjective is used adverbially here, so it's undeclined.

Beispielsatz

Es wird immer kälter.

It's getting colder and colder (or: increasingly cold).

Korrelativer Vergleich mit 'je... desto/umso' (the...the)

Correlative Comparison with 'je... desto/umso' (the...the)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This structure expresses a relationship where one change causes or corresponds to another change. 'Je' introduces a subordinate clause with the verb at the end, and 'desto' or 'umso' introduces the main clause, also with the verb often following the comparative. Both clauses use the comparative form of the adjective.

Beispielsatz

Je mehr du lernst, desto besser sprichst du Deutsch.

The more you learn, the better you speak German.

Auswahl mit 'von + Komparativ'

Choosing between two/among several with 'von + Comparative'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To indicate a choice or distinction among a group, especially when comparing two items, you can use 'von' followed by the definite article and the comparative adjective. This structure highlights which item possesses the quality to a greater degree within a specified set. The comparative adjective is declined here.

Beispielsatz

Von den beiden ist er der größere.

Of the two, he is the taller one.

Adjektive ohne Komparativ

Adjectives without Comparative Forms

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some adjectives describe absolute states or qualities that cannot be increased or decreased, and therefore do not form comparative or superlative forms. These include adjectives describing colors, materials, absolute states (e.g., dead, pregnant), or unique qualities. They are always used in their positive form.

Beispielsatz

tot, schwanger, golden, fertig, einzig, optimal

dead, pregnant, golden, finished, unique, optimal

Komparativ als Teil eines festen Ausdrucks

Comparative as part of a fixed expression

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some comparative forms are used in fixed expressions where their meaning might be slightly idiomatic or simply a common way to express a concept. These often function adverbially or as part of a set phrase, and their comparative nature is inherent to the expression. Examples include 'früher' (formerly) or 'später' (later).

Beispielsatz

Früher war alles besser.

Everything used to be better (formerly everything was better).

Vergleich von Nomen mit 'mehr/weniger + Nomen + als'

Comparing Nouns with 'more/less + Noun + than'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When comparing quantities of nouns, you use 'mehr' (more) or 'weniger' (less) directly before the noun, followed by 'als'. The nouns themselves do not change form, and 'mehr/weniger' act as quantifiers. This structure is used for countable and uncountable nouns alike.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe mehr Bücher als du.

I have more books than you.
