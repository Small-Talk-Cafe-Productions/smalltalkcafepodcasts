Tag

day

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Tag is a masculine noun. It is used to refer to a day or daytime. When used in greetings like 'Guten Tag', it's in the accusative case. The plural form is regular. Prepositions like 'am' (an dem) are often used for specific days (e.g., am Montag - on Monday) or parts of the day (e.g., am Tag - during the day).

Beispielsatz

Ich habe heute einen guten Tag.

I am having a good day today.

Nacht

night

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Nacht is a feminine noun. It refers to the period of darkness. The plural form involves an umlaut. Similar to 'Tag', 'in der Nacht' (in the night) or 'nachts' (at night, adverb) are common expressions. 'Gute Nacht' is a common farewell.

Beispielsatz

Ich lese oft in der Nacht.

I often read at night.

Morgen

morning

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Morgen is a masculine noun. It can mean 'morning' or 'tomorrow'. The plural form is the same as the singular. When referring to 'in the morning', 'am Morgen' (dative) or 'morgens' (adverb) are used. 'Guten Morgen' is a common greeting.

Beispielsatz

Am Morgen trinke ich Kaffee.

In the morning I drink coffee.

Abend

evening

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Abend is a masculine noun. It refers to the evening. The plural form is regular. 'Am Abend' (in the evening) or 'abends' (adverb) are common. 'Guten Abend' is a common greeting.

Beispielsatz

Wir essen am Abend zusammen.

We eat together in the evening.

Woche

week

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Woche is a feminine noun. It refers to a period of seven days. The plural form is regular. Prepositions like 'in der Woche' (during the week) or 'nächste Woche' (next week) are common. 'Am Wochenende' (on the weekend) is a fixed expression.

Beispielsatz

Ich arbeite fünf Tage in der Woche.

I work five days a week.

Wochenende

weekend

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Wochenende is a neuter noun. It refers to Saturday and Sunday. The plural form adds '-n'. The most common prepositional phrase is 'am Wochenende' (on the weekend), which uses the dative case.

Beispielsatz

Was machst du am Wochenende?

What are you doing on the weekend?

Arbeit

work

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Arbeit is a feminine noun. It can mean 'work' (as a task or job) or 'labor'. The plural form is regular. Common prepositions include 'bei der Arbeit' (at work) or 'zur Arbeit gehen' (to go to work). It's often used with verbs like 'haben' (to have work) or 'machen' (to do work).

Beispielsatz

Ich gehe jeden Tag zur Arbeit.

I go to work every day.

Haus

house

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Haus is a neuter noun. It refers to a house or building. The plural form involves an umlaut. Common expressions include 'zu Hause' (at home, fixed expression, no article) and 'nach Hause' (home, direction, no article). 'Im Haus' means 'in the house'.

Beispielsatz

Ich bin zu Hause.

I am at home.

Wohnung

apartment

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Wohnung is a feminine noun. It refers to an apartment or flat. The plural form is regular. Common prepositions are 'in der Wohnung' (in the apartment) or 'eine Wohnung mieten' (to rent an apartment). 'In' takes dative for location.

Beispielsatz

Meine Wohnung ist nicht sehr groß.

My apartment is not very big.

Zimmer

room

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Zimmer is a neuter noun. It refers to a room. The plural form is the same as the singular. Common prepositions include 'im Zimmer' (in the room) or 'ein Zimmer buchen' (to book a room). 'Im' is a contraction of 'in dem' (dative).

Beispielsatz

Ich habe ein großes Zimmer.

I have a big room.

Bett

bed

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Bett is a neuter noun. It refers to a bed. The plural form adds '-en'. Common expressions are 'ins Bett gehen' (to go to bed, accusative with 'in' for direction) and 'im Bett liegen' (to lie in bed, dative with 'in' for location).

Beispielsatz

Ich gehe um zehn Uhr ins Bett.

I go to bed at ten o'clock.

Tisch

table

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Tisch is a masculine noun. It refers to a table. The plural form is regular. Common prepositions include 'auf dem Tisch' (on the table, dative for location) and 'an den Tisch setzen' (to sit down at the table, accusative for direction).

Beispielsatz

Das Buch liegt auf dem Tisch.

The book is on the table.

Essen

food, meal

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Essen is a neuter noun. It can mean 'food' (uncountable) or 'a meal' (countable, but often used without plural in A1). As a verb, 'essen' means 'to eat'. When referring to a specific meal, it's often combined with a time of day (e.g., das Mittagessen).

Beispielsatz

Das Essen schmeckt sehr gut.

The food tastes very good.

Frühstück

breakfast

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Frühstück is a neuter noun. It refers to breakfast. The plural form adds '-e'. Common expressions include 'zum Frühstück' (for breakfast, dative) or 'Frühstück machen' (to make breakfast). The verb is 'frühstücken' (to have breakfast).

Beispielsatz

Ich esse Müsli zum Frühstück.

I eat muesli for breakfast.

Abendessen

dinner

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Abendessen is a neuter noun. It refers to dinner. The plural form is the same as the singular. Similar to other meals, 'zum Abendessen' (for dinner) is common. The verb is 'zu Abend essen' (to have dinner).

Beispielsatz

Wir kochen das Abendessen zusammen.

We cook dinner together.

Wasser

water

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Wasser is a neuter noun. It refers to water. The plural form 'Wässer' is rare and usually refers to different types of water (e.g., mineral waters). In everyday use, 'Wasser' is often treated as uncountable. It's frequently used with verbs like 'trinken' (to drink).

Beispielsatz

Ich möchte bitte ein Glas Wasser.

I would like a glass of water, please.

Kaffee

coffee

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Kaffee is a masculine noun. It refers to coffee. The plural form 'Kaffees' is rare and refers to different types of coffee or multiple cups of coffee. In everyday use, 'Kaffee' is often uncountable. It's frequently used with 'trinken' (to drink).

Beispielsatz

Ich trinke jeden Morgen einen Kaffee.

I drink a coffee every morning.

Tee

tea

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Tee is a masculine noun. It refers to tea. The plural form 'Tees' is rare, similar to 'Kaffee'. It's often treated as uncountable. Common verbs include 'trinken' (to drink) or 'Tee kochen' (to make tea).

Beispielsatz

Möchtest du einen Tee?

Would you like a tea?

Milch

milk

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Milch is a feminine noun. It refers to milk. It is almost always uncountable and thus has no plural in common usage. It's often used with 'trinken' (to drink) or 'in den Kaffee tun' (to put in coffee).

Beispielsatz

Ich trinke meine Milch kalt.

I drink my milk cold.

Brot

bread

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Brot is a neuter noun. It refers to bread. The plural form 'Brote' is used when referring to different types of bread or whole loaves. A single slice of bread is 'eine Scheibe Brot'. It's often used with 'essen' (to eat) or 'kaufen' (to buy).

Beispielsatz

Ich esse gern Brot zum Frühstück.

I like to eat bread for breakfast.

Supermarkt

supermarket

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Supermarkt is a masculine noun. It refers to a supermarket. The plural form involves an umlaut. Common prepositions include 'im Supermarkt' (in the supermarket, dative for location) and 'zum Supermarkt gehen' (to go to the supermarket, dative for direction).

Beispielsatz

Ich kaufe im Supermarkt ein.

I do my shopping in the supermarket.

Straße

street

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Straße is a feminine noun. It refers to a street or road. The plural form is regular. Common prepositions include 'auf der Straße' (on the street, dative for location) or 'über die Straße gehen' (to cross the street, accusative for direction).

Beispielsatz

Das Auto fährt auf der Straße.

The car is driving on the street.

Bus

bus

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Bus is a masculine noun. It refers to a bus. The plural form doubles the 's'. Common prepositions include 'mit dem Bus fahren' (to go by bus, dative with 'mit') or 'auf den Bus warten' (to wait for the bus, accusative with 'auf').

Beispielsatz

Ich fahre jeden Tag mit dem Bus zur Arbeit.

I go to work by bus every day.

Auto

car

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Auto is a neuter noun. It refers to a car. The plural form adds '-s'. Common expressions include 'mit dem Auto fahren' (to go by car, dative with 'mit') or 'ein Auto kaufen' (to buy a car).

Beispielsatz

Ich fahre mein Auto zur Werkstatt.

I'm driving my car to the garage.

Uhr

clock, watch, o'clock

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Uhr is a feminine noun. It can mean 'clock', 'watch', or 'o'clock'. The plural form is regular. When telling time, 'um' (at) is used with the time (e.g., um drei Uhr - at three o'clock). 'Wie spät ist es?' (What time is it?) is a common question.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe eine neue Uhr gekauft.

I bought a new watch.
