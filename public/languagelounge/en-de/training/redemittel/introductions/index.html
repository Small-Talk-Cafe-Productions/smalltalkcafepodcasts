Guten Tag!

Good day! / Hello!

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A formal greeting used throughout the day, typically until early evening. No specific grammatical rules apply beyond being a fixed phrase.

Beispielsatz

Guten Tag! Wie geht es Ihnen?

Good day! How are you?

Hallo!

Hello! / Hi!

📌 Grammatiknotiz

An informal greeting, suitable for friends, family, and people you know well. No specific grammatical rules apply.

Beispielsatz

Hallo! Wie geht's dir?

Hello! How are you?

Wie heißen Sie?

What is your name? (formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Formal question. The verb 'heißen' (to be called) is conjugated for 'Sie' (you, formal). The sentence structure is question word + verb + subject.

Beispielsatz

Entschuldigen Sie, wie heißen Sie?

Excuse me, what is your name?

Ich heiße...

My name is... / I am called...

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Standard way to state your name. 'heißen' is a regular verb. Subject 'Ich' (I) is followed by the conjugated verb 'heiße' and then your name.

Beispielsatz

Ich heiße Anna Schmidt.

My name is Anna Schmidt.

Mein Name ist...

My name is...

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Another common way to state your name. 'Mein Name' (my name) is the subject, followed by the verb 'ist' (is) and then your name.

Beispielsatz

Mein Name ist Thomas Müller.

My name is Thomas Müller.

Ich bin...

I am...

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Versatile phrase using the verb 'sein' (to be). Can be followed by a name, profession, or age. 'Ich' (I) is the subject, 'bin' is the conjugated verb.

Beispielsatz

Ich bin Maria.

I am Maria.

Woher kommen Sie?

Where are you from? (formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Formal question about origin. 'Woher' (from where) is the question word, followed by the verb 'kommen' (to come) conjugated for 'Sie' (you, formal).

Beispielsatz

Woher kommen Sie, wenn ich fragen darf?

Where are you from, if I may ask?

Ich komme aus...

I come from...

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Standard way to state your country or city of origin. 'aus' (from) is a preposition that always takes the dative case. 'Ich' (I) is the subject, 'komme' is the conjugated verb.

Beispielsatz

Ich komme aus Deutschland.

I come from Germany.

Wo wohnen Sie?

Where do you live? (formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Formal question about residence. 'Wo' (where) is the question word, followed by the verb 'wohnen' (to live) conjugated for 'Sie' (you, formal).

Beispielsatz

Entschuldigen Sie, wo wohnen Sie?

Excuse me, where do you live?

Ich wohne in...

I live in...

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Standard way to state your place of residence. 'in' (in) is a preposition that takes the dative case when indicating a fixed location. 'Ich' (I) is the subject, 'wohne' is the conjugated verb.

Beispielsatz

Ich wohne in München.

I live in Munich.

Was machen Sie beruflich?

What do you do for a living? (formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Formal question about profession. 'Was' (what) is the question word, followed by the verb 'machen' (to do) conjugated for 'Sie' (you, formal). 'beruflich' means 'professionally'.

Beispielsatz

Darf ich fragen, was machen Sie beruflich?

May I ask, what do you do for a living?

Ich bin... (Beruf)

I am a... (profession)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Standard way to state your profession. In German, you usually do not use an indefinite article (a/an) before a profession. 'Ich' (I) is the subject, 'bin' is the conjugated verb 'sein'.

Beispielsatz

Ich bin Lehrerin.

I am a teacher (female).

Ich spreche...

I speak...

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Used to state which languages you speak. 'Ich' (I) is the subject, 'spreche' is the conjugated verb 'sprechen' (to speak).

Beispielsatz

Ich spreche Deutsch und Englisch.

I speak German and English.

Ich lerne Deutsch.

I am learning German.

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Standard phrase to indicate you are currently learning German. 'Ich' (I) is the subject, 'lerne' is the conjugated verb 'lernen' (to learn).

Beispielsatz

Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Deutschland leben möchte.

I am learning German because I want to live in Germany.

Ich bin ... Jahre alt.

I am ... years old.

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Standard way to state your age. 'Ich' (I) is the subject, 'bin' is the conjugated verb 'sein' (to be). The number comes before 'Jahre alt' (years old).

Beispielsatz

Ich bin 30 Jahre alt.

I am 30 years old.

Es freut mich, Sie kennenzulernen.

It's a pleasure to meet you. (formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A polite and formal phrase. 'Es freut mich' (it pleases me) is a fixed expression. 'Sie' is the accusative form of 'you' (formal), and 'kennenzulernen' is an infinitive clause.

Beispielsatz

Guten Tag, Herr Meier. Es freut mich, Sie kennenzulernen.

Good day, Mr. Meier. It's a pleasure to meet you.

Schön, Sie kennenzulernen.

Nice to meet you. (formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A slightly shorter and very common formal phrase. 'Schön' (nice/beautiful) is an adjective. 'Sie' is the accusative form of 'you' (formal), and 'kennenzulernen' is an infinitive clause.

Beispielsatz

Hallo, ich bin Tom. Schön, Sie kennenzulernen.

Hello, I'm Tom. Nice to meet you.

Und Sie?

And you? (formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Used to politely ask the same question back to a formal person. It's a shortened form of 'Und wie geht es Ihnen?' or 'Und woher kommen Sie?' etc.

Beispielsatz

Mir geht es gut, danke. Und Sie?

I'm fine, thank you. And you?

Das ist...

This is...

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Used to introduce another person. 'Das' (that/this) acts as a demonstrative pronoun, followed by the verb 'ist' (is) and the name of the person being introduced.

Beispielsatz

Das ist meine Kollegin, Frau Schmidt.

This is my colleague, Mrs. Schmidt.

Darf ich mich vorstellen?

May I introduce myself?

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A polite and formal way to ask permission to introduce yourself. 'Darf' is the conjugated modal verb 'dürfen' (may/to be allowed). 'mich' is the accusative reflexive pronoun.

Beispielsatz

Guten Tag. Darf ich mich vorstellen? Mein Name ist Klaus Meier.

Good day. May I introduce myself? My name is Klaus Meier.
