dürfen

to be allowed to / may

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Modal verb. Expresses permission. Stem vowel changes in singular present. In Perfekt with another infinitive, a double infinitive construction (haben + infinitive + dürfen) is common, e.g., 'Ich habe es nicht tun dürfen'.

Beispielsatz

Darf ich herein?

May I come in?

können

to be able to / can

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Modal verb. Expresses ability or possibility. Stem vowel changes in singular present. In Perfekt with another infinitive, a double infinitive construction (haben + infinitive + können) is common.

Beispielsatz

Ich kann Deutsch sprechen.

I can speak German.

mögen

to like

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Modal verb. Expresses liking. Often replaced by 'möchten' for polite wishes. Stem vowel changes in singular present. In Perfekt with another infinitive, a double infinitive construction (haben + infinitive + mögen) is common.

Beispielsatz

Ich mag keinen Kaffee.

I don't like coffee.

möchten

would like to

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Modal-like verb, conditional form of 'mögen'. Expresses polite wishes. It does not have distinct Präteritum or Perfekt forms as a standalone verb; its past is usually expressed with 'mögen' in Präteritum ('mochte') or other constructions.

Beispielsatz

Ich möchte einen Kaffee bestellen.

I would like to order a coffee.

müssen

to have to / must

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Modal verb. Expresses necessity or obligation. Stem vowel changes in singular present. In Perfekt with another infinitive, a double infinitive construction (haben + infinitive + müssen) is common.

Beispielsatz

Ich muss arbeiten.

I have to work.

sollen

to be supposed to / should

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Modal verb. Expresses obligation, advice, or a rumor. Stem vowel changes in singular present. In Perfekt with another infinitive, a double infinitive construction (haben + infinitive + sollen) is common.

Beispielsatz

Du sollst deine Hausaufgaben machen.

You should do your homework.

wollen

to want to

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Modal verb. Expresses a strong desire or intention. Stem vowel changes in singular present. In Perfekt with another infinitive, a double infinitive construction (haben + infinitive + wollen) is common.

Beispielsatz

Ich will nach Hause gehen.

I want to go home.

haben

to have

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Used for possession and as an auxiliary verb for forming Perfekt with most verbs.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe ein Buch.

I have a book.

sein

to be

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Used for existence, state, and as an auxiliary verb for forming Perfekt with verbs of movement or change of state.

Beispielsatz

Ich bin müde.

I am tired.

werden

to become

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Means 'to become' and is used as an auxiliary verb for the future tense and passive voice (later levels).

Beispielsatz

Ich werde Arzt.

I will become a doctor.

gehen

to go / to walk

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Forms Perfekt with 'sein' as it indicates movement to a destination.

Beispielsatz

Ich gehe nach Hause.

I am going home.

kommen

to come

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Forms Perfekt with 'sein' as it indicates movement.

Beispielsatz

Kannst du bitte kommen?

Can you please come?

machen

to do / to make

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Regular verb. Weak conjugation.

Beispielsatz

Ich mache meine Hausaufgaben.

I do my homework.

sprechen

to speak

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Stem vowel changes in singular present (e -> i).

Beispielsatz

Ich spreche Deutsch.

I speak German.

essen

to eat

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Stem vowel changes in singular present (e -> i).

Beispielsatz

Was möchtest du essen?

What would you like to eat?

trinken

to drink

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb.

Beispielsatz

Ich trinke Wasser.

I drink water.

lesen

to read

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Stem vowel changes in singular present (e -> ie).

Beispielsatz

Ich lese gern Bücher.

I like to read books.

schreiben

to write

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb.

Beispielsatz

Ich schreibe eine E-Mail.

I am writing an email.

wissen

to know (a fact)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Often called a 'mixed verb' because it has characteristics of both strong and weak verbs. Conjugation pattern is similar to modal verbs in present tense.

Beispielsatz

Ich weiß die Antwort nicht.

I don't know the answer.

finden

to find / to think (an opinion)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Can also be used to express an opinion, e.g., 'Ich finde das gut' (I think that's good).

Beispielsatz

Ich kann meine Schlüssel nicht finden.

I can't find my keys.

glauben

to believe / to think

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Regular verb. Weak conjugation. Used to express belief or opinion.

Beispielsatz

Ich glaube dir.

I believe you.

brauchen

to need

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Regular verb. Weak conjugation. Can sometimes function modally with 'zu' in negative sentences (e.g., 'Du brauchst nicht zu kommen' = 'You don't need to come').

Beispielsatz

Ich brauche deine Hilfe.

I need your help.

verstehen

to understand

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Non-separable prefix 'ver-'. Forms Perfekt with 'haben'.

Beispielsatz

Ich verstehe nur Bahnhof.

I don't understand anything (lit. I only understand train station).

geben

to give

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Stem vowel changes in singular present (e -> i). Can also be used in the impersonal expression 'es gibt' (there is/are).

Beispielsatz

Kannst du mir das Buch geben?

Can you give me the book?

nehmen

to take

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Irregular verb. Stem vowel changes in singular present (e -> i).

Beispielsatz

Ich nehme einen Kaffee.

I'll take a coffee.
