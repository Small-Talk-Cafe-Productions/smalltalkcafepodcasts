Was sind Modalverben?

What are Modal Verbs?

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Modal verbs are special verbs that modify the meaning of a main verb. They express the speaker's attitude towards an action, such as ability, desire, necessity, or permission. In German, they are always used with another verb in its infinitive form.

Beispielsatz

Ich kann Deutsch sprechen.

I can speak German.

Satzbau mit Modalverben

Sentence Structure with Modal Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In a standard declarative sentence, the modal verb is always in the second position and conjugated according to the subject. The main verb, in its infinitive form, always stands at the very end of the sentence. This structure is fundamental for modal verbs.

Beispielsatz

Mein Bruder will heute Pizza essen.

My brother wants to eat pizza today.

Konjugationsmuster (Singular)

Conjugation Pattern (Singular)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A key characteristic of most modal verbs is their irregular conjugation in the singular. The first person singular (ich) and the third person singular (er/sie/es) forms are identical and do not have the typical '-t' ending for 'er/sie/es'. Many also have a stem vowel change in the singular forms.

Beispielsatz

Ich kann, du kannst, er/sie/es kann.

I can, you can, he/she/it can.

können (Fähigkeit, Möglichkeit)

can, to be able to (Ability, Possibility)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Können' expresses ability, skill, or possibility. It means someone is capable of doing something or that something is possible. The singular forms are irregular with a vowel change from 'ö' to 'a'.

Beispielsatz

Ich kann sehr gut Deutsch sprechen.

I can speak German very well.

wollen (Absicht, Wunsch)

to want to (Intention, Desire)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Wollen' expresses a strong intention or desire to do something. It implies a firm will or plan. The singular forms are irregular with a vowel change from 'o' to 'i'.

Beispielsatz

Ich will am Wochenende meine Freunde besuchen.

I want to visit my friends on the weekend.

müssen (Notwendigkeit, Zwang)

must, to have to (Necessity, Obligation)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Müssen' expresses a strong necessity or an external obligation. It means something absolutely has to be done, often due to rules, circumstances, or personal conviction. The singular forms are irregular with a vowel change from 'ü' to 'u'.

Beispielsatz

Ich muss jeden Tag arbeiten.

I have to work every day.

sollen (Ratschlag, Auftrag, Pflicht)

should, to be supposed to (Advice, Order, Duty)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Sollen' expresses advice, a recommendation, an order from someone else, or a duty/task. It's a milder form of obligation than 'müssen'. The singular forms are irregular but do not have a vowel change.

Beispielsatz

Du sollst deine Hausaufgaben machen.

You should do your homework.

dürfen (Erlaubnis, Verbot)

may, to be allowed to (Permission, Prohibition)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Dürfen' expresses permission or, when negated, prohibition. It indicates whether something is allowed or forbidden by rules, laws, or another person. The singular forms are irregular with a vowel change from 'ü' to 'a'.

Beispielsatz

Hier dürfen Kinder spielen.

Children are allowed to play here.

mögen (Vorliebe)

to like (Preference)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Mögen' expresses a general liking or preference for something or someone. At A1, it is most commonly used with nouns, not typically with an infinitive verb. The singular forms are irregular with a vowel change from 'ö' to 'a'.

Beispielsatz

Ich mag Schokolade sehr gern.

I like chocolate very much.

möchten (Höflicher Wunsch)

would like to (Polite Desire)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Möchten' is technically the Konjunktiv II form of 'mögen', but it functions as a standalone modal verb at A1. It expresses a polite wish or desire, often for something specific or an action. It is always used with an infinitive verb or a noun.

Beispielsatz

Ich möchte jetzt einen Kaffee trinken.

I would like to drink a coffee now.

Unterschied: können vs. dürfen

Difference: can (ability) vs. may (permission)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Können' refers to physical or mental ability, or a general possibility. 'Dürfen' refers to permission or authorization from an external source (rules, people). It's crucial not to mix these up, especially when asking for permission.

Beispielsatz

Ich kann schwimmen. (Ability) / Ich darf hier schwimmen. (Permission)

I can swim. / I am allowed to swim here.

Unterschied: müssen vs. sollen

Difference: must (strong obligation) vs. should (recommendation/duty)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Müssen' implies a strong, often unavoidable necessity or obligation, usually from external circumstances or rules. 'Sollen' suggests a milder obligation, a recommendation, or a duty assigned by someone else. The choice depends on the degree of compulsion.

Beispielsatz

Ich muss arbeiten. (I have no choice.) / Ich soll arbeiten. (Someone told me to / it's my duty.)

I must work. / I should work.

Negation mit Modalverben

Negation with Modal Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When negating a sentence with a modal verb, 'nicht' typically comes before the infinitive verb at the end of the sentence. If the modal verb is used without an infinitive (e.g., 'Ich mag keinen Kaffee'), 'nicht' or 'kein-' negates the noun directly.

Beispielsatz

Ich kann heute nicht kommen.

I cannot come today.

Modalverben in Fragen

Modal Verbs in Questions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In 'Ja/Nein' questions, the conjugated modal verb moves to the first position. In 'W-Fragen' (questions starting with a W-word), the W-word is in the first position, and the conjugated modal verb is in the second position. The infinitive verb remains at the end.

Beispielsatz

Kannst du mir helfen? / Was willst du essen?

Can you help me? / What do you want to eat?

Häufiger Fehler: Fehlender Infinitiv

Common Error: Missing Infinitive

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A very common mistake is to omit the main verb in its infinitive form at the end of the sentence. Modal verbs almost always require a main verb to complete their meaning, even if it seems implied in English. Only 'mögen' (to like) can stand alone with a noun.

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Ich muss nach Hause. Richtig: Ich muss nach Hause gehen.

Wrong: I must home. Right: I must go home.

Häufiger Fehler: Falsche Singular-Konjugation

Common Error: Incorrect Singular Conjugation

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Learners often apply regular verb conjugation rules to modal verbs, especially for the third person singular (er/sie/es). Remember that for most modal verbs, the 'ich' and 'er/sie/es' forms are identical and do not take the typical '-t' ending. Also, watch out for the stem vowel changes.

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Er kannst Deutsch sprechen. Richtig: Er kann Deutsch sprechen.

Wrong: He canst speak German. Right: He can speak German.
