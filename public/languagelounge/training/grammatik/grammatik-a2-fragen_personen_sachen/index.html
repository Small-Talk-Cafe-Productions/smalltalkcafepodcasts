Wer? Was? (Einführung)

Who? What? (Introduction)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

These are the two fundamental interrogative pronouns used to ask about people (`wer`) and things (`was`). They always stand at the beginning of a question. The choice between `wer` and `was` depends solely on whether you are asking about a person or a non-person (thing, animal, concept).

Beispielsatz

Wer ist das? Was ist das?

Who is that? What is that?

Wer? (Nominativ)

Who? (Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

`Wer` is used in the nominative case to ask for the subject of a sentence when that subject is a person. It functions as the grammatical subject itself and therefore determines the verb's conjugation (always singular, third person).

Beispielsatz

Wer hat angerufen?

Who called?

Was? (Nominativ)

What? (Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

`Was` is used in the nominative case to ask for the subject of a sentence when that subject is a thing, animal, or concept. Like `wer`, it functions as the grammatical subject and takes a singular, third-person verb.

Beispielsatz

Was liegt auf dem Tisch?

What is lying on the table?

Wen? (Akkusativ)

Whom? (Accusative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

`Wen` is the accusative form of `wer` and is used to ask for the direct object of a sentence when that direct object is a person. It answers the question 'Whom?' or 'Who?' when the person is receiving the action of the verb.

Beispielsatz

Wen hast du getroffen?

Whom did you meet? / Who did you meet?

Was? (Akkusativ)

What? (Accusative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

`Was` is used in the accusative case to ask for the direct object of a sentence when that direct object is a thing, animal, or concept. Importantly, the form of `was` does not change between the nominative and accusative cases.

Beispielsatz

Was kaufst du im Supermarkt?

What are you buying at the supermarket?

Wem? (Dativ)

To whom? For whom? (Dative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

`Wem` is the dative form of `wer` and is used to ask for the indirect object of a sentence when that indirect object is a person. It answers the question 'To whom?' or 'For whom?' and often appears with dative verbs or prepositions.

Beispielsatz

Wem gibst du das Buch?

To whom are you giving the book?

Wessen? (Genitiv)

Whose? (Genitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

`Wessen` is the genitive form of `wer` and is used to ask about possession for both people and things. It translates to 'Whose?' and is typically followed by the noun whose possession is being questioned.

Beispielsatz

Wessen Handy ist das?

Whose phone is that?

Präpositionen + wen? (Akkusativ für Personen)

Prepositions + whom? (Accusative for People)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a question about a person involves a preposition that always takes the accusative case, `wer` changes to `wen` and follows the preposition. Common accusative prepositions include `für`, `gegen`, `ohne`, `um`, `durch`.

Beispielsatz

Für wen ist dieses Geschenk?

For whom is this gift?

Präpositionen + wem? (Dativ für Personen)

Prepositions + whom? (Dative for People)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a question about a person involves a preposition that always takes the dative case, `wer` changes to `wem` and follows the preposition. Common dative prepositions include `mit`, `von`, `zu`, `nach`, `aus`, `bei`, `seit`.

Beispielsatz

Mit wem gehst du ins Kino?

With whom are you going to the cinema?

Präpositionen + was? (Akkusativ für Sachen)

Prepositions + what? (Accusative for Things)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a question about a thing involves a preposition that takes the accusative case, `was` follows the preposition. The form `was` does not change. While grammatically correct, it's often more common to use `wo-` compounds (e.g., `wofür`, `wogegen`) for things, especially in spoken German.

Beispielsatz

Für was interessierst du dich?

For what are you interested?

wo- + Präpositionen (für Sachen, Dativ/Genitiv)

wo- + Prepositions (for Things, Dative/Genitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To ask questions about things (not people) that require a dative or genitive preposition, or when the preposition refers to a location, we use `wo-` compounds. This is formed by `wo` + preposition (e.g., `womit`, `wovon`, `wozu`). This construction replaces `Präposition + was` for these cases.

Beispielsatz

Womit schreibst du den Brief?

With what are you writing the letter?

wor- + Präpositionen (für Sachen, bei Vokal)

wor- + Prepositions (for Things, with Vowel)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When forming `wo-` compounds for things and the preposition begins with a vowel (e.g., `über`, `auf`, `an`, `in`, `um`), an `r` is inserted between `wo` and the preposition for phonetic reasons. This results in `wor-` + preposition (e.g., `worüber`, `worauf`, `woran`).

Beispielsatz

Worüber sprecht ihr?

About what are you talking?

Keine Dativform für 'was'

No Dative form for 'was'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Unlike `wer` which has `wem` in the dative, the interrogative pronoun `was` does not have a distinct dative form. You cannot say 'wem' when referring to a thing. Instead, for dative questions about things, you must use `wo-` compounds (e.g., `womit`, `wovon`, `wozu`) or `Präposition + was` (less common for dative).

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Wem gibst du den Stift? (wenn gemeint ist 'mit was')

Incorrect: To whom do you give the pen? (if meaning 'with what')

Wer, Wen, Wem (Zusammenfassung der Fälle)

Who, Whom, To whom (Case Summary)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This card summarizes the declension of `wer` for asking about people in different cases. It's crucial to correctly identify the grammatical role (subject, direct object, indirect object) of the person being asked about to choose the right form.

Beispielsatz

Wer kommt? Wen siehst du? Wem hilfst du?

Who is coming? Whom do you see? Whom do you help?

Was vs. wo- (Zusammenfassung für Sachen)

What vs. wo- compounds (Summary for Things)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This card summarizes how to ask about things in different grammatical contexts. Use `was` for nominative and accusative questions. For questions involving prepositions (especially dative or genitive), use `wo-` compounds (or `wor-` before vowels) to refer to things.

Beispielsatz

Was ist das? Was kaufst du? Womit spielst du? Worüber sprichst du?

What is that? What are you buying? With what are you playing? About what are you talking?

Zwei-Wege-Präpositionen mit Personen

Two-Way Prepositions with People

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When asking about people with two-way prepositions (e.g., `an`, `auf`, `in`, `neben`, `über`, `unter`, `vor`, `zwischen`), the case depends on whether there is a change of location (accusative) or a static location (dative). Therefore, you will use either `an wen / auf wen` (movement) or `an wem / auf wem` (no movement).

Beispielsatz

An wen denkst du? An wem sitzt du?

About whom are you thinking? Next to whom are you sitting?

Zwei-Wege-Präpositionen mit Sachen (wor- Komposita)

Two-Way Prepositions with Things (wor- Compounds)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For questions about things involving two-way prepositions, `wor-` compounds are used, and the choice between accusative and dative meaning is often implied by the verb. For example, `worauf` can mean 'on what (static)' or 'onto what (movement)', but the `wor-` form remains the same. The context of the verb clarifies the case.

Beispielsatz

Worauf legst du das Buch? Worauf liegt das Buch?

Onto what are you putting the book? On what is the book lying?
