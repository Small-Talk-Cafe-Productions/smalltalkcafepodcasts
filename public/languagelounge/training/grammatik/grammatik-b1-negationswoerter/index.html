nicht (Verbnegation)

not (verb negation)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When 'nicht' negates the entire verb or the action expressed by the verb, it typically stands at the end of the sentence. If there's a second part of the verb (e.g., participle, infinitive, separable prefix), 'nicht' comes before that part.

Beispielsatz

Ich komme heute nicht.

I am not coming today.

nicht (Adjektiv/Adverb)

not (adjective/adverb negation)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To negate an adjective or an adverb, 'nicht' is placed directly before the word it negates. This emphasizes that only that specific quality or manner is being denied.

Beispielsatz

Das Wetter ist heute nicht schön.

The weather is not beautiful today.

nicht (Präpositionalphrase)

not (prepositional phrase negation)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When 'nicht' negates a specific prepositional phrase, it is placed directly before that phrase. This indicates that the location, time, or manner described by the phrase is incorrect.

Beispielsatz

Ich warte nicht auf den Bus.

I am not waiting for the bus.

nicht (Nomen mit Artikel/Possessivpronomen)

not (noun with article/possessive pronoun)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Use 'nicht' to negate a noun that is accompanied by a definite article (der, die, das) or a possessive pronoun (mein, dein, sein, etc.). 'Nicht' is placed directly before the article or pronoun.

Beispielsatz

Das ist nicht mein Auto.

That is not my car.

kein (Nomen ohne Artikel/Indefinitartikel)

no (indefinite noun negation)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Kein' is used to negate nouns that would otherwise appear without an article or with an indefinite article (ein/eine). It means 'no' or 'not a/an' and declines like the indefinite article 'ein'.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe kein Geld.

I have no money.

kein - Deklination

kein - Declension

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Kein' declines like the indefinite article 'ein' and possessive pronouns. Its endings change according to gender, number, and case of the noun it negates. It takes the same endings as 'ein' in singular and 'die' in plural.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe keinen Hunger. (Akk. Maskulin)

I have no hunger. / I am not hungry.

Kontrast: nicht vs. kein

Contrast: nicht vs. kein

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This is a fundamental distinction. Use 'nicht' to negate verbs, adjectives, adverbs, proper nouns, or nouns with definite articles/possessive pronouns. Use 'kein' to negate indefinite nouns (nouns without an article or with an indefinite article 'ein/eine').

Beispielsatz

Ich trinke keinen Kaffee. (indefinite noun) vs. Ich trinke den Kaffee nicht. (definite noun)

I don't drink coffee. vs. I don't drink the coffee.

nichts

nothing

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Nichts' is an indefinite negative pronoun, meaning 'nothing'. It is the negation of 'etwas' (something) and functions as a noun substitute. It does not decline and usually stands at the end of the sentence or before a second verb part.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe nichts verstanden.

I understood nothing.

noch nicht

not yet

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Noch nicht' is used to express that something has not happened up to the present moment, but is expected to happen in the future. It is the negation of 'schon' (already).

Beispielsatz

Ich habe die E-Mail noch nicht gelesen.

I have not read the email yet.

nicht mehr

no longer, not anymore

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Nicht mehr' is used to express that something that used to happen or be true, no longer happens or is true. It indicates a change in state or action. It is the negation of 'noch' (still).

Beispielsatz

Er wohnt nicht mehr hier.

He no longer lives here.

Kontrast: noch nicht vs. nicht mehr

Contrast: not yet vs. no longer

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This contrast is crucial for expressing temporal negation. 'Noch nicht' implies an action or state is pending but expected (e.g., 'It's not ready yet'). 'Nicht mehr' implies an action or state has ceased (e.g., 'It's no longer ready').

Beispielsatz

Ich bin noch nicht fertig. (I will be soon) vs. Ich bin nicht mehr fertig. (I was, but now I'm not)

I am not ready yet. vs. I am no longer ready.

nie / niemals

never

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Nie' and 'niemals' are adverbs of negation meaning 'never'. They negate frequency or occurrence. 'Niemals' is slightly more emphatic than 'nie'. They are the negation of 'immer' (always) or 'oft' (often).

Beispielsatz

Ich war noch nie in Berlin.

I have never been to Berlin.

niemand

nobody, no one

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Niemand' is an indefinite negative pronoun, meaning 'nobody' or 'no one'. It is the negation of 'jemand' (somebody/someone). It declines similarly to 'man' or 'jemand', primarily in the accusative and dative cases.

Beispielsatz

Niemand hat mich angerufen.

Nobody called me.

nirgends / nirgendwo

nowhere

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Nirgends' and 'nirgendwo' are adverbs of place meaning 'nowhere'. They are the negation of 'irgendwo' (somewhere) or 'überall' (everywhere). 'Nirgends' is often preferred in spoken language.

Beispielsatz

Ich kann meine Schlüssel nirgends finden.

I can't find my keys anywhere (nowhere).

gar nicht / überhaupt nicht

not at all, absolutely not

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Gar nicht' and 'überhaupt nicht' are used for emphatic negation, meaning 'not at all' or 'absolutely not'. They strengthen the 'nicht' and are placed directly before it, or before the element being negated if 'nicht' is already before it.

Beispielsatz

Ich mag das gar nicht.

I don't like that at all.

keiner / keine / keines (Pronomen)

none (pronoun form)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a noun negated by 'kein' is omitted because it's clear from context, 'kein' functions as an indefinite negative pronoun. It takes the same endings as the definite article 'der, die, das' in the respective case and gender.

Beispielsatz

Hast du Geschwister? Nein, ich habe keine.

Do you have siblings? No, I have none.

Kontrast: nichts vs. keiner/keine/keines

Contrast: nothing vs. none

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Nichts' is a general negative pronoun meaning 'nothing' and does not refer to a specific type of noun. 'Keiner/keine/keines' are negative pronouns that replace a specific noun that would otherwise be negated by 'kein', meaning 'none' of a particular kind.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe nichts im Kühlschrank. (general) vs. Ich habe keine Milch im Kühlschrank. (specific noun implied)

I have nothing in the fridge. vs. I have no milk in the fridge (or 'I have none' if milk is understood).
