Der Akkusativ (Wen oder was?)

The Accusative Case (Whom or what?)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Akkusativ is used for the direct object of a sentence. It answers the question 'Wen?' (Whom?) for people/animals or 'Was?' (What?) for things. This case indicates who or what receives the action of the verb.

Beispielsatz

Ich sehe den Mann.

I see the man.

Akkusativ vs. Nominativ

Accusative vs. Nominative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Nominativ is the subject of the sentence, answering 'Wer oder was?' (Who or what?). The Akkusativ is the direct object, answering 'Wen oder was?'. The main difference in form is that only masculine nouns change their article from Nominativ to Akkusativ.

Beispielsatz

Der Mann (Nom.) sieht den Hund (Akk.).

The man (subject) sees the dog (direct object).

Bestimmte Artikel im Akkusativ (Übersicht)

Definite Articles in the Accusative Case (Overview)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This table shows how definite articles change in the Akkusativ case. Notice that only the masculine article 'der' changes to 'den'. Feminine, neuter, and plural articles remain the same as in Nominativ.

Beispielsatz

Ich kaufe den Tisch, die Lampe, das Buch und die Stühle.

I buy the table, the lamp, the book, and the chairs.

der → den (Maskulin)

Masculine Definite Article

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a masculine noun is the direct object in a sentence, its definite article 'der' changes to 'den'. This is the most crucial article change to remember for the Akkusativ case.

Beispielsatz

Ich suche den Mann.

I am looking for the man.

die → die (Feminin)

Feminine Definite Article

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For feminine nouns acting as direct objects, the definite article 'die' remains unchanged in the Akkusativ case. It looks exactly the same as in Nominativ.

Beispielsatz

Er kauft die Zeitung.

He buys the newspaper.

das → das (Neutrum)

Neuter Definite Article

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Similar to feminine nouns, neuter nouns that are direct objects keep their definite article 'das' unchanged in the Akkusativ case. There is no difference from the Nominativ form.

Beispielsatz

Sie liest das Buch.

She reads the book.

die → die (Plural)

Plural Definite Article

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For plural nouns serving as direct objects, the definite article 'die' also remains unchanged in the Akkusativ case. This applies to all genders in plural form.

Beispielsatz

Wir besuchen die Freunde.

We visit the friends.

Unbestimmte Artikel im Akkusativ (Übersicht)

Indefinite Articles in the Accusative Case (Overview)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Indefinite articles (ein, eine, ein) follow the same pattern as definite articles in Akkusativ. Only the masculine form 'ein' changes to 'einen'. Feminine and neuter forms remain unchanged. 'Kein' (no/not any) also follows this pattern.

Beispielsatz

Ich brauche einen Stift, eine Tasse und ein Blatt.

I need a pen, a cup, and a sheet of paper.

ein → einen (Maskulin)

Masculine Indefinite Article

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For masculine nouns that are direct objects, the indefinite article 'ein' takes an '-en' ending, becoming 'einen'. This change is parallel to 'der' becoming 'den'.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe einen Hund.

I have a dog.

eine → eine (Feminin)

Feminine Indefinite Article

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The indefinite article 'eine' for feminine nouns remains unchanged in the Akkusativ case. It is identical to its Nominativ form.

Beispielsatz

Ich möchte eine Pizza.

I would like a pizza.

ein → ein (Neutrum)

Neuter Indefinite Article

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For neuter nouns, the indefinite article 'ein' also remains unchanged in the Akkusativ case. It is identical to its Nominativ form.

Beispielsatz

Er hat ein Auto.

He has a car.

kein → keinen (Maskulin), keine (Feminin/Plural), kein (Neutrum)

Negative Indefinite Article ('Kein')

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The negative indefinite article 'kein' (no/not any) follows the same declension pattern as 'ein'. Only the masculine form changes from 'kein' to 'keinen' in Akkusativ. Feminine 'keine', neuter 'kein', and plural 'keine' remain unchanged.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe keinen Bruder, keine Schwester und kein Auto.

I have no brother, no sister, and no car.

Possessivartikel im Akkusativ (Übersicht)

Possessive Articles in the Accusative Case (Overview)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Possessive articles (mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, ihr/Ihr) behave exactly like indefinite articles in Akkusativ. Only the masculine forms receive an '-en' ending (e.g., 'meinen', 'deinen'). Feminine, neuter, and plural forms remain unchanged.

Beispielsatz

Ich suche meinen Rucksack, deine Tasche und sein Handy.

I am looking for my backpack, your bag, and his phone.

Personalpronomen im Akkusativ

Personal Pronouns in the Accusative Case

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Personal pronouns also change their form when used as direct objects in the Akkusativ case. It's important to memorize these specific forms, as they are very common in everyday conversation.

Beispielsatz

Ich liebe dich.

I love you.

Verben mit Akkusativ

Verbs taking the Accusative Case

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Many common verbs in German require a direct object in the Akkusativ case. These verbs describe actions that are performed on something or someone. Examples include 'haben', 'suchen', 'finden', 'brauchen', 'lieben', 'sehen', 'kaufen', 'essen', 'trinken', 'besuchen', 'kennen', 'mögen'.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe einen Hund.

I have a dog.

Präpositionen mit Akkusativ

Prepositions always taking the Accusative Case

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Certain prepositions always trigger the Akkusativ case for the noun or pronoun that follows them, regardless of the verb or context. The most important ones for A1 are: durch, für, gegen, ohne, um.

Beispielsatz

Ich gehe durch den Park.

I walk through the park.
