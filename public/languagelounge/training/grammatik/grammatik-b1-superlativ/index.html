Der Superlativ: Grundform (am + -sten)

The Superlative: Basic Form (predicative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The superlative is used to compare three or more things and state which one possesses a quality to the highest degree. The basic predicative form, used when the adjective describes the subject and stands after the verb, is formed with 'am' + adjective stem + '-sten'. This form acts adverbially and is invariable.

Beispielsatz

Dieses Buch ist am interessantesten.

This book is the most interesting.

Der Superlativ: Attributive Form (der/die/das + -ste)

The Superlative: Attributive Form

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When the superlative adjective modifies a noun directly (i.e., stands before it), it must be declined. This form uses the definite article (der, die, das) followed by the adjective stem + '-ste' and the appropriate adjective ending. It follows the rules of weak adjective declension after a definite article.

Beispielsatz

Das ist der schnellste Zug Deutschlands.

That is the fastest train in Germany.

Regelmäßige Bildung: -sten / -ste

Regular Formation: -sten / -ste

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Most adjectives form their superlative regularly by adding '-sten' for the predicative form (am + -sten) or '-ste' for the attributive form (der/die/das + -ste) to the adjective stem. No further changes are usually required for these adjectives.

Beispielsatz

Dieser Film ist am lustigsten.

This film is the funniest.

Bildung: Adjektive auf -d, -t, -s, -ß, -sch, -z, -x

Formation: Adjectives ending in -d, -t, -s, -ß, -sch, -z, -x

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For adjectives whose stem ends in -d, -t, -s, -ß, -sch, -z, or -x, an 'e' is inserted before the '-sten' or '-ste' ending. This 'e' makes the pronunciation easier and smoother. For example, 'alt' becomes 'am ältesten' or 'der älteste'.

Beispielsatz

Das ist der breiteste Fluss der Region.

That is the widest river in the region.

Bildung: Umlaut bei a, o, u

Formation: Umlaut with a, o, u

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Many monosyllabic adjectives with the stem vowels 'a', 'o', or 'u' take an umlaut (ä, ö, ü) in the superlative form, similar to their comparative form. This rule applies to both predicative and attributive superlatives. Common examples include 'alt', 'jung', 'kurz', 'lang', 'oft', 'scharf', 'stark', 'warm'.

Beispielsatz

Mein jüngster Bruder ist noch in der Schule.

My youngest brother is still in school.

Bildung: Adjektive auf -el, -er

Formation: Adjectives ending in -el, -er

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For adjectives whose stem ends in '-el' or '-er', the 'e' before the 'l' or 'r' is dropped when forming the superlative. This simplifies the word and avoids an awkward double 'e' sound. For example, 'dunkel' becomes 'dunkl-' before adding '-sten' or '-ste'.

Beispielsatz

Das ist der dunkelste Raum im Haus.

That is the darkest room in the house.

Unregelmäßiger Superlativ: gut

Irregular Superlative: good

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The adjective 'gut' (good) has an irregular superlative form. It changes to 'besser' in the comparative and 'am besten' (predicative) or 'der/die/das beste' (attributive) in the superlative. This is a common and important irregularity to memorize.

Beispielsatz

Das ist die beste Lösung für das Problem.

That is the best solution for the problem.

Unregelmäßiger Superlativ: gern

Irregular Superlative: gladly / like to

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The adverb 'gern' (gladly, like to) also has an irregular superlative form. It changes to 'lieber' in the comparative and 'am liebsten' in the superlative. This form is always predicative/adverbial and expresses what one likes to do most.

Beispielsatz

Ich esse am liebsten Pizza.

I like to eat pizza the most.

Unregelmäßiger Superlativ: viel

Irregular Superlative: much/many

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The adjective/adverb 'viel' (much/many) has an irregular superlative form. It changes to 'mehr' in the comparative and 'am meisten' (predicative/adverbial) or 'der/die/das meiste' (attributive) in the superlative. It indicates the largest quantity or amount.

Beispielsatz

Er hat am meisten Geld gespart.

He has saved the most money.

Unregelmäßiger Superlativ: nah

Irregular Superlative: near

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The adjective 'nah' (near) has an irregular superlative form. It changes to 'näher' in the comparative and 'am nächsten' (predicative) or 'der/die/das nächste' (attributive) in the superlative. Note the umlaut and the 'ch' sound.

Beispielsatz

Die Bushaltestelle ist am nächsten.

The bus stop is the nearest.

Unregelmäßiger Superlativ: hoch

Irregular Superlative: high

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The adjective 'hoch' (high) has an irregular superlative form. It changes to 'höher' in the comparative and 'am höchsten' (predicative) or 'der/die/das höchste' (attributive) in the superlative. The 'c' in 'hoch' is dropped before adding the endings.

Beispielsatz

Der Mount Everest ist der höchste Berg der Welt.

Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.

Unregelmäßiger Superlativ: oft

Irregular Superlative: often

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The adverb 'oft' (often) has an irregular superlative form. It changes to 'öfter' or 'häufiger' in the comparative and 'am häufigsten' or 'am öftesten' in the superlative. 'Am häufigsten' is generally more common and preferred in formal contexts.

Beispielsatz

Sie besucht ihre Großeltern am häufigsten.

She visits her grandparents most often.

Superlativ mit 'aller-'

Superlative with 'aller-' (the very best/most)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To emphasize the superlative, especially in the attributive form, you can add 'aller-' before the adjective. This means 'the very best', 'the absolute most', or 'the greatest of all'. It is commonly used with 'beste' (allerbeste) but can be applied to other superlatives as well.

Beispielsatz

Das ist der allerbeste Kaffee, den ich je getrunken habe!

That is the very best coffee I have ever drunk!

Adjektive ohne Superlativ

Adjectives without Superlative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Some adjectives describe absolute states or qualities that cannot be graded, and therefore do not have a superlative form. Examples include 'fertig' (finished), 'tot' (dead), 'schwanger' (pregnant), 'einzig' (unique), 'leer' (empty), 'voll' (full), 'optimal' (optimal), 'maximal' (maximal), 'minimal' (minimal). You cannot be 'more dead' or 'most pregnant'.

Beispielsatz

Das Glas ist voll.

The glass is full.

Superlativ mit Präpositionen

Superlative with Prepositions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When comparing a superlative within a group, prepositions like 'von' (of/among) or 'unter' (among) are often used, especially with the predicative 'am + -sten' form. 'Von' is typically used for things or places, while 'unter' is used for people or a collective group. This clarifies the scope of the comparison.

Beispielsatz

Er ist der größte von allen Schülern.

He is the tallest of all students.

Häufiger Fehler: am + -sten vs. Attributiver Superlativ

Common Error: 'am + -sten' vs. Attributive Superlative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A very common mistake is confusing the predicative 'am + -sten' form with the attributive 'der/die/das + -ste' form. 'Am + -sten' is an adverbial form and never modifies a noun directly. The attributive form always precedes and declines with a noun. Do not combine 'am' with a declined adjective before a noun.

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Das ist der am schnellsten Zug. Richtig: Das ist der schnellste Zug. / Der Zug ist am schnellsten.

Wrong: That is the 'am fastest' train. Correct: That is the fastest train. / The train is the fastest.

Attributiver Superlativ: Deklination nach unbestimmtem Artikel oder Possessivpronomen

Attributive Superlative: Declension after Indefinite Article or Possessive Pronoun

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When an attributive superlative follows an indefinite article (ein, eine) or a possessive pronoun (mein, dein, sein, etc.), it follows mixed adjective declension rules. This means the adjective takes strong endings in Nominative Masculine and Nominative/Accusative Neuter, and weak endings in all other cases. The '-ste' ending is still present before the declension suffix.

Beispielsatz

Das war ein schönster Tag in meinem Leben.

That was a most beautiful day in my life.
