Negation mit nicht und kein (Überblick)

Negation with nicht and kein (Overview)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

German uses two main words for negation: 'nicht' and 'kein'. 'Nicht' negates verbs, adjectives, adverbs, proper nouns, definite articles, possessive pronouns, and personal pronouns. 'Kein' negates nouns that appear with an indefinite article or no article at all, functioning as a negative indefinite article.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe kein Auto, aber ich fahre nicht gern Fahrrad.

I don't have a car, but I don't like riding a bike.

Negation mit nicht (Allgemeine Regel)

Negation with nicht (General Rule)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Nicht' is used to negate verbs, adjectives, adverbs, proper nouns, definite articles, possessive pronouns, and personal pronouns. It makes a statement negative without changing the grammatical case of any noun. Its placement depends on what specific element is being negated.

Beispielsatz

Er ist nicht müde.

He is not tired.

nicht bei Verben (Satznegation)

nicht with Verbs (Sentence Negation)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When 'nicht' negates the entire verb or the whole sentence, it typically stands at the end of the sentence. If there's a separable prefix or an infinitive/participle at the end (e.g., with modal verbs or perfect tense), 'nicht' comes before that final verb part.

Beispielsatz

Ich komme heute Abend nicht.

I am not coming this evening.

nicht bei Adjektiven und Adverbien

nicht with Adjectives and Adverbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When 'nicht' negates an adjective or an adverb, it is placed directly before the adjective or adverb it modifies. This makes the specific quality or manner negative, rather than the entire action or statement.

Beispielsatz

Das Wetter ist nicht schön.

The weather is not nice.

nicht bei Präpositionalphrase / Satzteil

nicht with Prepositional Phrase / Specific Sentence Part

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To negate a specific part of the sentence, such as a prepositional phrase, a temporal expression, or a place, 'nicht' is placed directly before the element it negates. This clarifies exactly what is being denied, often implying an alternative.

Beispielsatz

Ich fahre nicht nach Berlin.

I am not driving to Berlin.

nicht bei Eigennamen, bestimmten Artikeln, Possessivpronomen, Personalpronomen

nicht with Proper Nouns, Definite Articles, Possessive Pronouns, Personal Pronouns

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When negating a proper noun, a noun with a definite article, a possessive pronoun, or a personal pronoun, 'nicht' is placed directly before the element being negated. This indicates that the specific person or item is not involved or is not the one referred to.

Beispielsatz

Das ist nicht der Lehrer.

That is not the teacher.

Negation mit kein (Allgemeine Regel)

Negation with kein (General Rule)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Kein' is used to negate nouns that appear with an indefinite article (ein/eine) or no article at all (e.g., in plural or with uncountable nouns). It functions like an indefinite article but in its negative form, and therefore must be declined according to gender, number, and case, just like 'ein'.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe kein Geld.

I don't have money.

kein (Nominativ)

kein (Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the nominative case, 'kein' negates the subject of the sentence or a noun following verbs like 'sein' or 'werden'. The forms are 'kein' for masculine and neuter singular nouns, and 'keine' for feminine singular and all plural nouns.

Beispielsatz

Das ist kein Hund.

That is not a dog.

kein (Akkusativ)

kein (Accusative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the accusative case, 'kein' negates the direct object of a verb. Only masculine singular nouns change their form to 'keinen'. Feminine, neuter, and plural forms remain 'keine', 'kein', and 'keine' respectively, mirroring their nominative forms.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe keinen Bruder.

I don't have a brother.

kein (Dativ)

kein (Dative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the dative case, 'kein' negates an indirect object or a noun following a dative preposition. The forms are 'keinem' for masculine and neuter singular, 'keiner' for feminine singular, and 'keinen' for plural nouns. Remember that plural nouns in the dative case often take an additional '-n' ending if they don't already have one (e.g., Kindern, Hunden).

Beispielsatz

Ich gebe keinem Mann das Buch.

I don't give the book to any man.

kein (Pluralformen)

kein (Plural Forms)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For plural nouns, 'kein' always takes the ending '-e' in nominative and accusative, becoming 'keine'. In the dative case, it becomes 'keinen'. It's crucial to remember that the plural noun itself often takes an additional '-n' ending in the dative case if it doesn't already have one (e.g., 'Kinder' becomes 'Kindern').

Beispielsatz

Ich habe keine Geschwister.

I don't have siblings.

kein als Pronomen

kein as a Pronoun

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Kein' can also be used as a pronoun, standing alone without a noun, when the noun is clear from context. In this case, it still follows the same declension rules for gender, number, and case as if the noun were explicitly present. The neuter singular form is often 'keins'.

Beispielsatz

Hast du ein Auto? Nein, ich habe keins.

Do you have a car? No, I don't have one.

nicht ein vs. kein

nicht ein vs. kein

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Kein' is essentially a contracted and more idiomatic form of 'nicht ein' (not an/a). While 'nicht ein' is grammatically correct, 'kein' is almost always preferred and sounds more natural in spoken and written German when negating indefinite nouns. Using 'nicht ein' can sometimes add a specific emphasis.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe kein Buch.

I don't have a book.

schon negieren mit nicht mehr

Negating schon (already) with nicht mehr (no longer)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To negate the adverb 'schon' (already), which indicates something is happening or has happened, you typically use the phrase 'nicht mehr' (no longer). This combination expresses that an action or state has ceased or is not continuing.

Beispielsatz

Er wohnt schon hier. -> Er wohnt nicht mehr hier.

He already lives here. -> He no longer lives here.

noch negieren mit nicht mehr

Negating noch (still) with nicht mehr (no longer)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Similar to 'schon', to negate 'noch' (still), meaning something continues to happen or exist, you use the phrase 'nicht mehr' (no longer). This indicates the cessation of a continuous action or state, or that something is no longer available.

Beispielsatz

Er arbeitet noch. -> Er arbeitet nicht mehr.

He is still working. -> He is no longer working.

viel/viele negieren mit nicht viel/nicht viele

Negating viel/viele (much/many) with nicht viel/nicht viele

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When negating 'viel' (much) or 'viele' (many), you use 'nicht' directly before them. You do not use 'kein' because 'viel/viele' are quantifiers, not indefinite articles. They are often used with nouns that would otherwise not take an article, such as uncountable nouns or plural nouns.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe viel Zeit. -> Ich habe nicht viel Zeit.

I have much time. -> I don't have much time.

Häufige Fehler: nicht vs. kein

Common Errors: nicht vs. kein confusion

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A very common mistake is confusing 'nicht' and 'kein'. Remember the core distinction: 'kein' is for negating nouns with indefinite articles or no article, and it must be declined. 'Nicht' is for everything else: verbs, adjectives, adverbs, proper nouns, definite articles, possessive pronouns, and personal pronouns, and it is invariable.

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Ich habe nicht ein Auto. Richtig: Ich habe kein Auto.

Wrong: I don't have a car. Right: I don't have a car.
