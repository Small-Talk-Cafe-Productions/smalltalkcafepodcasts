Trennbare Verben (Definition)

Separable Verbs (Definition)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Separable verbs consist of a verb stem and a separable prefix. In main clauses, the prefix 'separates' from the verb stem and moves to the very end of the sentence, while the verb stem is conjugated and placed in the second position. This separation is a key characteristic of these verbs.

Beispielsatz

Ich rufe meine Mutter an.

I call my mother.

Trennbare Verben im Hauptsatz (Präsens)

Separable Verbs in Main Clauses (Present Tense)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In a declarative main clause in the present tense, the conjugated verb stem occupies the second position, and the separable prefix moves to the very end of the sentence. This creates a 'bracket' structure around the rest of the sentence elements. The prefix always remains at the end.

Beispielsatz

Der Zug fährt um 10 Uhr ab.

The train departs at 10 o'clock.

Häufige trennbare Präfixe (Teil 1)

Common Separable Prefixes (Part 1)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Many prefixes are always separable. These prefixes often indicate direction, completion, or a specific action. Common examples include 'an-', 'auf-', 'aus-', 'ein-', 'mit-', 'nach-', 'vor-', 'zu-'. Memorizing these helps identify separable verbs.

Beispielsatz

Ich mache die Tür zu.

I close the door.

Häufige trennbare Präfixe (Teil 2)

Common Separable Prefixes (Part 2)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Other common separable prefixes include 'ab-', 'her-', 'hin-', 'weg-', 'zurück-', 'fest-', 'los-', 'hoch-', 'runter-'. These prefixes often add nuances of movement, state, or direction to the base verb. Pay attention to the stress, which usually falls on the prefix.

Beispielsatz

Bitte hol mich vom Bahnhof ab.

Please pick me up from the train station.

Bedeutungswandel durch Präfixe

Change of Meaning through Prefixes

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Separable prefixes often significantly change the meaning of the base verb. For example, 'sehen' means 'to see', but 'fernsehen' means 'to watch TV'. 'Kommen' means 'to come', but 'mitkommen' means 'to come along'. Understanding the prefix's contribution to the meaning is crucial.

Beispielsatz

Ich mache die Tür auf. (öffnen)

I open the door.

Trennbare Verben mit Modalverben

Separable Verbs with Modal Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a separable verb is used with a modal verb (können, müssen, wollen, dürfen, sollen, mögen), the separable verb does not separate. Instead, it remains as a complete infinitive at the very end of the sentence, after the main verb. The modal verb is conjugated in the second position.

Beispielsatz

Ich muss heute Abend einkaufen.

I have to go shopping tonight.

Trennbare Verben im Imperativ

Separable Verbs in the Imperative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In imperative sentences (commands), separable verbs still separate. The conjugated verb stem (in the imperative form) comes first, and the prefix goes to the very end of the sentence. This follows the general rule for main clauses.

Beispielsatz

Ruf mich bitte an!

Please call me!

Trennbare Verben in Ja/Nein-Fragen

Separable Verbs in Yes/No Questions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In yes/no questions, the conjugated verb stem comes first, and the separable prefix goes to the very end of the sentence. This maintains the 'bracket' structure, similar to declarative main clauses, but with the verb at the beginning.

Beispielsatz

Kommst du heute Abend mit?

Are you coming along tonight?

Trennbare Verben in W-Fragen

Separable Verbs in W-Questions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In W-questions (questions starting with 'wer', 'was', 'wann', 'wo', 'wie', etc.), the W-word comes first, followed by the conjugated verb stem in the second position. The separable prefix then moves to the very end of the sentence, completing the bracket.

Beispielsatz

Wann stehst du normalerweise auf?

When do you usually get up?

Trennbare Verben mit 'zu' (Infinitivsatz)

Separable Verbs with 'zu' (Infinitive Clause)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a separable verb is used in an infinitive clause with 'zu' (e.g., after verbs like 'versuchen', 'vergessen', or 'es ist wichtig'), the 'zu' is inserted between the separable prefix and the verb stem. This forms a single word, like 'anzurufen' or 'einzukaufen'.

Beispielsatz

Ich versuche, dich anzurufen.

I try to call you.

Trennbare Verben im Perfekt

Separable Verbs in the Perfect Tense

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the perfect tense, separable verbs form their past participle by inserting '-ge-' between the prefix and the verb stem (e.g., 'anrufen' becomes 'angerufen'). The auxiliary verb (haben or sein) is conjugated in the second position, and the past participle goes to the end.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe meine Mutter angerufen.

I called my mother.

Negation mit trennbaren Verben

Negation with Separable Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To negate a sentence with a separable verb, 'nicht' is typically placed before the separable prefix at the end of the sentence. If there's a direct object, 'nicht' usually comes before the object or the prefix, depending on what is being negated. For general negation, 'nicht' precedes the prefix.

Beispielsatz

Ich rufe meine Mutter nicht an.

I am not calling my mother.

Beispiel: 'anrufen' (rufen + an)

Example: 'anrufen' (to call)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Anrufen' is a very common separable verb meaning 'to call (on the phone)'. The base verb 'rufen' means 'to call/shout'. The prefix 'an-' adds the nuance of initiating contact. Remember to separate 'an' to the end in main clauses and insert 'ge' for Perfekt: 'angerufen'.

Beispielsatz

Ich rufe dich später an.

I'll call you later.

Beispiel: 'aufstehen' (stehen + auf)

Example: 'aufstehen' (to get up)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Aufstehen' means 'to get up' or 'to stand up'. The base verb 'stehen' means 'to stand'. The prefix 'auf-' indicates an upward movement. This verb often uses 'sein' as its auxiliary in the perfect tense because it describes a change of state/movement.

Beispielsatz

Ich stehe jeden Morgen um 6 Uhr auf.

I get up every morning at 6 o'clock.

Beispiel: 'einkaufen' (kaufen + ein)

Example: 'einkaufen' (to shop)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Einkaufen' means 'to shop' or 'to buy groceries'. The base verb 'kaufen' means 'to buy'. The prefix 'ein-' often implies 'in' or 'into', suggesting buying things into a household or for a specific purpose. It uses 'haben' in the perfect tense.

Beispielsatz

Ich kaufe heute im Supermarkt ein.

I'm shopping at the supermarket today.

Kontrast: Untrennbare Verben (Kurzinfo)

Contrast: Inseparable Verbs (Brief Info)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

While many verbs are separable, some verbs have prefixes that never separate. These are called inseparable verbs. Common inseparable prefixes include 'be-', 'emp-', 'ent-', 'er-', 'ge-', 'miss-', 'ver-', 'zer-'. They are always stressed on the verb stem, not the prefix, and do not take 'ge-' in the Perfekt.

Beispielsatz

Ich besuche meine Oma.

I visit my grandma.
