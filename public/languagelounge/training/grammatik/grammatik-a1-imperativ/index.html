Imperativ (Befehlsform)

Imperative (Command Form)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Imperative is used to give commands, instructions, requests, or advice. It exists in four forms, addressing 'du' (informal singular), 'ihr' (informal plural), 'Sie' (formal singular/plural), and 'wir' (let's/suggestion). It's crucial for everyday communication in German.

Beispielsatz

Komm bitte pünktlich!

Please come on time!

Imperativ 'du' (Regelmäßige Verben)

Imperative 'you' (informal singular) - Regular Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To form the 'du' Imperative for regular verbs, take the verb stem (infinitive minus -en/-n). The ending '-e' is optional but often used for better pronunciation or emphasis, especially in spoken German. The personal pronoun 'du' is always omitted.

Beispielsatz

Mach (e) die Hausaufgaben!

Do the homework!

Imperativ 'du' (Verben auf -t, -d, -ig, -el, -er)

Imperative 'you' (informal singular) - Verbs ending in -t, -d, -ig, -el, -er

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For 'du' Imperative, verbs whose stem ends in -t, -d, -ig, -el, or -er must add an '-e' for easier pronunciation. This is not optional like with other regular verbs. The 'du' pronoun is always omitted.

Beispielsatz

Arbeite fleißig!

Work diligently!

Imperativ 'du' (Starke Verben e -> i/ie)

Imperative 'you' (informal singular) - Strong Verbs with e -> i/ie vowel change

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For strong verbs that change 'e' to 'i' or 'ie' in the 'du' form of the present tense, this vowel change also applies in the 'du' Imperative. However, no '-e' ending is added. The 'du' pronoun is omitted.

Beispielsatz

Sprich lauter!

Speak louder!

Imperativ 'du' (Starke Verben a -> ä)

Imperative 'you' (informal singular) - Strong Verbs with a -> ä vowel change

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For strong verbs that change 'a' to 'ä' in the 'du' form of the present tense, this vowel change does NOT apply in the 'du' Imperative. The Imperative uses the original 'a' stem and no '-e' ending is added. The 'du' pronoun is omitted.

Beispielsatz

Fahr vorsichtig!

Drive carefully!

Imperativ 'ihr' (Alle Verben)

Imperative 'you' (informal plural) - All Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The 'ihr' Imperative is the easiest form. It is identical to the 'ihr' form of the verb in the present tense. Simply take the verb stem and add '-t'. The personal pronoun 'ihr' is always omitted. This applies to all verbs, regular and strong.

Beispielsatz

Macht die Tür zu!

Close the door! (to multiple people)

Imperativ 'Sie' (Alle Verben)

Imperative 'you' (formal singular/plural) - All Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The 'Sie' Imperative is formed by placing the infinitive verb first, followed by the pronoun 'Sie'. This form is used for formal commands or requests, addressing one or more people politely. It applies to all verbs, regular and strong.

Beispielsatz

Kommen Sie bitte herein!

Please come in! (formal)

Imperativ 'wir' (Alle Verben)

Imperative 'we' (Let's/Suggestion) - All Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The 'wir' Imperative is used to make suggestions or proposals, equivalent to 'Let's...' in English. It is formed by placing the infinitive verb first, followed by the pronoun 'wir'. This applies to all verbs, regular and strong.

Beispielsatz

Gehen wir ins Kino!

Let's go to the cinema!

Imperativ mit trennbaren Verben

Imperative with Separable Prefix Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When forming the Imperative with separable prefix verbs, the prefix always moves to the end of the sentence, just like in the present tense. The conjugated verb part (stem for 'du', -t for 'ihr', infinitive for 'Sie'/'wir') remains at the beginning.

Beispielsatz

Mach die Tür auf!

Open the door!

Irregulärer Imperativ: sein

Irregular Imperative: to be

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'sein' (to be) is highly irregular in the Imperative. Its forms must be memorized: 'Sei!' for 'du', 'Seid!' for 'ihr', and 'Seien Sie!' for 'Sie'. The 'wir' form is 'Seien wir!'. Note the 'e' in 'Seien' for formal and 'wir' forms.

Beispielsatz

Sei pünktlich!

Be on time! (informal singular)

Irregulärer Imperativ: haben

Irregular Imperative: to have

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'haben' (to have) is also irregular in the 'du' Imperative, dropping the '-e' that would normally be expected for a verb ending in '-b'. Its forms are: 'Hab!' for 'du', 'Habt!' for 'ihr', and 'Haben Sie!' for 'Sie'. The 'wir' form is 'Haben wir!'.

Beispielsatz

Hab keine Angst!

Don't be afraid! (informal singular, lit. Have no fear!)

Irregulärer Imperativ: werden

Irregular Imperative: to become

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'werden' (to become/will) is irregular in the 'du' Imperative, as it drops the 'e' and doesn't add an '-e' ending. Its forms are: 'Werd!' for 'du', 'Werdet!' for 'ihr', and 'Werden Sie!' for 'Sie'. The 'wir' form is 'Werden wir!'.

Beispielsatz

Werd nicht krank!

Don't get sick! (informal singular)

Imperativ mit 'bitte'

Imperative with 'please'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To make commands or requests more polite, the word 'bitte' (please) can be added. It can be placed at the beginning, in the middle, or at the end of the sentence. Its position often depends on emphasis and flow, but it usually comes after the verb or after the subject in 'Sie'/'wir' forms.

Beispielsatz

Gib mir bitte das Buch!

Please give me the book!

Negation des Imperativs

Negation of the Imperative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To negate an Imperative, use 'nicht' (not) or 'kein-' (no/not a) depending on whether you are negating a verb/adjective or a noun. 'Nicht' usually comes at the end or before the element it negates. 'Kein-' declines like an indefinite article.

Beispielsatz

Mach das nicht!

Don't do that!

Imperativ mit Akkusativ- und Dativobjekten

Imperative with Accusative and Dative Objects

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When using pronouns as objects in an Imperative sentence, the direct object (Akkusativ) usually comes before the indirect object (Dativ) if both are pronouns. If only one is a pronoun, it usually comes after the verb. If both are nouns, the Dativ noun often precedes the Akkusativ noun.

Beispielsatz

Gib mir den Stift!

Give me the pen!

Imperativ mit Reflexivverben

Imperative with Reflexive Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For reflexive verbs in the Imperative, the reflexive pronoun (mich, dich, sich, uns, euch, sich) is placed directly after the conjugated verb. Remember that the reflexive pronoun for 'du' is 'dich' and for 'ihr' is 'euch'.

Beispielsatz

Setz dich!

Sit down! (informal singular)
