alegrarse

to be glad / to rejoice

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; takes the preposition 'de' when followed by a cause.

Ejemplo

Me alegro mucho de que hayas aprobado todos tus exámenes este trimestre.

I am very glad that you passed all your exams this term.

asustarse

to get scared

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; often followed by 'de' or 'por' to express the cause of fear.

Ejemplo

El niño se asustó mucho cuando oyó aquel ruido extraño en el desván.

The boy got very scared when he heard that strange noise in the attic.

avergonzarse

to be ashamed / to feel embarrassed

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar stem-changing reflexive verb (o>ue); takes the preposition 'de'.

Ejemplo

Me avergüenzo de haber perdido la paciencia delante de mis compañeros.

I am ashamed of having lost my patience in front of my colleagues.

calmarse

to calm down

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; frequently used as an imperative command.

Ejemplo

Por favor, cálmate y dime exactamente qué ha pasado en la calle.

Please calm down and tell me exactly what happened on the street.

compadecer

to pity / to feel sorry for

📌 Nota gramatical

-er verb with a spelling change in the first-person singular present (zc ->zco).

Ejemplo

Compadezco a las personas que tienen que trabajar al aire libre con este frío.

I feel sorry for people who have to work outdoors in this cold.

desesperarse

to despair / to lose patience

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; expresses extreme frustration or loss of hope.

Ejemplo

No te desesperes si el autobús tarda un poco más de lo habitual.

Don't despair if the bus takes a little longer than usual.

emocionarse

to get emotional / to be thrilled

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; used when touched by deep positive or negative feelings.

Ejemplo

Siempre me emociono mucho cuando veo películas basadas en hechos reales.

I always get very emotional when I watch movies based on true events.

enamorarse

to fall in love

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; normally takes the preposition 'de'.

Ejemplo

Carlos se enamoró de María la primera vez que la vio en la biblioteca.

Carlos fell in love with María the first time he saw her in the library.

enfadarse

to get angry / to get annoyed

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; synonym of 'enojarse' (very common in Latin America).

Ejemplo

No te enfades conmigo, solo estaba intentando darte un buen consejo.

Don't get angry with me, I was only trying to give you good advice.

entusiasmarse

to get excited / to become enthusiastic

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; normally followed by 'con' or 'por'.

Ejemplo

Los niños se entusiasmaron mucho cuando les dijimos que iríamos al parque de atracciones.

The children got very excited when we told them we would go to the amusement park.

estresarse

to get stressed

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; widely used in modern contexts concerning work or studies.

Ejemplo

Es normal estresarse un poco antes de una entrevista de trabajo importante.

It's normal to get a bit stressed before an important job interview.

extrañar

to miss

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular verb; synonym of 'echar de menos' (more common in Spain).

Ejemplo

Extraño mucho a mi familia ahora que vivo y trabajo en el extranjero.

I miss my family a lot now that I live and work abroad.

frustrarse

to get frustrated

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; used when things do not turn out as expected.

Ejemplo

Es normal frustrarse cuando un problema de matemáticas no sale a la primera.

It's normal to get frustrated when a math problem doesn't work out on the first try.

ilusionarse

to look forward to / to get excited about hopes

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; related to positive anticipation and dreams.

Ejemplo

Me ilusiona mucho pensar en el viaje que haremos este próximo verano.

I get very excited thinking about the trip we'll take this coming summer.

lamentar

to regret / to feel sorry about

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular verb; often used to express sorrow or regret over a situation.

Ejemplo

Lamento mucho no haber podido asistir a tu fiesta de cumpleaños.

I am very sorry for not having been able to attend your birthday party.

molestarse

to get annoyed / to bother (oneself)

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; can mean to take the trouble or to get annoyed.

Ejemplo

No te molestes en preparar la cena; ya he comido algo rápido fuera.

Don't bother preparing dinner; I've already eaten something quick outside.

preocuparse

to worry

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; usually followed by the preposition 'por'.

Ejemplo

No te preocupes por el examen de mañana; has estudiado bastante bien.

Don't worry about tomorrow's exam; you have studied quite well.

sorprenderse

to be surprised

📌 Nota gramatical

-er regular reflexive verb; takes the preposition 'de' when followed by a clause.

Ejemplo

Me sorprendí mucho al ver que había aprobado el examen sin apenas estudiar.

I was very surprised to see that I had passed the exam with hardly studying.

temer

to fear

📌 Nota gramatical

-er regular verb; often used to express apprehension or fear about a possibility.

Ejemplo

Temo que llueva esta tarde y tengamos que cancelar la barbacoa.

I fear that it will rain this afternoon and we'll have to cancel the barbecue.

tranquilizarse

to calm down / to relax

📌 Nota gramatical

-ar regular reflexive verb; synonym of 'calmarse'.

Ejemplo

Los médicos le pidieron a la familia que se tranquilizara mientras operaban.

The doctors asked the family to calm down while the operation was taking place.
