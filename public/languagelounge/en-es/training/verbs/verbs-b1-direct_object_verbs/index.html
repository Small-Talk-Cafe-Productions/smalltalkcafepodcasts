comprar

to buy

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) regular verb. Frequently takes a direct object representing the item purchased.

Ejemplo

¿Puedes comprar un coche nuevo cuando ahorres suficiente dinero?

Can you buy a new car when you save enough money?

vender

to sell

📌 Nota gramatical

Second conjugation (-er) regular verb. Often used in commercial contexts with a direct object.

Ejemplo

Mi tío decidió vender su antigua casa de la playa este verano.

My uncle decided to sell his old beach house this summer.

entender

to understand

📌 Nota gramatical

Second conjugation (-er) stem-changing verb (e>ie). Takes a direct object (usually a concept, language, or explanation).

Ejemplo

¿Puedes explicarlo otra vez porque no entiendo las instrucciones?

Can you explain it again because I don't understand the instructions?

conocer

to know (a person or place) / to meet

📌 Nota gramatical

Second conjugation (-er) irregular verb with a -zc- spelling change in the first-person singular present (conozco). Takes a direct object (people or places).

Ejemplo

Conozco un restaurante excelente donde sirven comida tradicional.

I know an excellent restaurant where they serve traditional food.

escribir

to write

📌 Nota gramatical

Third conjugation (-ir) regular verb. Frequently takes a direct object representing the text or message.

Ejemplo

Siempre escribo una lista larga antes de ir al supermercado.

I always write a long list before going to the supermarket.

leer

to read

📌 Nota gramatical

Second conjugation (-er) regular verb with spelling changes in the 3rd person past (leyó, leyeron). Takes a direct object like books or articles.

Ejemplo

Me gusta leer novelas históricas cuando tengo tiempo libre.

I like to read historical novels when I have free time.

organizar

to organize

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) regular verb with a spelling change in the preterit (organicé) to maintain the hard /g/ sound. Takes a direct object.

Ejemplo

Vamos a organizar una fiesta sorpresa para celebrar su cumpleaños.

We are going to organize a surprise party to celebrate her birthday.

terminar

to finish / to complete

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) regular verb. Often takes a direct object such as a task, project, or meal.

Ejemplo

Espero terminar este informe antes de que empiece la reunión.

I hope to finish this report before the meeting starts.

empezar

to start / to begin

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) stem-changing verb (e>ie) with a spelling change in the preterit (empecé). Can take direct objects like projects or films.

Ejemplo

Vamos a empezar un curso nuevo de español la semana próxima.

We are going to start a new Spanish course next week.

olvidar

to forget

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) regular verb. Commonly used with direct objects (e.g., keys, dates) or reflexively (olvidarse de).

Ejemplo

Nunca voy a olvidar el viaje maravilloso que hicimos a Perú.

I am never going to forget the wonderful trip we made to Peru.

encontrar

to find / to meet

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) stem-changing verb (o>ue). Frequently takes a direct object representing lost items or people.

Ejemplo

Por fin pude encontrar mis llaves en el fondo del bolso.

At last I was able to find my keys at the bottom of the bag.

perder

to lose / to miss

📌 Nota gramatical

Second conjugation (-er) stem-changing verb (e>ie). Takes direct objects like objects, opportunities, or trains/flights.

Ejemplo

Debes tener cuidado para no perder tu tarjeta de crédito.

You must be careful not to lose your credit card.

recibir

to receive

📌 Nota gramatical

Third conjugation (-ir) regular verb. Typically takes a direct object such as letters, packages, or news.

Ejemplo

Ayer recibí una carta muy bonita de mis abuelos.

Yesterday I received a very nice letter from my grandparents.

entregar

to deliver / to hand in

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) regular verb with a spelling change in the preterit (entregué). Takes a direct object like documents or packages.

Ejemplo

Tienes que entregar el proyecto antes del viernes por la tarde.

You have to hand in the project before Friday afternoon.

traer

to bring

📌 Nota gramatical

Second conjugation (-er) irregular verb with a -g- in the first-person singular present (traigo) and irregular preterit stem (traj-). Takes a direct object.

Ejemplo

¿Puedes traer algo de beber para la fiesta de esta noche?

Can you bring something to drink for tonight's party?

dejar

to leave / to let / to allow

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) regular verb. Often used with direct objects (leaving items or permission/letting someone do something).

Ejemplo

Olvidé las llaves y tuve que dejar la puerta abierta.

I forgot the keys and had to leave the door open.

invitar

to invite

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) regular verb. Takes a direct object representing the person invited.

Ejemplo

Quiero invitar a todos mis amigos a cenar en mi casa.

I want to invite all my friends to have dinner at my house.

elegir

to choose / to elect

📌 Nota gramatical

Third conjugation (-ir) stem-changing verb (e>i). Takes a direct object representing the chosen option or person.

Ejemplo

Es muy difícil elegir solo un plato de este menú tan variado.

It is very difficult to choose just one dish from this varied menu.

repetir

to repeat

📌 Nota gramatical

Third conjugation (-ir) stem-changing verb (e>i). Takes a direct object representing words, actions, or grades.

Ejemplo

¿Puedes repetir la pregunta porque no la escuché bien?

Can you repeat the question because I didn't hear it well?

crear

to create

📌 Nota gramatical

First conjugation (-ar) regular verb. Typically takes a direct object representing art, companies, or digital content.

Ejemplo

Ella quiere crear su propio negocio de diseño gráfico.

She wants to create her own graphic design business.
