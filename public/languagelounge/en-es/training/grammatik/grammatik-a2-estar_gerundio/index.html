Estar + gerundio (Acción en curso)

Present progressive for actions happening right now

📌 Nota gramatical

The progressive construction in Spanish is formed by combining the auxiliary verb 'estar' (to be) conjugated in the present tense with the gerund (gerundio) of the main action verb. It describes an action happening at the exact moment of speaking. Unlike English, Spanish does not use this form for future arrangements.

Ejemplo

Formación de gerundios regulares (-ando)

Regular gerund formation for -ar verbs

📌 Nota gramatical

To form the gerund of regular -ar verbs, drop the infinitive ending '-ar' and add the suffix '-ando'. This is equivalent to the English '-ing' ending. The resulting form is invariable; it does not change for gender or number.

Ejemplo

Formación de gerundios regulares (-iendo)

Regular gerund formation for -er and -ir verbs

📌 Nota gramatical

To form the gerund of regular -er and -ir verbs, drop the infinitive endings and add the suffix '-iendo'. Both verb groups share this exact same gerund ending in Spanish, simplifying the memorization process for regular verbs.

Ejemplo

Cambio ortográfico: -iendo a -yendo

Spelling change: -iendo to -yendo for vowel-ending stems

📌 Nota gramatical

When the stem of an -er or -ir verb ends in a vowel (like leer, oír, traer, caer), the 'i' of '-iendo' changes to 'y' to prevent three vowels from clashing in pronunciation. Thus, 'leer' becomes 'leyendo'.

Ejemplo

Cambio vocálico en -ir: e > i

Stem-changing -ir verbs: e to i in the gerund

📌 Nota gramatical

Certain -ir verbs that undergo a stem change in the present tense (specifically e > i) maintain this vowel change in their gerund form. Common examples include pedir (pidiendo), decir (diciendo), seguir (siguiendo), servir (sirviendo), and repetir (repitiendo).

Ejemplo

Cambio vocálico en -ir: o > u

Stem-changing -ir verbs: o to u in the gerund

📌 Nota gramatical

The verb 'dormir' (to sleep) and 'morir' (to die) undergo a stem change from 'o' to 'u' in the gerund form. Therefore, 'dormir' becomes 'durmiendo' and 'morir' becomes 'muriendo'.

Ejemplo

Colocación de pronombres reflexivos y de objeto

Pronoun placement with estar + gerund

📌 Nota gramatical

Pronouns (reflexive, direct, or indirect object pronouns) can be placed in two valid positions with the progressive construction: either directly before the conjugated verb 'estar', or attached to the end of the gerund. When attached to the gerund, an accent mark must be added to maintain the original stress.

Ejemplo

Conjugación del auxiliar 'estar' (Presente)

Conjugation of the auxiliary verb 'estar' in present tense

📌 Nota gramatical

To build the present progressive, 'estar' must match the subject in person and number: estoy (I), estás (you singular informal), está (he/she/formal you), estamos (we), estáis (you plural Spain), and están (they/you plural). All forms except the first-person singular carry written accent marks.

Ejemplo

Uso con acciones temporales de corta duración

Using progressive for temporary, ongoing actions

📌 Nota gramatical

The progressive form emphasizes that an action is in progress temporarily. If an action is a general habit or permanent state, the simple present tense is required instead, even if English might sometimes blur the distinction.

Ejemplo

Negación con estar + gerundio

Forming negative sentences with the progressive

📌 Nota gramatical

To make a progressive sentence negative, simply place the negative particle 'no' directly before the conjugated verb 'estar'. Never insert 'no' between the auxiliary 'estar' and the gerund.

Ejemplo

Preguntas con estar + gerundio

Asking questions using the progressive tense

📌 Nota gramatical

To form a question, invert the subject and the auxiliary verb 'estar', or keep the subject at the end, followed by the gerund. Question marks must appear at both the beginning (¿) and the end (?) of the sentence.

Ejemplo

Diferencia: Presente simple vs. Estar + gerundio

Distinction between simple present and present progressive

📌 Nota gramatical

The simple present expresses routines, habits, and permanent truths (e.g., Leo libros = I read books / I am a reader). In contrast, 'estar + gerundio' strictly highlights the physical action unfolding right at this moment (e.g., Estoy leyendo un libro = I am reading a book right now).
