Introduction to Pretérito Indefinido

What is the Pretérito Indefinido (Simple Past) used for?

📌 Nota gramatical

The pretérito indefinido (also called pretérito perfecto simple) is used to express completed actions at a specific moment in the past. These actions are viewed as finished events with a definite beginning and end. ⚠️ Using the indefinido for ongoing past states or habits, confusing it with the imperfect tense (pretérito imperfecto).

Ejemplo

Ayer compré un coche nuevo.

Yesterday I bought a new car.

Regular -AR Verbs: Overview and Endings

What are the personal endings for regular -AR verbs in the past?

📌 Nota gramatical

To conjugate regular -AR verbs in the pretérito indefinido, remove the -ar ending and add: -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -asteis, -aron. Note that the first person singular (-é) and third person singular (-ó) carry written accent marks. ⚠️ Omitting the written accent on -é (yo) or -ó (él/ella/usted), which changes the tense to the present (hablo vs habló).

Ejemplo

Hablé con el director ayer.

I spoke with the director yesterday.

Regular -ER and -IR Verbs: Shared Endings

What are the personal endings for regular -ER and -IR verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

Regular -ER and -IR verbs share the exact same set of endings in the pretérito indefinido: -í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -isteis, -ieron. The first and third person singular also require written accents (-í, -ió). ⚠️ Mixing up -ER/-IR endings with -AR endings, or forgetting that -imos is identical for both -ir verbs in present and past (e.g., vivimos).

Ejemplo

Comí una manzana esta mañana.

I ate an apple this morning.

First Person Singular: -AR Verbs (-é)

How do you conjugate the 'yo' form for regular -AR verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

The 'yo' form ends in -é with an acute accent. For example, 'bailar' becomes 'bailé'. The accent is crucial to distinguish it from the present tense 'bailo' (depending on the person) or 'baile'. ⚠️ Writing 'yo hable' instead of 'hablé', accidentally using the present subjunctive form instead of the past indicative.

Ejemplo

Ayer bailé toda la noche.

Yesterday I danced all night.

Second Person Singular: -AR Verbs (-aste)

How do you conjugate the 'tú' form for regular -AR verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

The 'tú' form adds '-aste' to the verb stem. There is no written accent on this ending. It applies to all regular -AR verbs without exception. ⚠️ Adding an accent to the final 'e' (-asté), which is incorrect in standard Spanish orthography.

Ejemplo

Compraste un regalo muy caro.

You bought a very expensive gift.

Third Person Singular: -AR Verbs (-ó)

How do you conjugate él, ella, usted for regular -AR verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

The third-person singular takes the ending '-ó' with a strong accent. This clearly distinguishes 'él habló' (he spoke) from 'él habla' (he speaks). ⚠️ Dropping the accent mark on -ó, leading to ambiguity between present and past tense in writing.

Ejemplo

El profesor explicó la lección.

The teacher explained the lesson.

First and Second Person Plural: -AR Verbs (-amos, -asteis)

How do you form nosotros and vosotros for regular -AR verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

The 'nosotros' form (-amos) is identical to the present tense form for -AR verbs. Context (time markers like 'ayer') tells you it is past. The 'vosotros' form is '-asteis'. ⚠️ Assuming 'nosotros cantamos' is always present tense; always look for time indicators like 'ayer' or 'el año pasado'.

Ejemplo

Cantamos una canción en la fiesta.

We sang a song at the party.

Third Person Plural: -AR Verbs (-aron)

How do you conjugate ellos, ellas, ustedes for regular -AR verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

The third-person plural ending is '-aron'. There is no accent mark on this ending. Note the final 'n' indicating plural. ⚠️ Pronouncing or spelling the ending as '-aron' with an accent, or writing '-aran' (which belongs to the imperfect subjunctive).

Ejemplo

Mis amigos llegaron muy tarde.

My friends arrived very late.

First Person Singular: -ER and -IR Verbs (-í)

How do you conjugate the 'yo' form for regular -ER and -IR verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

The 'yo' form for both -ER and -IR verbs is '-í' with an acute accent. For example, 'beber' becomes 'bebí' and 'escribir' becomes 'escribí'. ⚠️ Forgetting the accent mark on -í, which can cause confusion with other grammatical forms.

Ejemplo

Ayer bebí dos litros de agua.

Yesterday I drank two liters of water.

Third Person Singular: -ER and -IR Verbs (-ió)

How do you conjugate él, ella, usted for regular -ER/-IR verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

The third-person singular for regular -ER and -IR verbs is '-ió' with a written accent on the 'ó'. This applies uniformly to all regular verbs of these two conjugations. ⚠️ Writing '-io' without the accent mark, which violates standard spelling rules for this verb ending.

Ejemplo

El tren salió a las tres.

The train left at three o'clock.

Plural Forms: -ER and -IR Verbs (-imos, -isteis, -ieron)

What are the plural endings for regular -ER and -IR verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

The plural endings are '-imos' (nosotros), '-isteis' (vosotros), and '-ieron' (ellos/ellas/ustedes). Note that '-imos' is identical to the present tense 'nosotros' form for -IR verbs. ⚠️ Writing '-ieron' as '-ieron' with an accent, or mixing up the 'i' and 'e' in the third-person plural ending.

Ejemplo

Aprendimos mucho en la clase.

We learned a lot in the class.

Time Markers for Pretérito Indefinido

What common time expressions trigger the pretérito indefinido?

📌 Nota gramatical

Words and phrases that indicate a specific, completed point in time trigger the indefinido. Common examples include: 'ayer' (yesterday), 'anoche' (last night), 'el año pasado' (last year), 'el lunes pasado' (last Monday), and 'anteayer' (day before yesterday). ⚠️ Using time markers of completed points in the past with the present tense or imperfect tense.

Ejemplo

El lunes pasado fui al médico.

Last Monday I went to the doctor.

Word order and sentence structure rules for Pretérito indefinido: Regular Verbs (A2): In main clauses, subordinate clauses, and with negation or adverbial phrases, ensure Introduction to Pretérito Indefinido, Regular -AR Verbs: Overview and Endings, First Person Singular: -AR Verbs, Second Person Singular: -AR Verbs occupy their strict grammatical position. ⚠️ Misplacing elements relative to the conjugated verb, negation particle, or clause boundary.

Register and usage nuance (A2): While everyday spoken communication frequently uses conversational shortcuts, formal written texts and CEFR A2 exams require precise adherence to Pretérito indefinido: Regular Verbs. ⚠️ Using overly colloquial shortcuts in formal written contexts or stiff bookish phrasing where natural flow is required.

Ejemplo

Decision framework for Pretérito indefinido: Regular Verbs (A2): Before producing a sentence, verify 1) grammatical agreement (gender, number, person), 2) tense/mood triggers, and 3) exact word order. ⚠️ Producing clauses without checking case, mood triggers, or subject-verb agreement.
