Concessive Clauses (aunque, a pesar de que)

Aunque + Indicativo (Hecho conocido o seguro)

Although / Even though + Indicative (Known or certain fact)

📌 Nota gramatical

Use 'aunque' followed by the indicative mood when the speaker presents the concessive fact as something true, real, or already known to the listener. It often translates to 'even though' or 'although' in English.

Ejemplo

Aunque + Subjuntivo (Hecho hipotético, improbable o futuro)

Although / Even if + Subjunctive (Hypothetical, unlikely, or future fact)

📌 Nota gramatical

Use 'aunque' followed by the subjunctive mood when the obstacle or concessive condition is hypothetical, uncertain, unfulfilled, or refers to the future. It often translates to 'even if' in English.

Ejemplo

A pesar de que + Indicativo (Hecho real pasado o presente)

Despite the fact that / In spite of the fact that + Indicative

📌 Nota gramatical

'A pesar de que' is followed by the indicative when introducing a real, verified obstacle. It is slightly more formal than 'aunque' and emphasizes the difficulty caused by the circumstance.

Ejemplo

A pesar de + Infinitivo (Mismo sujeto)

Despite / In spite of + Infinitive (Same subject)

📌 Nota gramatical

When the subject of the main clause and the concessive clause is the same, 'a pesar de' is followed directly by an infinitive verb (or a noun phrase) instead of 'a pesar de que' + subjunctive/indicative.

Ejemplo

A pesar de + Sustantivo

Despite / In spite of + Noun

📌 Nota gramatical

'A pesar de' can also be followed directly by a noun phrase to express a contrast or obstacle without a verb. This structure is extremely common in both written and spoken B2 Spanish.

Ejemplo

Aun cuando + Indicativo / Subjuntivo

Even when / Even if + Indicative / Subjunctive

📌 Nota gramatical

'Aun cuando' functions very similarly to 'aunque', but adds a stronger nuance of emphasis ('even when' or 'even if'). It takes the indicative for facts and the subjunctive for hypothetical scenarios.

Ejemplo

Por mucho / más + que + Subjuntivo

No matter how much / However much + Subjunctive

📌 Nota gramatical

This structure expresses extreme concession. It translates to 'no matter how much' or 'however much'. The verb that follows 'que' must be in the subjunctive if it refers to a future or hypothetical action.

Ejemplo

Por muy + adjetivo/adverbio + que + Subjuntivo

No matter how + adjective/adverb + Subjunctive

📌 Nota gramatical

Used to emphasize that an extreme quality or state is still not enough to prevent the main action. It translates to 'no matter how [adjective/adverb]' or 'however [adjective/adverb]'.

Ejemplo

Por más que / Por mucho que + Sustantivo + que + Subjuntivo

No matter how much / many + Noun + Subjunctive

📌 Nota gramatical

When combining 'por mucho' or 'por más' with a noun, the noun must be placed immediately after 'mucho/más', followed by 'que' and a subjunctive verb.

Ejemplo

Y eso que + Indicativo (Aclaración concesiva coloquial)

And that too / Even though / Despite the fact that + Indicative

📌 Nota gramatical

'Y eso que' is a very common colloquial concessive connector used to add an unexpected, contrastive detail after a statement has already been made. It is always followed by the indicative.

Ejemplo

Con lo + adjetivo/adverbio + que + Indicativo / Subjuntivo

Considering how + adjective/adverb + that + Indicative / Subjunctive

📌 Nota gramatical

This structure expresses a concessive nuance based on a known characteristic or state ('considering how...'). It often takes the indicative for general truths or the subjunctive for unreal/hypothetical states.

Ejemplo

A estas alturas, + Concesión con de todas formas / aun así

At this point, + Concession with anyway / even so

📌 Nota gramatical

At B2 level, students should combine subordinate concessive clauses with connecting adverbs like 'aun así' (even so), 'de todas formas' (anyway), or 'no obstante' (nevertheless) to link complex sentences smoothly.
