Time Expressions: desde, hace, durante

Desde (Starting Point of an Action)

Using 'desde' to indicate when an ongoing action started (equivalent to 'since').

📌 Nota gramatical

'Desde' is used with the present tense in Spanish to describe an action that started in the past and continues into the present. It translates to 'since' when followed by a specific point in time (a year, a month, a day, or a specific hour).

Ejemplo

Desde hace (Duration from Past to Present)

Using 'desde hace' to express how long an ongoing action has lasted (equivalent to 'for').

📌 Nota gramatical

When you want to specify a duration of time (e.g., three years, two weeks) for an action that is still continuing, combine 'desde' with 'hace'. This structure answers the question '¿Cuánto tiempo...?' and translates to 'for' in English.

Ejemplo

Desde que (Since + Clause)

Using 'desde que' followed by a full sentence or event to indicate when something started.

📌 Nota gramatical

'Desde que' means 'since' when it introduces a dependent clause containing a conjugated verb rather than just a time phrase. The verb in the dependent clause is usually in the preterite or present tense depending on context.

Ejemplo

Hace + Time (Ago in the Past)

Using 'hace' followed by a time period to mean 'ago'.

📌 Nota gramatical

To express how long ago a completed action took place, use 'hace' + time expression + preterite tense verb. It places an event at a specific distance in the past relative to the present moment.

Ejemplo

Hace... que... (Duration for Completed or Ongoing Actions)

Using the 'Hace + time + que + verb' structure to express duration.

📌 Nota gramatical

Another common way to express duration is placing the time expression between 'hace' and 'que', followed by a present-tense verb. This is completely interchangeable with the 'verb + desde hace' structure.

Ejemplo

Durante (During a Time Period)

Using 'durante' to state the duration of an action or event (equivalent to 'during' or 'for').

📌 Nota gramatical

'Durante' is used to answer the question '¿Cuánto tiempo?' when looking at an action in its entirety, often with past or future tenses. It shows how long a completed action lasted, similar to 'for' in English with completed actions.

Ejemplo

Durante vs. Por (Time Duration Contrast)

Understanding when to use 'durante' versus 'por' for time duration.

📌 Nota gramatical

Both 'durante' and 'por' can mean 'for' when expressing duration. 'Durante' emphasizes the entire duration of an event, while 'por' is often interchangeable with 'durante' in simple time spans but emphasizes exchange, cause, or vague passing of time in other contexts. At A2 level, use 'durante' for specific time blocks.

Ejemplo

Desde... hasta... (From... Until...)

Using the correlative pair 'desde... hasta...' to mark precise limits in time.

📌 Nota gramatical

To define both the beginning and the end of a scheduled event or routine, use 'desde' (from/since) paired with 'hasta' (until/to). This works for hours, days, months, and years.

Ejemplo

Asking About Time: ¿Desde cuándo?

Asking 'Since when?' in Spanish.

📌 Nota gramatical

To inquire about the starting point of an ongoing action, use the interrogative phrase '¿Desde cuándo?'. The answer will naturally employ 'desde' or 'desde hace'.

Ejemplo

Asking About Duration: ¿Cuánto tiempo...?

Asking 'How long...?' in Spanish.

📌 Nota gramatical

To ask about the total duration of an action or event, use '¿Cuánto tiempo...?' followed by the verb. It can be paired with present, past, or future tenses.

Ejemplo

En (Time to Complete an Action)

Using 'en' to express the amount of time it takes to complete an action.

📌 Nota gramatical

While 'durante' shows how long an action takes up, 'en' indicates the time limit or span within which an action is successfully completed (equivalent to 'in' in English, as in 'I read the book in two days').

Ejemplo

Por (Approximate Time or Period of Day)

Using 'por' to indicate vague time periods or parts of the day.

📌 Nota gramatical

'Por' is used for general periods of the day ('por la mañana', 'por la tarde', 'por la noche') and for approximate times or seasons ('por Navidad', 'por el año pasado'). It does not express precise duration.
