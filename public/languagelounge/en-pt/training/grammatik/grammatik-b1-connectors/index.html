No entanto / Todavia

However / Nevertheless

📌 Nota gramatical

Used to introduce a contrast or limitation, equivalent to 'however' or 'nevertheless'. They can often begin a sentence followed by a comma or appear after a semicolon. 'No entanto' is extremely common in both written and spoken European Portuguese. ⚠️ Learners often use 'mas' (but) everywhere to express contrast, failing to use formal discourse markers like 'no entanto' to elevate sentence structure at the B1 level.

Exemplo

Estudámos muito para o teste; no entanto, o resultado não foi o esperado.

We studied hard for the test; however, the result was not what we expected.

Por conseguinte / Portanto

Therefore / Consequently

📌 Nota gramatical

Used to express a logical consequence or result. 'Portanto' is more conversational and frequent in daily speech, while 'por conseguinte' is slightly more formal and written. ⚠️ Confusing 'por isso' (that's why / therefore) with 'por conseguinte', although both express cause and effect. 'Portanto' and 'por isso' are much more common in spoken European Portuguese.

Exemplo

Perdeste o passaporte e, portanto, não podes viajar para fora da Europa.

You lost your passport and, therefore, you cannot travel outside Europe.

Além disso

Furthermore / In addition / Besides

📌 Nota gramatical

Used to add information, arguments, or points to a previous statement. It corresponds to 'furthermore', 'moreover', or 'besides' in English. It is typically followed by a comma when starting a clause. ⚠️ Directly translating English 'also' or 'plus' into inappropriate structures instead of using the idiomatic phrase 'além disso'.

Exemplo

O novo telemóvel tem uma câmara excelente; além disso, a bateria dura dois dias.

The new mobile phone has an excellent camera; furthermore, the battery lasts two days.

Por um lado... por outro (lado)

On the one hand... on the other hand

📌 Nota gramatical

Used to present two opposing viewpoints or aspects of a situation. The structure balances arguments, where 'por um lado' introduces the first perspective and 'por outro' introduces the counter-perspective. ⚠️ Forgetting to include the second half ('por outro lado') when presenting a balanced argument, leaving the thought incomplete.

Exemplo

Por um lado, morar no centro é prático; por outro lado, é muito barulhento.

On the one hand, living downtown is practical; on the other hand, it is very noisy.

Embora + Subjuntivo

Although / Even though

📌 Nota gramatical

A concessive conjunction used to express contrast or an obstacle that does not prevent an action. In European Portuguese, 'embora' almost always triggers the Present Subjunctive (Presente do Conjuntivo) when referring to present or future situations. ⚠️ Using the Indicative mood after 'embora' (e.g., embora está) instead of the mandatory Subjunctive (embora esteja).

Exemplo

Embora esteja a chover, vamos dar uma volta pela vila.

Although it is raining, we are going for a walk around the town.

Enquanto

While / As long as

📌 Nota gramatical

Used to indicate simultaneity between two actions happening at the same time. It can also mean 'whereas' when showing contrast. When denoting simultaneous ongoing actions, European Portuguese often uses the continuous structure (estar a + infinitive) alongside it. ⚠️ Confusing 'enquanto' (while) with 'quando' (when). 'Enquanto' emphasizes duration and simultaneous action.

Exemplo

Tu limpas a casa enquanto eu vou ao supermercado comprar peixe fresco.

You clean the house while I go to the supermarket to buy fresh fish.

Visto que / Dado que

Since / Given that / Seeing that

📌 Nota gramatical

Causal connectors used to explain the reason or justification for something, slightly more formal than 'porque' (because). They are interchangeable in most B1 contexts when introducing a known fact. ⚠️ Using the subjunctive mood after these connectors. 'Visto que' and 'dado que' state a factual reason and require the indicative mood.

Exemplo

Visto que perdestes o autocarro, tendes de esperar pelo próximo.

Since you missed the bus, you have to wait for the next one.

Para que + Subjuntivo

So that / In order that

📌 Nota gramatical

Expresses purpose or intention. When the subject of the main clause is different from the subject of the subordinate clause, 'para que' requires the Subjunctive mood (Conjuntivo). ⚠️ Using the infinitive when subjects change. If subjects are the same, use just 'para + infinitive' (para entender). When subjects change, 'para que + subjunctive' is required.

Exemplo

Estudo português todos os dias para que os meus colegas me entendam melhor.

I study Portuguese every day so that my colleagues understand me better.

Apesar de + Infinitivo

Despite / In spite of

📌 Nota gramatical

Expresses a contrast or concession, similar to 'embora', but followed by an infinitive verb when the subject is the same, or 'apesar de que' + indicative/subjunctive for changing subjects. ⚠️ Using a conjugated verb directly after 'apesar de' without adding 'que' (apesar de é is incorrect; it must be apesar de ser or apesar de que é).

Exemplo

Apesar de estar cansado, ele continuou a trabalhar no projeto até tarde.

Despite being tired, he kept working on the project until late.

Portanto / Logo / Por isso

Therefore / Consequently / That's why

📌 Nota gramatical

Sequential and consecutive markers. 'Por isso' is extremely common in spoken European Portuguese to bridge a cause with its natural consequence in casual conversation. ⚠️ Confusing 'por isso' with 'para isso'. 'Por isso' means 'that's why/therefore' (result), whereas 'para isso' means 'for that purpose' (goal).

Exemplo

Esqueci-me do guarda-chuva em casa e, por isso, fiquei completamente encharcado.

I forgot my umbrella at home and, therefore, I got completely soaked.

Em vez de / Em lugar de

Instead of

📌 Nota gramatical

Used to indicate an alternative or replacement, meaning 'instead of'. It is typically followed by an infinitive verb when the subject remains the same. ⚠️ Forgetting the preposition 'de' (em vez ficar instead of the correct em vez de ficar).

Exemplo

Em vez de ficares a ver televisão, deves arrumar o teu quarto.

Instead of staying home watching television, you should tidy up your room.

Por outro lado

On the other hand

📌 Nota gramatical

Specifically used to introduce a contrasting perspective, argument, or alternative viewpoint. Often paired with 'por um lado' to structure an essay or a spoken debate. ⚠️ Translating literally as 'no outro lado' (which literally means 'on the other side' of a physical street or object) rather than using 'por outro lado' for discourse contrast.

Exemplo

A vida no campo é muito sossegada; por outro lado, há menos opções de lazer.

Life in the countryside is very quiet; on the other hand, there are fewer leisure options.

Word order and sentence structure: Connectors & Discourse Markers

📌 Nota gramatical

Word order and sentence structure rules for Connectors & Discourse Markers (B1): In main clauses, subordinate clauses, and with negation or adverbial phrases, ensure No entanto / Todavia, Por conseguinte / Portanto, Além disso, Por um lado... por outro occupy their strict grammatical position. ⚠️ Misplacing elements relative to the conjugated verb, negation particle, or clause boundary.

Exemplo

Perdeste o passaporte e, portanto, não podes viajar para fora da Europa.

You lost your passport and, therefore, you cannot travel outside Europe.

Formal register vs. colloquial spoken usage: Connectors & Discourse Markers

📌 Nota gramatical

Register and usage nuance (B1): While everyday spoken communication frequently uses conversational shortcuts, formal written texts and CEFR B1 exams require precise adherence to Connectors & Discourse Markers. ⚠️ Using overly colloquial shortcuts in formal written contexts or stiff bookish phrasing where natural flow is required.

Exemplo

O novo telemóvel tem uma câmara excelente; além disso, a bateria dura dois dias.

The new mobile phone has an excellent camera; furthermore, the battery lasts two days.

Decision framework and practical summary: Connectors & Discourse Markers

📌 Nota gramatical

Decision framework for Connectors & Discourse Markers (B1): Before producing a sentence, verify 1) grammatical agreement (gender, number, person), 2) tense/mood triggers, and 3) exact word order. ⚠️ Producing clauses without checking case, mood triggers, or subject-verb agreement.

Exemplo

Por um lado, morar no centro é prático; por outro lado, é muito barulhento.

On the one hand, living downtown is practical; on the other hand, it is very noisy.
