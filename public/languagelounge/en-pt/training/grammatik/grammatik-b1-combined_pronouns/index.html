Contração de 'me' + 'o/a/os/as' → mo, ma, mos, mas

Contraction of 1st person singular indirect object (me) with direct object pronouns

📌 Nota gramatical

In European Portuguese, when an indirect object clitic pronoun ('me') and a 3rd-person direct object clitic ('o, a, os, as') occur together with the same verb, they contract into a single combined form: 'me + o = mo', 'me + a = ma', 'me + os = mos', 'me + as = mas'. They agree in gender and number with the thing given or shown. ⚠️ Keeping the two pronouns separate as '*deu-me o' instead of contracting them into 'deu-mo'.

Exemplo

Este relógio antigo pertencia ao meu avô; ele deu-mo no meu aniversário.

This old watch belonged to my grandfather; he gave it to me on my birthday.

Contração de 'te' + 'o/a/os/as' → to, ta, tos, tas

Contraction of 2nd person singular indirect object (te) with direct object pronouns

📌 Nota gramatical

When the informal indirect object pronoun 'te' (to you) combines with direct object pronouns 'o, a, os, as', the 'e' drops and produces 'to, ta, tos, tas'. Like all clitics in European Portuguese, it attaches to the verb with a hyphen in affirmative main clauses (enclisis). ⚠️ Replacing the combined pronoun with 'a ti' alone and omitting the direct object reference completely.

Exemplo

Tenho aqui a chave da garagem; empresto-ta até segunda-feira.

I have the garage key here; I'll lend it to you until Monday.

Contração de 'lhe/lhes' + 'o/a/os/as' → lho, lha, lhos, lhas

Contraction of 3rd person indirect object (lhe/lhes) with direct object pronouns

📌 Nota gramatical

Both singular 'lhe' (to him/her/you-formal) and plural 'lhes' (to them/you-plural) contract identically with 'o, a, os, as' to form 'lho, lha, lhos, lhas'. Therefore, 'lho' can mean 'it to him', 'it to her', 'it to you (formal)', or 'it to them', clarified by context. ⚠️ Adding an extra plural '-s' to the indirect part (e.g., '*lhes-o') instead of using 'lho/lha/lhos/lhas' for both singular and plural recipients.

Exemplo

O cliente pediu a fatura e a funcionária entregou-lha imediatamente.

The customer asked for the invoice and the employee handed it to him immediately.

Contração de 'nos' + 'o/a/os/as' → no-lo, no-la, no-los, no-las

Contraction of 1st person plural indirect object (nos) with direct object pronouns

📌 Nota gramatical

When 'nos' combines with 'o, a, os, as', the final '-s' of 'nos' assimilates into '-l' on the direct object pronoun, separated by a hyphen: 'no-lo, no-la, no-los, no-las'. In enclisis, the verb links with a first hyphen: 'explicou-no-lo'. ⚠️ Writing '*nos-o' without changing the 's' to 'l' ('no-lo').

Exemplo

A regra parecia complicada, mas a professora explicou-no-la com muita clareza.

The rule seemed complicated, but the teacher explained it to us very clearly.

Contração de 'vos' + 'o/a/os/as' → vo-lo, vo-la, vo-los, vo-las

Contraction of 2nd person plural indirect object (vos) with direct object pronouns

📌 Nota gramatical

Following the same assimilation rule as 'nos', the 2nd person plural indirect object 'vos' combines with 'o, a, os, as' to form 'vo-lo, vo-la, vo-los, vo-las'. In European Portuguese, 'vos' is actively used as the object pronoun corresponding to 'vocês' as well as 'vós'. ⚠️ Forgetting that in verbs ending in '-mos', the final '-s' of the verb also drops before 'vo-lo' ('enviamo-vo-las').

Exemplo

Tenho um segredo importante e digo-vo-lo se prometerem guardar sigilo.

I have an important secret and I'll tell it to you all if you promise to keep it confidential.

Posição enclítica (depois do verbo) em frases afirmativas

Enclitic position (after the verb) without phonetic changes to the verb stem

📌 Nota gramatical

Unlike simple direct object pronouns ('o/a'), which change to '-lo/-la' after '-r/-s/-z' or '-no/-na' after nasal sounds, combined pronouns ('mo, to, lho') already start with a consonant ('m, t, lh'). Therefore, the verb form NEVER drops its final '-r', '-s', or '-z' before 'mo, to, lho' (e.g., 'Vou dar-to', NOT '*Vou dá-to'). ⚠️ Dropping the final '-r' of the infinitive before 'mo/to/lho' (writing '*entregá-lho' instead of the correct 'entregar-lho').

Exemplo

Se encontrares o meu guarda-chuva azul, podes trazer-mo amanhã?

If you find my blue umbrella, can you bring it to me tomorrow?

Posição proclítica (antes do verbo) com palavras atrativas

Proclitic position (before the verb) triggered by attractors

📌 Nota gramatical

Whenever the clause contains a proclisis trigger—such as negation ('não, nunca, ninguém'), subordinate conjunctions ('que, porque, se, quando'), interrogatives ('quem, onde'), or certain adverbs ('já, ainda, sempre')—the combined pronoun moves before the verb without a hyphen (except inside 'no-lo' / 'vo-lo'). ⚠️ Leaving the combined pronoun attached after the verb in a negative sentence ('*Não emprestei-lha' instead of 'Não lha emprestei').

Exemplo

Ele pediu-me a bicicleta emprestada, mas eu não lha pude emprestar.

He asked to borrow my bicycle, but I couldn't lend it to him.

Mesóclise com pronomes combinados no Futuro e Condicional

Mesoclisis with combined pronouns in the Future and Conditional tenses

📌 Nota gramatical

In affirmative main clauses without proclisis triggers, Future and Conditional verbs in European Portuguese place the combined pronoun inside the verb between the infinitive stem and the ending (mesoclisis): 'dar-mo-á', 'dir-to-ia', 'entregar-lho-emos'. Again, the '-r' of the stem stays intact before 'mo/to/lho' (except 'dizer/fazer/trazer' stems 'dir-/far-/trar-'). ⚠️ Using proclisis at the beginning of a sentence with a Future or Conditional verb ('*Lho enviarei') instead of mesoclisis ('Enviar-lho-ei').

Exemplo

Assim que o contrato estiver assinado, enviar-lho-emos por correio registado.

As soon as the contract is signed, we will send it to him by registered mail.

Tempos compostos com o auxiliar 'ter'

Placement of combined pronouns in compound tenses with auxiliary 'ter'

📌 Nota gramatical

In compound tenses formed with 'ter + particípio passado' (e.g., Pretérito Mais-que-Perfeito Composto or Condicional Composto), the combined pronoun always attaches to the auxiliary verb 'ter' (either enclitically with a hyphen or proclitically before it), never to the past participle. ⚠️ Attaching the combined pronoun to the past participle ('*tinha dito-mo' instead of 'tinha-mo dito').

Exemplo

A Maria tinha-mo dito na semana passada, mas eu esqueci-me completamente.

Maria had told it to me last week, but I completely forgot.

Alternativa analítica no português falado ('a mim', 'a ti', 'a ele/ela')

Analytical alternative in spoken European Portuguese using prepositional phrases

📌 Nota gramatical

While 'mo, to, lho' are common and natural in European Portuguese, speakers also frequently use the direct object clitic ('o, a, os, as') attached to the verb combined with an emphatic prepositional phrase ('a mim, a ti, a ele/ela, a si, a nós, a vocês')—especially to clarify ambiguity in 'lho/lha'. ⚠️ Omitting the direct object clitic completely when using 'a ele/a ela' in standard registers.

Exemplo

Como a carta era urgente, entreguei-a a ela pessoalmente.

Since the letter was urgent, I handed it to her in person.

Word order and sentence structure rules for Combined Pronouns (mo, to, lho) (B1): In main clauses, subordinate clauses, and with negation or adverbial phrases, ensure Tempos compostos com o auxiliar 'ter' occupy their strict grammatical position. ⚠️ Misplacing elements relative to the conjugated verb, negation particle, or clause boundary.

Exemplo

Register and usage nuance (B1): While everyday spoken communication frequently uses conversational shortcuts, formal written texts and CEFR B1 exams require precise adherence to Combined Pronouns (mo, to, lho). ⚠️ Using overly colloquial shortcuts in formal written contexts or stiff bookish phrasing where natural flow is required.

Exemplo
