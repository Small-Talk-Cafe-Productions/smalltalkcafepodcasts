Introduction to the Personal Infinitive

What is the personal infinitive in European Portuguese?

📌 Nota gramatical

Unlike most languages, European Portuguese has a conjugated infinitive. It inflects for person and number, allowing us to specify who performs the action of the infinitive verb. It is formed by adding personal endings (-es, -mos, -des, -em) to the basic infinitive stem, though the 1st and 3rd person singular remain identical to the root form. ⚠️ Using the impersonal infinitive when the subject changes, or using a conjugated subjunctive form instead of the personal infinitive after prepositions.

Exemplo

É importante estudares para o teste de amanhã.

It is important for you to study for tomorrow's test.

Regular Endings of the Personal Infinitive

How do you conjugate regular verbs in the personal infinitive?

📌 Nota gramatical

The paradigm uses the root infinitive for the 1st (eu) and 3rd (ele/ela/você) persons singular. The remaining persons add specific endings: -es (tu), -mos (nós), -des (vós), and -em (eles/elas/vocês). This applies to all regular verbs regardless of whether they end in -ar, -er, or -ir. ⚠️ Forgetting to add the -es ending for the 'tu' form, saying 'antes de tu sair' instead of 'antes de tu saíres'.

Exemplo

Ao entrares em casa, tira os sapatos por favor.

Upon entering the house, take off your shoes please.

Use after prepositions with a different subject

When must the personal infinitive be used with prepositions?

📌 Nota gramatical

When a preposition (such as 'para', 'por', 'antes de', 'depois de') introduces an infinitive clause whose subject is different from the main clause's subject, the personal infinitive is obligatory to clarify who is performing the action. ⚠️ Using the impersonal infinitive after 'para' even when the subjects of the main and subordinate clauses are different.

Exemplo

Liguei-te para saberes a hora da reunião.

I called you so that you would know the meeting time.

Subject clarification in subordinate clauses

How does the personal infinitive clarify who is doing the action?

📌 Nota gramatical

In complex sentences, if the infinitive clause has its own distinct subject, the personal infinitive makes this explicit without needing a full 'que' (that) clause with the subjunctive. This creates a concise and elegant sentence structure typical of European Portuguese. ⚠️ Translating word-for-word from English using indicative or subjunctive clauses where Portuguese naturally prefers the personal infinitive.

Exemplo

É fundamental chegarmos a horas à entrevista.

It is fundamental that we arrive on time for the interview.

Contrast: Identical vs. Different Subjects

When do we NOT conjugate the infinitive?

📌 Nota gramatical

If the subject of the main clause and the subject of the infinitive clause are exactly the same, you must use the impersonal (unconjugated) infinitive. Conjugating it when subjects are identical sounds unnatural and grammatically incorrect. ⚠️ Conjugating the infinitive even when the subject is identical to the main verb (e.g., quero viajarmos instead of quero viajar).

Exemplo

Quero viajar para Lisboa nas minhas férias.

I want to travel to Lisbon on my vacation.

Personal Infinitive with 'ao' (upon / when)

How is the personal infinitive used with the preposition 'ao'?

📌 Nota gramatical

The combination 'ao' + personal infinitive expresses simultaneity ('upon doing' or 'when doing'). When the action is done by someone other than the main clause subject, or to emphasize the specific actor, the personal infinitive takes its respective endings. ⚠️ Omitting the contraction 'ao' (using 'a o') or failing to conjugate the infinitive when multiple people perform the action.

Exemplo

Ao entrarem na sala de banho, acendam a luz.

Upon entering the bathroom, turn on the light.

Personal Infinitive with 'para' (in order to / for)

How does 'para' trigger the personal infinitive?

📌 Nota gramatical

The preposition 'para' frequently introduces purpose clauses. When the person doing the action of the main clause differs from the person doing the action of the subordinate clause, the personal infinitive is mandatory. ⚠️ Using a subjunctive clause with 'para que' when a personal infinitive construction would be much more idiomatic and concise.

Exemplo

Trabalho muito para os meus filhos terem uma boa vida.

I work hard so that my children can have a good life.

Personal Infinitive with 'por' (because of / for)

How is the personal infinitive used with 'por' for causation?

📌 Nota gramatical

When 'por' expresses cause or reason ('because of...'), the personal infinitive follows it to indicate who performed the action that caused the situation in the main clause. ⚠️ Using the impersonal infinitive after 'por' when attributing the action to a specific plural or singular subject.

Exemplo

Fomos penalizados por chegarmos atrasados ao trabalho.

We were penalized for arriving late to work.

Personal Infinitive with Temporal Prepositions

How do temporal expressions use the personal infinitive?

📌 Nota gramatical

Prepositions and phrases like 'antes de' (before), 'depois de' (after), and 'até' (until) frequently govern the personal infinitive to mark sequential actions performed by different subjects. ⚠️ Dropping the prepositional phrases or mistaking the temporal sequence markers for indicative verb tenses.

Exemplo

Depois de eles limparem a cozinha, fomos passear.

After they cleaned the kitchen, we went for a walk.

Irregular Verbs in the Personal Infinitive

How do irregular verbs conjugate in the personal infinitive?

📌 Nota gramatical

Many common irregular verbs form their personal infinitive based on their future subjunctive stem or strong root forms (e.g., ter -> tiverdes, vir -> vierem, fazer -> fizerem, dizer -> disserem). Learning these stems is crucial for intermediate fluency. ⚠️ Applying regular -ar/-er/-ir endings to irregular verbs instead of using the correct root derived from the future subjunctive.

Exemplo

Se vós fordes a Coimbra, visitai a universidade.

If you go to Coimbra, visit the university.

Personal Infinitive with Inflected Free Infinitive Clauses

Can the personal infinitive stand alone as a main clause?

📌 Nota gramatical

In certain exclamatory or independent contexts, European Portuguese permits an inflected infinitive to express surprise, indignation, or a rhetorical exclamation about an action performed by a specific group or person. ⚠️ Using standard indicative verb forms in these rhetorical exclamations instead of the emphatic personal infinitive structure.

Exemplo

Nós a fazermos uma coisa dessas? Nunca!

Us doing a thing like that? Never!

Omission of Subject Pronouns with Clear Endings

Do you always need to state the pronoun with the personal infinitive?

📌 Nota gramatical

Because the personal infinitive endings (-es, -mos, -des, -em) are distinct for most persons, explicit subject pronouns (tu, nós, vós, eles) are frequently omitted when the context makes the actor perfectly clear. ⚠️ Redundantly adding every pronoun even when the verb ending clearly communicates the person, making speech sound overly rigid.

Exemplo

É necessário estudardes mais para passardes no exame.

It is necessary for you to study more to pass the exam.

Word order and sentence structure: The Personal Infinitive (Infinitivo pessoal)

Word order and sentence structure rules for The Personal Infinitive (Infinitivo pessoal) (B1): In main clauses, subordinate clauses, and with negation or adverbial phrases, ensure Introduction to the Personal Infinitive, Personal Infinitive with 'ao', Personal Infinitive with 'para', Personal Infinitive with 'por' occupy their strict grammatical position. ⚠️ Misplacing elements relative to the conjugated verb, negation particle, or clause boundary.

Formal register vs. colloquial spoken usage: The Personal Infinitive (Infinitivo pessoal)

Register and usage nuance (B1): While everyday spoken communication frequently uses conversational shortcuts, formal written texts and CEFR B1 exams require precise adherence to The Personal Infinitive (Infinitivo pessoal). ⚠️ Using overly colloquial shortcuts in formal written contexts or stiff bookish phrasing where natural flow is required.

Exemplo
