O que são verbos reflexos?

What are reflexive verbs?

📌 Nota gramatical

Reflexive verbs indicate that the subject performs an action upon itself. In European Portuguese, they require a reflexive pronoun that matches the subject: me (myself), te (yourself), se (himself/herself/yourself formal), nos (ourselves), and se (themselves/yourselves).

Exemplo

Próclise vs. Enclise (Regra Geral)

Proclisis vs. Enclisis (General Rule)

📌 Nota gramatical

In European Portuguese, the default rule for affirmative main clauses is enclisis: the pronoun goes AFTER the verb, attached with a hyphen (e.g., 'levanto-me'). Proclisis (pronoun before the verb) is only used when a trigger word is present.

Exemplo

Conjugação do verbo levantar-se (Presente do Indicativo)

Conjugation of levantar-se (Present Indicative)

📌 Nota gramatical

To conjugate a reflexive verb, combine the regular verb ending with the appropriate reflexive pronoun attached by a hyphen in European Portuguese: eu levanto-me, tu vestes-te, ele/ela/você deita-se, nós levantamo-nos (note the loss of 's' before 'nos'), eles/elas/vocês sentam-se.

Exemplo

Apalancamento por palavras atrativas (Próclise)

Attraction by trigger words (Proclisis)

📌 Nota gramatical

Certain words (negative words like 'não', interrogative words, and relative pronouns) pull the reflexive pronoun BEFORE the verb (proclisis). In European Portuguese, when a trigger word is present, you MUST place the pronoun before the verb.

Exemplo

Frases interrogativas com pronomes reflexos

Interrogative sentences with reflexive pronouns

📌 Nota gramatical

Question words (como, onde, quando, porque) act as trigger words and pull the reflexive pronoun before the verb in European Portuguese. The pronoun sits directly between the question word and the conjugated verb.

Exemplo

Verbos reflexos na rotina diária

Reflexive verbs in daily routine

📌 Nota gramatical

At level A1, reflexive verbs are heavily used for daily hygiene and routine actions: levantar-se (to get up), deitar-se (to go to bed), lavar-se (to wash oneself), pentear-se (to comb one's hair), and vestir-se (to get dressed).

Exemplo

O pronome com 'estar a' + infinitivo

The pronoun with 'estar a' + infinitive

📌 Nota gramatical

In European Portuguese, the progressive tense uses 'estar a + infinitive'. For reflexive verbs, the pronoun can be attached to the infinitive (e.g., 'estou a levantar-me') or placed before the auxiliary 'estar' if there is a trigger word ('não estou a levantar-me').

Exemplo

O pronome com verbos modais (querer, poder, dever)

O pronome com verbos modais (querer, poder, dever)

📌 Nota gramatical

When a modal verb is followed by a reflexive infinitive, you can attach the pronoun to the infinitive ('quero deitar-me') or place it before the modal verb ('quero-me deitar'). In European Portuguese, attaching it to the infinitive is very natural.

Exemplo

Frases afirmativas vs. negativas

Affirmative vs. negative sentences

📌 Nota gramatical

Affirmative sentences require enclisis (pronoun after the verb: 'sento-me'). As soon as a negative particle like 'não' is introduced, proclisis is triggered (pronoun before the verb: 'não me sento').

Exemplo

O pronome reflexo com 'tu' (2ª pessoa)

The reflexive pronoun with 'tu' (2nd person)

📌 Nota gramatical

For the informal 'tu' pronoun, the reflexive pronoun is 'te'. In affirmative sentences, it attaches to the verb with a hyphen ('tu vestes-te'). In negative sentences, it precedes the verb ('tu não te vestes').

Exemplo

O pronome reflexo com 'você / ele / ela' (3ª pessoa)

The reflexive pronoun with 'você / ele / ela' (3rd person)

📌 Nota gramatical

For third-person singular (ele, ela, você), the reflexive pronoun is always 'se'. It attaches to affirmative verbs ('ele deita-se') and goes before negative verbs ('ela não se deita').

Exemplo

O pronome reflexo com 'nós' (1ª pessoa do plural)

The reflexive pronoun with 'nós' (1st person plural)

📌 Nota gramatical

For the first-person plural 'nós', the reflexive pronoun is 'nos'. In affirmative sentences, the verb drops its final '-s' before adding '-nos' (e.g., 'levantamos' becomes 'levantamo-nos'). In negative sentences, it precedes the verb ('nós não nos levantamos').
