Adjective Agreement & Position

Standard -o/-a Adjectives (Type 1)

Adjectives ending in -o change to -a for feminine singular.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Italian adjectives must agree in gender and number with the noun they modify. Type 1 adjectives end in -o in the masculine singular. To make them feminine singular, change -o to -a.

Esempio

Masculine and Feminine Plural Endings (Type 1)

Change masculine -o to -i and feminine -a to -e for plurals.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Building on Type 1 adjectives, the masculine singular (-o) becomes masculine plural (-i), and the feminine singular (-a) becomes feminine plural (-e). Agreement is strictly tied to the noun's gender and number.

Esempio

Invariant -e Adjectives (Type 2)

Adjectives ending in -e are the same for masculine and feminine singular.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Type 2 adjectives end in -e in the singular for both masculine and feminine nouns. They do not change their vowel for gender, meaning one form serves both sexes in the singular.

Esempio

Plural of Type 2 (-e) Adjectives

Change singular -e to plural -i for both genders.

📌 Nota grammaticale

For Type 2 adjectives ending in -e in the singular, the plural form for both masculine and feminine nouns is always -i. Therefore, 'gentile' becomes 'gentili' for all plural nouns.

Esempio

Basic Adjective Position: After the Noun

Most descriptive adjectives follow the noun in Italian.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Unlike English where adjectives usually precede the noun ('a red car'), Italian descriptive adjectives (colors, nationalities, shapes, sizes) standardly follow the noun they modify.

Esempio

Common Pre-Noun Adjectives (BANGS)

Certain common adjectives of Beauty, Age, Number, Goodness precede the noun.

📌 Nota grammaticale

High-frequency adjectives expressing beauty, age, goodness, or size (such as bello, buono, grande, piccolo, nuovo, vecchio) frequently come before the noun in Italian, just like in English.

Esempio

Mixed-Gender Plural Rule

A mixed group of masculine and feminine nouns takes the masculine plural.

📌 Nota grammaticale

When an adjective modifies two or more nouns of different genders, Italian grammar defaults to the masculine plural form for the adjective to encompass the mixed group.

Esempio

Adjectives of Nationality

Nationality adjectives follow the noun and end in -o/-a or -e.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Adjectives indicating nationality or origin (e.g., italiano, francese, tedesco) always follow the noun. They follow regular gender and number agreement rules (-o/-a or -e/-i).

Esempio

Colors Agreement

Color adjectives follow the noun and agree in gender and number.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Colors act as standard descriptive adjectives. They follow the noun and change their endings based on whether the noun is masculine/feminine and singular/plural (e.g., bianco/bianca/bianchi/bianche).

Esempio

Invariable Colors and Foreign Words

Some colors like blu, rosa, and viola never change their endings.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Certain color adjectives derived from nouns (like rosa, viola, blu, marrone in casual speech) or foreign loan words do not change for gender or number. They remain identical across all categories.

Esempio

Bello (Beautiful) Before Nouns

The adjective 'bello' drops its ending like definite articles before nouns.

📌 Nota grammaticale

When 'bello' is placed before the noun, it drops its final vowels and changes form to match the exact definite article (il, lo, la, i, gli, le) that would precede that noun (e.g., bel ragazzo, bell'amico, bella casa).

Esempio

Buono (Good) Before Nouns

The adjective 'buono' truncates its ending like indefinite articles.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Similar to 'bello', when 'buono' is placed before a noun, it mirrors the forms of the indefinite articles 'un', 'uno', or 'una' (e.g., buon caffè, buon amico, buona pizza).
