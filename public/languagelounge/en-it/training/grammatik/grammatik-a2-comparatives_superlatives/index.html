Comparative of Equality (tanto... quanto / così... come)

Expressing that two things are equal in quality using 'as... as'.

📌 Nota grammaticale

To show equality, use 'tanto... quanto' or ' così... come' before adjectives or adverbs. 'Tanto' and 'quanto' can change to match gender and number when used with nouns (tanti... quanti), but remain invariable with adjectives. ⚠️ Translating 'as' literally with 'come' alone for adjectives, forgetting the paired structure 'così... come' or 'tanto... quanto'.

Esempio

Marco è alto quanto suo fratello.

Marco is as tall as his brother.

Comparative of Superiority (più... di)

Expressing that something has more of a quality than something else using 'more... than'.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Formed by placing 'più' (more) before the adjective and 'di' (than) before the compared noun or pronoun. If comparing two adjectives, nouns, or verbs, use 'che' instead of 'di'. ⚠️ Using 'che' instead of 'di' before simple nouns. Remember: use 'di' for nouns/pronouns, and 'che' to compare two infinitives, verbs, or alternative qualities of the same subject.

Esempio

Il treno è più veloce dell'auto.

The train is faster than the car.

Comparative of Inferiority (meno... di / meno... che)

Expressing that something has less of a quality than something else using 'less... than'.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Formed by placing 'meno' (less) before the adjective. Follow the same rule as superiority for the second part: use 'di' before nouns and pronouns, and use 'che' before verbs, adjectives, or contrasting nouns. ⚠️ Confusing 'di' and 'che' after 'meno'. Just like 'più', 'meno' requires 'di' when followed by a direct noun phrase.

Esempio

Questo computer è meno costoso di quello.

This computer is less expensive than that one.

Comparative with 'che' (Comparing two qualities or actions)

Using 'che' instead of 'di' when comparing two adjectives, verbs, or nouns directly.

📌 Nota grammaticale

When you compare two distinct adjectives describing the same person/thing, or two verbs/infinitives, you must use 'che' instead of 'di' after 'più' or 'meno'. ⚠️ Using 'di' when comparing two adjectives that belong to the same noun ('Maria è più alta di intelligente' is wrong; it must be 'più alta che intelligente').

Esempio

Luca è più intelligente che simpatico.

Luca is more intelligent than nice.

Relative Superlative (il più / il meno)

Expressing the highest or lowest degree of a quality within a group ('the most' / 'the least').

📌 Nota grammaticale

Formed by combining the definite article (il, la, i, le) with 'più' or 'meno' and the adjective. The preposition 'di' or 'tra/fra' is used to specify the group ('in' the world, 'of' the class). ⚠️ Forgetting to repeat the definite article before 'più' or 'meno' when it follows the noun (e.g., writing 'la città più bella' is correct, but writing 'la più bella città' also works; forgetting the article entirely is wrong).

Esempio

Roma è la città più bella d'Italia.

Rome is the most beautiful city in Italy.

Absolute Superlative with '-issimo/a/i/e'

Expressing a very high degree of a quality without comparison ('very' or 'extremely').

📌 Nota grammaticale

Drop the final vowel of the adjective and add '-issimo' (masculine singular), '-issima' (feminine singular), '-issimi' (masculine plural), or '-issime' (feminine plural). Adjectives ending in '-co/-go' often take an 'h' to maintain the hard sound (-issimo). ⚠️ Forgetting the spelling change in adjectives ending in -co/-go (e.g., 'fresco' becomes 'freschissimo', not 'frescissimo').

Esempio

La pizza è deliziosa e caldissima.

The pizza is delicious and very hot.

Irregular Comparatives: buono, cattivo, grande, piccolo

Adjectives with special irregular comparative forms (better, worse, bigger, smaller).

📌 Nota grammaticale

Some common adjectives have irregular comparative forms: 'buono' (good) -> 'migliore' (better); 'cattivo' (bad) -> 'peggiore' (worse); 'grande' (big) -> 'maggiore' (older/bigger); 'piccolo' (small) -> 'minore' (younger/smaller). Regular forms (più buono) are also heard in spoken Italian, but the irregulars are preferred. ⚠️ Using 'più buono' instead of 'migliore'. While native speakers sometimes use 'più buono', 'migliore' is grammatically standard and preferred in writing.

Esempio

Questo caffè è migliore di quello.

This coffee is better than that one.

Irregular Superlatives: ottimo, pessimo, massimo, minimo

Special absolute superlative forms for common irregular adjectives.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Along with regular absolute superlatives (buonissimo), these adjectives have special Latin-derived absolute superlatives: 'buono' -> 'ottimo' (excellent); 'cattivo' -> 'pessimo' (terrible); 'grande' -> 'massimo' (maximum/greatest); 'piccolo' -> 'minimo' (minimum/smallest). ⚠️ Saying 'molto ottimo' or 'molto pessimo'. Words like 'ottimo' and 'pessimo' already mean 'very good' or 'very bad', so adding 'molto' is redundant and incorrect.

Esempio

Ho fatto un esame con un risultato ottimo.

I took an exam with an excellent result.

Irregular Adverbs: bene, male, molto, poco

Comparative and superlative forms of common adverbs (well, badly, much, little).

📌 Nota grammaticale

Adverbs also have irregular comparative and superlative forms. 'Bene' (well) -> 'meglio' (better) -> 'benissimo' (very well); 'male' (badly) -> 'peggio' (worse) -> 'malissimo' (very badly); 'molto' (much) -> 'più' (more) -> 'moltissimo' (very much); 'poco' (little) -> 'meno' (less) -> 'pochissimo' (very little). ⚠️ Mixing up adjective and adverb forms. Remember that adverbs modify verbs, so 'parlare bene' becomes 'parlare meglio', not 'migliore'.

Esempio

Oggi parlo italiano meglio di ieri.

Today I speak Italian better than yesterday.

Alternative Absolute Superlatives with Adverbs

Making adjectives very intense using adverbs like 'molto', 'assai', or 'davvero'.

📌 Nota grammaticale

Instead of adding '-issimo', you can form an absolute superlative by placing an intensifier adverb before the regular adjective. Common adverbs include 'molto' (very), 'davvero' (really), 'estremamente' (extremely), and 'assai' (very/quite). ⚠️ Confusing the absolute superlative suffix '-issimo' with adverbial intensifiers. Both are correct, but you cannot combine them ('molto buonissimo' is incorrect).

Esempio

Questo gelato è davvero buono.

This ice cream is really good.

Superlative with Possessive Adjectives (Il mio migliore amico)

Expressing relative superlatives involving possession ('my best friend').

📌 Nota grammaticale

When a relative superlative noun phrase includes a possessive adjective, Italian drops the definite article before 'più/meno' or irregular forms like 'migliore' if the possessive comes right before the noun, but keeps the article if the possessive follows or in standard direct structures ('il mio amico migliore' or 'il mio migliore amico'). ⚠️ Dropping the article completely when it is required: 'È mio migliore amico' is missing the definite article. It must be 'È il mio migliore amico'.

Esempio

Luigi è il mio migliore amico.

Luigi is my best friend.

Comparing Quantities (più... di / meno... di with nouns)

Comparing amounts or quantities using 'more/less ... than' with nouns.

📌 Nota grammaticale

When comparing quantities with nouns, use 'più di' (more than) or 'meno di' (less than) directly before the noun. Remember that 'di' contracts with definite articles (del, dello, della, dei, degli, delle). ⚠️ Using 'che' instead of 'di' when comparing quantities with a noun phrase ('Ho più libri che te' is a common colloquial mistake, but standard grammar requires 'più libri di te').

Esempio

Ho più libri di te.

I have more books than you.

Contrast rule and common errors: Comparatives & Superlatives

📌 Nota grammaticale

Key contrast in Comparatives & Superlatives: Pay special attention to distinguishing core forms (Comparative of Equality, Comparative of Superiority, Comparative of Inferiority, Comparative with 'che') from their false friends or overlapping structures. Primary pitfall: Translating 'as' literally with 'come' alone for adjectives, forgetting the paired structure 'così... come' or 'tanto... quanto'. ⚠️ Avoid the common error: Translating 'as' literally with 'come' alone for adjectives, forgetting the paired structure 'così... come' or 'tanto... quanto'.

Esempio

Marco is as tall as his brother.

Ordine delle parole e posizione sintattica: Comparativi e Superlativi

Word order and sentence structure: Comparatives & Superlatives

📌 Nota grammaticale

Word order and sentence structure rules for Comparatives & Superlatives (A2): In main clauses, subordinate clauses, and with negation or adverbial phrases, ensure Comparative of Equality, Comparative of Superiority, Comparative of Inferiority, Comparative with 'che' occupy their strict grammatical position. ⚠️ Misplacing elements relative to the conjugated verb, negation particle, or clause boundary.

Esempio

Il treno è più veloce dell'auto.

The train is faster than the car.

Registro formale vs uso colloquiale: Comparativi e Superlativi

Formal register vs. colloquial spoken usage: Comparatives & Superlatives

📌 Nota grammaticale

Register and usage nuance (A2): While everyday spoken communication frequently uses conversational shortcuts, formal written texts and CEFR A2 exams require precise adherence to Comparatives & Superlatives. ⚠️ Using overly colloquial shortcuts in formal written contexts or stiff bookish phrasing where natural flow is required.

Esempio

Questo computer è meno costoso di quello.

This computer is less expensive than that one.

Regola di decisione e sintesi pratica: Comparativi e Superlativi

Decision framework and practical summary: Comparatives & Superlatives

📌 Nota grammaticale

Decision framework for Comparatives & Superlatives (A2): Before producing a sentence, verify 1) grammatical agreement (gender, number, person), 2) tense/mood triggers, and 3) exact word order. ⚠️ Producing clauses without checking case, mood triggers, or subject-verb agreement.

Esempio

Luca è più intelligente che simpatico.

Luca is more intelligent than nice.
