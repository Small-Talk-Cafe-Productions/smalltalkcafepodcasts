The Imperative (Informal & Formal)

L'imperativo tu (-are verbs)

Informal imperative for -are verbs (tu form)

📌 Nota grammaticale

To give an informal command to someone using 'tu' with regular -are verbs, use the third-person singular present form (-a) for regular verbs. For example, parlare becomes parla!

Esempio

L'imperativo tu (-ere and -ire verbs)

Informal imperative for -ere and -ire verbs (tu form)

📌 Nota grammaticale

For -ere and -ire verbs, the informal 'tu' imperative uses the -i ending. This matches the second-person singular present indicative for -ere verbs, but can differ for -ire verbs that take an -isc- infix.

Esempio

L'imperativo tu con i verbi in -isc-

Informal imperative for -isc- verbs (tu form)

📌 Nota grammaticale

Verbs like finire, capire, and pulire that insert -isc- in the present indicative keep this extension in the informal imperative tu form, taking the -i ending (finisci, capisci).

Esempio

L'imperativo formale Lei (-are verbs)

Formal imperative using 'Lei' (-are verbs)

📌 Nota grammaticale

When giving commands to someone you address formally as 'Lei', the verb takes the third-person subjunctive ending, which uses -i for -are verbs (parlare -> parli).

Esempio

L'imperativo formale Lei (-ere and -ire verbs)

Formal imperative using 'Lei' (-ere and -ire verbs)

📌 Nota grammaticale

For formal 'Lei' commands, -ere and -ire verbs use the -a ending (prendere -> prenda, aprire -> apra, finire -> finisca).

Esempio

L'imperativo negativo (tu)

Negative informal imperative (tu + non + infinitive)

📌 Nota grammaticale

To tell someone informally NOT to do something, use 'non' followed directly by the infinitive of the verb, NOT the conjugated imperative form.

Esempio

L'imperativo negativo (Lei)

Negative formal imperative (Lei + non + conjugated verb)

📌 Nota grammaticale

Unlike the informal 'tu', the formal negative imperative for 'Lei' uses 'non' followed by the normally conjugated formal imperative form (non + prenda).

Esempio

I verbi irregolari comuni (andare, dare, dire, fare, stare)

Common irregular imperative forms (tu)

📌 Nota grammaticale

Several common verbs have short, monosyllabic irregular forms in the informal tu imperative, often accompanied by apostrophes or doubled consonants: va' (andare), da' (dare), di' (dire), fa' (fare), sta' (stare).

Esempio

Il verbo essere e avere all'imperativo

Imperative forms for essere and avere

📌 Nota grammaticale

Both essere and avere have special imperative forms for both tu and Lei. Essere: sii (tu), sia (Lei). Avere: abbi (tu), abbia (Lei).

Esempio

I verbi irregolari formali (essere, avere, andare, fare)

Formal irregular imperative forms (Lei)

📌 Nota grammaticale

Formal Lei commands follow the subjunctive present, meaning irregulars like essere (sia), avere (abbia), andare ( vada ), and fare (faccia) use their standard subjunctive counterparts.

Esempio

I verbi modali all'imperativo

Modal verbs in the imperative

📌 Nota grammaticale

Modal verbs (dovere, potere, volere) can technically take imperative forms, but 'dovere' and 'potere' are rarely used. 'Volere' is frequently used in the imperative as 'vogli' (tu) or 'voglia' (Lei) to mean 'have the kindness to'.

Esempio

I pronomi con l'imperativo (tu e Lei)

Pronoun placement with the imperative

📌 Nota grammaticale

With affirmative imperatives (tu and Lei), direct/indirect object pronouns attach directly to the end of the verb to form one single word (e.g., 'compralo'). With short irregulars (va', fa'), the consonant doubles (fallo, vallo).
