Introduction to L'Imperfetto

Expressing past habits, ongoing actions, and background descriptions

📌 Nota grammaticale

The imperfetto (imperfect tense) is used in Italian to describe ongoing, habitual, or repeated actions in the past, as well as physical and mental states, weather, and time. It is equivalent to 'used to', 'would', or the English past continuous ('was/were -ing'). ⚠️ Using the imperfetto for a completed, single action with a specific time frame instead of the passato prossimo.

Esempio

Da piccolo mangiavo sempre la pizza.

When I was little, I always ate pizza.

Regular -ARE Verbs Conjugation

Conjugating verbs ending in -are in the imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

To conjugate regular -are verbs in the imperfetto, drop the -are from the infinitive and add the endings: -avo, -avi, -ava, -avamo, -avate, -avano. Note that the first-person singular ends in -avo. ⚠️ Confusing the 1st person singular ending (-avo) with the present tense ending (-o).

Esempio

Io parlavo spesso con Maria.

I often spoke with Maria.

Regular -ERE Verbs Conjugation

Conjugating verbs ending in -ere in the imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

To conjugate regular -ere verbs, drop the -ere from the infinitive and add the endings: -evo, -evi, -eva, -evamo, -evate, -evano. All persons share the 'ev-' stem characteristic of this group. ⚠️ Using -avo endings for -ere verbs because -are is the most common verb class.

Esempio

Tu leggevi un libro interessante.

You were reading an interesting book.

Regular -IRE Verbs Conjugation

Conjugating verbs ending in -ire in the imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

To conjugate regular -ire verbs, drop the -ire and add the endings: -ivo, -ivi, -iva, -ivamo, -ivate, -ivano. Unlike the present tense, -ire verbs in the imperfetto do not insert the '-isc-' infix. ⚠️ Incorrectly adding the present tense '-isc-' extension (e.g., finischivo instead of finivo).

Esempio

Io finivo sempre i compiti.

I always finished my homework.

Irregular Verb: Essere

Conjugating 'essere' (to be) in the imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

The verb 'essere' is irregular in the imperfetto. Its stem changes to 'er-', yielding: ero, eri, era, eravamo, eravate, erano. It is frequently used for age, time, and descriptions in the past. ⚠️ Using regular forms like esserevo or confusing 'ero' with the present tense 'sono'.

Esempio

Quando ero giovane, ero molto timido.

When I was young, I was very shy.

Irregular Verb: Avere

Conjugating 'avere' (to have) in the imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

Although 'avere' is an -ere verb, it maintains a slightly contracted historical stem in the imperfetto: avevo, avevi, aveva, avevamo, avevate, avevano. Note the absence of the 'e' from the typical -ere stem before the ending. ⚠️ Treating 'avere' as a completely regular -ere verb and saying avevevi or avevo without the 'v'.

Esempio

Io avevo un cane nero.

I had a black dog.

Irregular Contracted Verbs: Bere, Dire, Tradurre

Conjugating contraction-stem verbs in the imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

Verbs with contracted Latin-derived infinitives like bere (bev-) and dire (dic-) use their historical roots before adding the standard -ere/-are endings. Bere becomes bevevo; dire becomes dicevo; tradurre becomes traducevo. ⚠️ Trying to conjugate based on the modern short infinitive bere rather than the historical root bev-.

Esempio

Tutti i giorni bevevo un caffè.

Every day I used to drink a coffee.

Expressing Habitual Actions (Past Routines)

Using the imperfetto for repetitive actions and habits

📌 Nota grammaticale

The imperfetto is the primary tense for expressing repeated, habitual actions in the past, often translated as 'used to' or 'would' in English. Signal words include sempre (always), spesso (often), ogni tanto (every now and then), and da piccolo (when little). ⚠️ Using the passato prossimo for repetitive past habits instead of the imperfetto.

Esempio

Da bambina giocavo sempre con le bambole.

As a little girl, I always played with dolls.

Background Descriptions: Weather, Time, and Age

Setting the scene in the past using the imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

Descriptions of atmospheric conditions, time of day, seasons, physical appearance, and age in the past always require the imperfetto because these act as the background setting rather than completed events. ⚠️ Using the passato prossimo for weather or age descriptions (e.g., ha fatto caldo for background weather).

Esempio

Faceva molto caldo e pioveva.

It was very hot and it was raining.

Simultaneous Actions: Mentre

Describing two actions happening at the same time in the past

📌 Nota grammaticale

When two ongoing actions occur simultaneously in the past, the conjunction mentre (while) is typically followed by two verbs in the imperfetto. Neither action has a defined beginning or end. ⚠️ Using the passato prossimo for background simultaneous actions.

Esempio

Io leggevo mentre Maria cucinava.

I was reading while Maria was cooking.

Interrupted Actions: Imperfetto + Passato Prossimo

Combining ongoing background action with a sudden interrupting event

📌 Nota grammaticale

A very common narrative pattern uses the imperfetto to describe an ongoing background state or action, which is suddenly interrupted by a single, completed event expressed in the passato prossimo, often joined by quando (when). ⚠️ putting both verbs in the imperfetto or both in the passato prossimo, ignoring the interruption dynamic.

Esempio

Dormivo quando è suonata la sveglia.

I was sleeping when the alarm clock rang.

Mental States, Desires, and Opinions

Expressing feelings, thoughts, and wishes in the past

📌 Nota grammaticale

Verbs expressing internal states, emotions, thoughts, or intentions—such as volere (to want), sapere (to know), pensare (to think), credere (to believe), and volere—are almost always expressed in the imperfetto when describing past feelings without a specific starting point. ⚠️ Using the passato prossimo for psychological states (ho voluto implies a decision made, whereas volevo implies a general desire).

Esempio

Non sapevo la risposta alla domanda.

I didn't know the answer to the question.

Ordine delle parole e posizione sintattica: L'Imperfetto

Word order and sentence structure: The Imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

Word order and sentence structure rules for The Imperfetto (A2): In main clauses, subordinate clauses, and with negation or adverbial phrases, ensure Introduction to L'Imperfetto, Regular -ARE Verbs Conjugation, Regular -ERE Verbs Conjugation, Regular -IRE Verbs Conjugation occupy their strict grammatical position. ⚠️ Misplacing elements relative to the conjugated verb, negation particle, or clause boundary.

Esempio

I often spoke with Maria.

Registro formale vs uso colloquiale: L'Imperfetto

Formal register vs. colloquial spoken usage: The Imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

Register and usage nuance (A2): While everyday spoken communication frequently uses conversational shortcuts, formal written texts and CEFR A2 exams require precise adherence to The Imperfetto. ⚠️ Using overly colloquial shortcuts in formal written contexts or stiff bookish phrasing where natural flow is required.

Esempio

Tu leggevi un libro interessante.

You were reading an interesting book.

Regola di decisione e sintesi pratica: L'Imperfetto

Decision framework and practical summary: The Imperfetto

📌 Nota grammaticale

Decision framework for The Imperfetto (A2): Before producing a sentence, verify 1) grammatical agreement (gender, number, person), 2) tense/mood triggers, and 3) exact word order. ⚠️ Producing clauses without checking case, mood triggers, or subject-verb agreement.

Esempio

Io finivo sempre i compiti.

I always finished my homework.
