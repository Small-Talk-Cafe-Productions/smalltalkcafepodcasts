Introduction and core function: expressing polite requests and desires

How to use the present conditional for politeness and wishes in Italian

📌 Nota grammaticale

The condizionale presente (present conditional) is used to express what someone would do under certain conditions. For B1 learners, its primary communicative function is softening requests, desires, or opinions so they sound polite rather than demanding. It often translates to 'would' in English. ⚠️ Using the present indicative ('Voglio un caffè') instead of the conditional ('Vorrei'), which sounds abrupt and demanding to Italian ears.

Esempio

Vorrei un caffè macchiato e un cornetto alla crema, per favore.

I would like a macchiato coffee and a cream croissant, please.

Regular -ARE verbs conjugation paradigm

Conjugating regular verbs ending in -ARE in the conditional mood

📌 Nota grammaticale

To conjugate regular -ARE verbs in the condizionale presente, drop the final 'e' of the infinitive, change the thematic vowel 'a' to 'e', and add the standard conditional endings: -ei, -esti, -ebbe, -emmo, -este, -ebbero. Note that parlare becomes parlerei. ⚠️ Forgetting to change the thematic vowel from 'a' to 'e', incorrectly saying parlar-ei instead of parler-ei.

Esempio

Se avessi più tempo libero, parlerei volentieri più spesso con te.

If I had more free time, I would gladly speak with you more often.

Regular -ERE and -IRE verbs conjugation paradigm

Conjugating regular verbs ending in -ERE and -IRE in the conditional mood

📌 Nota grammaticale

For regular -ERE and -IRE verbs, drop the final 'e' of the infinitive stem and add the conditional endings directly (-ei, -esti, -ebbe, -emmo, -este, -ebbero). The stem vowel 'e' or 'i' of the infinitive is retained. For example, scrivere becomes scriverei and dormire becomes dormirei. ⚠️ Dropping the vowel of the -ire or -ere infinitive entirely, leading to malformed stems like scriv-ei instead of scriverei.

Esempio

Scriverei una lettera formale al direttore, ma non conosco il suo indirizzo.

I would write a formal letter to the director, but I don't know his address.

First contraction group: -ARE verbs dropping 'a' in the stem

Irregular conditional stems for -ARE verbs like volere, dovere, potere, andare

📌 Nota grammaticale

Certain common verbs undergo contraction in the conditional stem. Verbs like volere, dovere, potere, and andare drop the 'a' of the infinitive ending, changing their stems to vorr-, dovr-, potr-, and andr-, before taking the standard conditional endings. For example, potere becomes potrei. ⚠️ Treating modal verbs as completely regular based on their infinitive, producing incorrect forms like poterei or doverei.

Esempio

Non potrei mai trasferirmi in una città che non ha mare.

I could never move to a city that has no sea.

Second contraction group: dropping the vowel 'e' from the infinitive stem

Irregular conditional stems for verbs like rimanere, tenere, venire, cadere

📌 Nota grammaticale

Verbs like rimanere, tenere, venire, and cadere drop the vowel 'e' from their infinitive suffix and often double the 'r' in the stem (rimarr-, terr-, verr-, cadr-). This is identical to their future tense stems. For instance, venire becomes verrei. ⚠️ Forgetting the double 'r' sound in pronunciation and writing, creating incorrect forms like venrei or rimarebbe.

Esempio

Verrei alla tua festa di compleanno, ma purtroppo devo lavorare fino tardi.

I would come to your birthday party, but unfortunately I have to work until late.

Contracted stems for ESSERE and Avere

The irregular conditional stems of auxiliary verbs essere and avere

📌 Nota grammaticale

The auxiliary verbs essere and avere have completely irregular stems in the conditional: essere becomes sar- (sarei, saresti, sarebbe, saremmo, sareste, sarebbero) and avere becomes avr- (avrei, avresti, avrebbe, avremmo, avreste, avrebbero). Mastering these is essential for building compound conditional structures later. ⚠️ Using regular endings directly on the infinitive like esserebbe or averei, confusing the auxiliary roots.

Esempio

Sarei molto felice di accettare questa fantastica offerta di lavoro.

I would be very happy to accept this fantastic job offer.

Expressing personal opinions and soft advice with dovere and potere

Giving polite advice or soft suggestions using modal verbs in the conditional

📌 Nota grammaticale

At B1 level, you can use the conditional of modal verbs (dovresti, potresti) to give gentle advice, recommendations, or express personal opinions without sounding overly critical or authoritative. It frames suggestions as friendly possibilities rather than strict commands. ⚠️ Using the imperative mood ('Studia di più!') when trying to give friendly suggestions, which sounds overly harsh.

Esempio

Dovresti consultare un medico esperto se questo fastidioso mal di schiena continua.

You should consult an expert doctor if this annoying back pain continues.

Expressing unverified news and journalistic hypothesis (Il condizionale giornalistico)

Reporting unconfirmed facts, rumors, or news using the conditional tense

📌 Nota grammaticale

A very common usage in Italian media (news, newspapers, television) is the 'condizionale giornalistico'. It is used to report events, crimes, or statements that have not yet been officially verified or confirmed by authorities, protecting journalists from legal liability. ⚠️ Interpreting journalistic conditional as a firm fact, missing the underlying nuance that the information is unconfirmed rumor.

Esempio

Il famoso politico sarebbe coinvolto in un grave scandalo finanziario internazionale.

The famous politician is allegedly involved in a serious international financial scandal.

Expressing future in the past (Il futuro nel passato)

Relating a past event to something that was going to happen later

📌 Nota grammaticale

When narrating past events, the conditional present acts as a 'future in the past'. It expresses an action that was future relative to a point in the past. This often occurs in indirect speech when the main reporting clause is in the past tense (e.g., 'Diceva che...'). ⚠️ Using the simple future tense (verrà) inside a past-tense narrative instead of the conditional (sarebbe venuto).

Esempio

Marco ha detto che sarebbe venuto a trovarci appena finito il turno.

Marco said that he would come to visit us as soon as he finished his shift.

Hypothetical clauses: Second Conditional (Periodo ipotetico della possibilità)

Expressing present or future hypothetical scenarios and their possible outcomes

📌 Nota grammaticale

In conditional sentences of possibility (second conditional), the main clause uses the condizionale presente, while the subordinate clause (introduced by 'se') uses il congiuntivo imperfetto. This structure describes theoretical situations that are possible in the present or future. ⚠️ Mixing indicative and conditional in the 'se' clause, such as saying Se ho soldi, comprerei, which violates grammatical agreement.

Esempio

Se avessi abbastanza soldi, comprerei una bella casa vicino al mare.

If I had enough money, I would buy a nice house near the sea.

Expressing personal doubts, uncertainty, and polite disagreement

Soften assertions or disagreeing gently using the present conditional

📌 Nota grammaticale

At B1 level, expressing opinions politely is crucial. Instead of flatly stating 'Non è vero' or 'Questo è sbagliato', Italians frequently use the conditional of verbs like pensare, credere, or modal structures to soften disagreements, show hesitation, or voice personal doubt. ⚠️ Sounding overly blunt by using direct present tense verbs when expressing controversial opinions in professional settings.

Esempio

Non saprei dirti con certezza se questa sia la decisione migliore.

I couldn't tell you with certainty whether this is the best decision.

Common orthographic traps: Verbs in -CIARE, -GIARE, and -GARE, -CIARE

Spelling rules for verbs ending in -ciare, -gare, -iare in the conditional

📌 Nota grammaticale

When conjugating verbs ending in -ciare and -giare, the unstressed 'i' of the stem is dropped in the conditional because the conditional ending already starts with 'e' (e.g., mangerei, not mangiererei). For verbs ending in -care and -gare (like pagare, giocare), an 'h' must be inserted to maintain the hard sound: pagherei, giocherei. ⚠️ Forgetting to add the 'h' in -gare/-care verbs (pagerei instead of pagherei), or keeping the extra 'i' in -ciare verbs (mangierei with two i's).

Esempio

Se avessi fame, mangerei volentieri un piatto di spaghetti al pomodoro.

If I were hungry, I would gladly eat a plate of spaghetti with tomato sauce.

Word order and sentence structure: The Condizionale presente

Word order and sentence structure rules for The Condizionale presente (B1): In main clauses, subordinate clauses, and with negation or adverbial phrases, ensure Regular -ARE verbs conjugation paradigm, Contracted stems for ESSERE and Avere, Expressing future in the past occupy their strict grammatical position. ⚠️ Misplacing elements relative to the conjugated verb, negation particle, or clause boundary.

Registro formale vs uso colloquiale: Il condizionale presente

Formal register vs. colloquial spoken usage: The Condizionale presente

📌 Nota grammaticale

Register and usage nuance (B1): While everyday spoken communication frequently uses conversational shortcuts, formal written texts and CEFR B1 exams require precise adherence to The Condizionale presente. ⚠️ Using overly colloquial shortcuts in formal written contexts or stiff bookish phrasing where natural flow is required.

Esempio

Scriverei una lettera formale al direttore, ma non conosco il suo indirizzo.

I would write a formal letter to the director, but I don't know his address.

Regola di decisione e sintesi pratica: Il condizionale presente

Decision framework and practical summary: The Condizionale presente

📌 Nota grammaticale

Decision framework for The Condizionale presente (B1): Before producing a sentence, verify 1) grammatical agreement (gender, number, person), 2) tense/mood triggers, and 3) exact word order. ⚠️ Producing clauses without checking case, mood triggers, or subject-verb agreement.

Esempio

I could never move to a city that has no sea.
