Introduction to the Passive Voice with 'Essere'

How to form the passive voice using 'essere' + past participle

📌 Nota grammaticale

The passive voice shifts the focus from the agent (doer) to the recipient of the action. To form it, use the auxiliary verb 'essere' in the correct tense followed by the past participle of a transitive verb. Crucially, the past participle must agree in gender and number with the new grammatical subject (masculine/feminine, singular/plural).

Agent Introduction with 'Da' (By)

How to express the doer of the action in the passive voice

📌 Nota grammaticale

In passive sentences, the person or thing performing the action is introduced by the preposition 'da' (by). When 'da' is followed by a definite article (il, lo, la, i, gli, le), it contracts into preposizioni articolate (dal, dallo, dalla, dai, dagli, dalle).

The Passive with 'Venire'

Using 'venire' instead of 'essere' in simple tenses

📌 Nota grammaticale

The verb 'venire' can replace 'essere' to form the passive voice in simple tenses (present, imperfect, future, etc.). However, 'venire' cannot be used in compound tenses (like the passato prossimo). It adds a dynamic nuance, emphasizing that an action is actively taking place.

Esempio

Exclusion of 'Venire' in Compound Tenses

Why 'essere' is mandatory for compound passive forms

📌 Nota grammaticale

Because 'venire' intrinsically denotes action and motion, it lacks the state-of-being aspect required for compound passive tenses. Therefore, whenever you need to express a completed passive action (passato prossimo, trapassato prossimo), 'essere' (conjugated in the appropriate tense) plus 'stato/stata/stati/state' is the only correct choice.

Esempio

The Passive with 'Andare' (Necessity/Obligation)

Using 'andare' to express that something needs to be done

📌 Nota grammaticale

The verb 'andare' can be used with a past participle to form a special passive structure that carries a strong sense of necessity, duty, or obligation (similar to 'must be done' or 'needs to be'). This is primarily used in simple tenses and conveys a practical requirement.

Comparison: Essere vs. Venire vs. Andare

Choosing the right auxiliary for the passive voice

📌 Nota grammaticale

Understanding the subtle differences is vital: 'Essere' describes a state or a general passive action across all tenses. 'Venire' describes an active process in simple tenses only. 'Andare' implies obligation or necessity ('needs to be done'). Selecting the wrong auxiliary alters the intended nuance.

The Passive in the Passato Prossimo

Forming the passive voice for past completed actions

📌 Nota grammaticale

To form the passato prossimo passive, combine the auxiliary 'essere' in the present tense, the invariable past participle 'stato' (or stata, stati, state), and the main verb's past participle. Remember that the final past participle agrees with the subject of the passive sentence.

Esempio

The Passive in the Imperfetto (Imperfect Tense)

Expressing ongoing or habitual past passive actions

📌 Nota grammaticale

When describing continuous, repeated, or background passive actions in the past, use 'essere' in the imperfetto (ero, eri, era, eravamo, eravate, erano) plus the past participle. Alternatively, 'venire' in the imperfetto (vo, vi, va, vamo vate, vano) is also common for action in progress.

The Passive with Modal Verbs (dovere, potere, volere)

Using modal verbs with passive infinitives

📌 Nota grammaticale

When modal verbs (dovere, potere, volere) modify a passive construction, the modal verb is followed by the passive infinitive 'essere' (or 'venire') plus the past participle. Alternatively, the modal can precede the main verb with 'essere' attached to the modal's subject rules.

Esempio

Alternative to Passive: The Si Passivante

Using 'si' + 3rd person verb as a passive alternative

📌 Nota grammaticale

In everyday Italian, native speakers frequently avoid the formal passive voice with 'essere' and instead use the 'si passivante'. The pronoun 'si' is combined with a singular or plural verb matching the following noun, which acts as the grammatical subject.

Esempio

Passive Voice with Future Tense

Expressing future events using the passive voice

📌 Nota grammaticale

To speak about future actions in the passive voice, use 'essere' or 'venire' conjugated in the future simple tense (sarò, sarai, sarà, saremo, sarete, saranno), followed by the agreeing past participle. This is common in formal writing, news reports, and official announcements.

Esempio

Avoiding the Passive Voice in Conversational Italian

Stylistic preference for active and impersonal forms in speech

📌 Nota grammaticale

While the passive voice is common in written Italian, journalism, and formal texts, everyday spoken Italian heavily favors active constructions, impersonal structures with 'si', or restructuring the sentence to use 'loro' or 'si' to sound more natural and less rigid.
