Introduction à la nominalisation

Introduction to nominalization in formal writing

📌 Note de grammaire

Nominalization involves transforming verbs, adjectives, or clauses into nouns. In French, it is a key stylistic device for formal writing, reports, and journalism because it increases information density and removes the need for a personal subject.

Exemple

Verbes en -er transformés en noms en -ation

Verbs ending in -er transformed into nouns in -ation

📌 Note de grammaire

Many regular verbs ending in -er form their corresponding action noun by adding the suffix -ation (or -ition). This pattern is extremely productive for converting active verbs into formal abstract nouns.

Exemple

Verbes en -re / -ir transformés en noms en -ment

Verbs ending in -re or -ir transformed into nouns in -ment

📌 Note de grammaire

Verbs often form masculine nouns indicating the result or process of an action using the suffix -ment. This is particularly common with verbs ending in -re (like changer -> changement) or -ir.

Exemple

Transformation des adjectifs en noms en -té / -ité

Transformation of adjectives into nouns ending in -té / -ité

📌 Note de grammaire

Qualitative adjectives are regularly converted into abstract nouns using the suffix -té or -ité (e.g., clair -> clarté, rapide -> rapidité). These nouns are invariably feminine and add elegance to professional writing.

Exemple

Noms d'action en -ance et -ence

Action nouns ending in -ance and -ence

📌 Note de grammaire

Adjectives ending in -ant or -ent often yield feminine abstract nouns ending in -ance or -ence respectively. These are heavily relied upon in administrative, corporate, and formal academic French.

Exemple

Paires supplétives (racines différentes)

Suppletive pairs (unrelated noun/verb roots)

📌 Note de grammaire

Some common verbs do not take standard suffixes to form nouns; instead, they use entirely different lexical roots (suppletion). Writers must memorize these pairs for natural formal expression.

Exemple

Allègement de la syntaxe par la nominalisation

Simplifying syntax through nominalization

📌 Note de grammaire

Complex subordinate clauses introduced by parce que or quand can be condensed into prepositional phrases containing a nominalized noun (e.g., grâce à + nom instead of parce que).

Exemple

Éviter les pronoms sujets lourds

Avoiding heavy subject pronouns

📌 Note de grammaire

In formal French, repeating subject pronouns or using vague pronouns like on or vous can sound colloquial. Nominalization allows writers to frame actions objectively without attributing them to a specific person.

Exemple

La nominalisation avec les verbes de perception ou d'opinion

Nominalization with verbs of perception or opinion

📌 Note de grammaire

Verbs expressing belief, thought, or observation (penser, observer, constater) can be transformed into nouns (la pensée, l'observation, le constat) to introduce formal statements objectively.

Exemple

Précision des déterminants dans les groupes nominaux

Precision of determiners in nominal groups

📌 Note de grammaire

When nominalizing, the choice of determiner changes the nuance. Definite articles (le, la, les) imply general or specific known actions, whereas possessive adjectives highlight responsibility or authorship.

Exemple

Registres de langue : Oral vs Écrit formel

Registers of language: Spoken vs Formal written

📌 Note de grammaire

At B2 level, distinguishing between spoken syntax (subject + verb + subordinate clause) and written syntax (nominal group + strong verb) is essential for mastering professional emails, cover letters, and essays.

Exemple

Pièges fréquents et sur-nominalisation

Frequent traps and over-nominalization

📌 Note de grammaire

While nominalization elevates style, 'over-nominalization' makes text dense, bureaucratic, and difficult to read (la novlangue). Writers should balance nominal groups with clear, active verbs when necessary.
