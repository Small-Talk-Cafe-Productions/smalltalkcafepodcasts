Passé composé vs Imparfait

Le passé composé et l'imparfait • A2 • 12 cartes

📐

Structure

Exemples

Action soudaine ou ponctuelle (Passé composé)

Sudden or one-time completed actions in the past

📌 Note de grammaire

Use the passé composé for actions that happened at a specific moment, interrupted a state, or advanced the storyline forward. It views the event as a completed whole.

Exemple

État, description ou habitude (Imparfait)

States, descriptions, or recurring habits in the past

📌 Note de grammaire

Use the imparfait to describe ongoing background situations, weather, physical or emotional states, and past habits ('used to do'). There is no clear beginning or end.

Exemple

Action interrompue (Imparfait + Passé composé)

Interrupted action (Ongoing background action + interrupting action)

📌 Note de grammaire

A very common pattern combines both tenses: an ongoing action in the imparfait is suddenly interrupted by a new action in the passé composé. The word 'quand' or 'lorsque' often links them.

Exemple

Série d'actions successives (Passé composé)

A sequence of consecutive actions in a story

📌 Note de grammaire

When narrating a sequence of events that happened one after another, pushing the story forward, use the passé composé for every action in the chain.

Exemple

Circonstances et arrière-plan (Imparfait)

Setting the scene: weather, time, place, and mood

📌 Note de grammaire

At the beginning of a story or description, the imparfait sets the stage (weather, time, atmosphere) before the main events (passé composé) take place.

Exemple

Actions simultanées dans le passé (Imparfait + Imparfait)

Simultaneous ongoing actions in the past

📌 Note de grammaire

When two or more background actions were happening at the same time without interrupting each other, use the imparfait for both verbs, often connected by 'pendant que'.

Exemple

Changement d'état mental ou émotionnel (Passé composé)

Sudden realization or change of mind/feeling

📌 Note de grammaire

Verbs of mental state (vouloir, pouvoir, savoir, connaître, penser, croire) usually take the imparfait. However, when used in the passé composé, they often imply a sudden change, a specific realization, or an action successfully completed.

Exemple

Verbes d'état mental en arrière-plan (Imparfait)

Ongoing mental states or opinions in the past

📌 Note de grammaire

When expressing what someone thought, wanted, knew, or felt over an extended, unspecified period without a sharp beginning or end, use the imparfait.

Exemple

Durée limitée ou nombre de fois précis (Passé composé)

Actions with a specific duration or frequency count

📌 Note de grammaire

If an action happened a specific number of times ('three times') or lasted for a bounded, quantified duration ('for two hours'), use the passé composé because it frames the action with clear boundaries.

Exemple

Habitudes répétées sans limite de fin (Imparfait)

Unquantified habitual actions in the past

📌 Note de grammaire

When describing past routines, customs, or things people 'used to' do regularly without specifying an exact total count or finish date, use the imparfait.

Exemple

Réaction immédiate ou événement marquant (Passé composé)

Sudden reaction or striking event breaking the routine

📌 Note de grammaire

When a narrative breaks its calm, descriptive flow (imparfait) with a sudden, striking event or reaction that demands attention, that event takes the passé composé.

Exemple

Bilan global d'une période (Passé composé)

Overall summary or result of a past time period

📌 Note de grammaire

When looking back at a specific time period and summarizing the total outcome, success, or completed experience, use the passé composé even if it spanned a while.
