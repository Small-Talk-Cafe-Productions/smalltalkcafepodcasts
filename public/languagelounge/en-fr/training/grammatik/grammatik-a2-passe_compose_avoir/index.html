Structure générale du passé composé

General structure of the passé composé

📌 Note de grammaire

The passé composé is a compound past tense used to express completed actions in the past. It is formed with two parts: an auxiliary verb (either avoir or être) in the present tense, followed by the past participle of the main verb. The vast majority of French verbs use avoir as their auxiliary. ⚠️ Learners often forget to include the auxiliary verb and only use the past participle (e.g., Je mangé instead of J'ai mangé).

Exemple

J'ai mangé une pomme.

I ate an apple.

Verbes en -er : participe passé en -é

-er verbs: past participle in -é

📌 Note de grammaire

To form the past participle of regular verbs ending in -er, drop the -er from the infinitive and add an -é with an acute accent. This group makes up the largest class of verbs in French and always follows this exact pattern. ⚠️ Confusing the past participle (-é) with the infinitive (-er) in writing, since they sound identical in speech.

Exemple

Nous avons parlé français hier.

We spoke French yesterday.

Verbes en -ir : participe passé en -i

-ir verbs: past participle in -i

📌 Note de grammaire

For regular second-group verbs ending in -ir (like finir, choisir), the past participle is formed by dropping the -r, leaving an -i at the end. This is a reliable and consistent pattern for this entire verb family. ⚠️ Applying the -é ending to -ir verbs by mistake because -er verbs are the most commonly practiced group.

Exemple

J'ai choisi une belle robe.

I chose a nice dress.

Verbes en -re : participe passé en -u

-re verbs: past participle in -u

📌 Note de grammaire

Many regular verbs ending in -re (such as attendre, vendre, entendre) form their past participle by replacing the final -re with -u. Note that this group also contains many common irregular verbs with unique endings. ⚠️ Dropping too many letters or keeping the -re when forming the participle, such as writing attendre instead of attendu.

Exemple

Il a attendu le bus longtemps.

He waited for the bus a long time.

Participe passé irrégulier : avoir -> eu

Irregular past participle: avoir -> eu

📌 Note de grammaire

The extremely common verb avoir (to have) uses itself as the auxiliary, meaning its passé composé uses avoir + eu. The pronunciation of the past participle 'eu' sounds like the French vowel /y/. ⚠️ Trying to use être as the auxiliary for avoir, or struggling to pronounce the short 'eu' sound correctly.

Exemple

J'ai eu une bonne idée.

I had a good idea.

Participe passé irrégulier : être -> été

Irregular past participle: être -> été

📌 Note de grammaire

Although the verb être (to be) uses itself as an auxiliary in the passé composé (je suis allé), the verb être itself takes the auxiliary avoir, followed by the irregular past participle été. ⚠️ Confusing the past participle été with the summer season (l'été), or mistakenly trying to conjugate être with itself (je suis été).

Exemple

J'ai été très occupé aujourd'hui.

I was very busy today.

Participe passé irrégulier : faire -> fait

Irregular past participle: faire -> fait

📌 Note de grammaire

The verb faire (to do/make) is highly irregular. Its past participle is fait, where the final 't' is silent unless followed by a liaison. It is used constantly in everyday conversational French. ⚠️ Writing faivé or faidu by trying to apply regular verb endings to this deeply irregular high-frequency verb.

Exemple

Elle a fait ses devoirs ce soir.

She did her homework tonight.

Participe passé irrégulier : prendre -> pris

Irregular past participle: prendre -> pris

📌 Note de grammaire

Verbs ending in -endre that are derived from prendre (like comprendre, apprendre) follow the same irregular pattern, changing the ending to -is. The 's' in pris is normally silent in the masculine singular form. ⚠️ Writing prendu by assuming all -re verbs follow the regular -u participle pattern.

Exemple

Tu as pris un café ce matin.

You took a coffee this morning.

La négation au passé composé

Negation in the passé composé

📌 Note de grammaire

To make a sentence negative in the passé composé, the negative particles (ne ... pas, ne ... jamais, etc.) wrap strictly around the auxiliary verb, NOT the past participle. The structure is: subject + ne + auxiliary (avoir) + pas + past participle. ⚠️ Placing pas after the past participle instead of before it (e.g., J'ai aimé pas).

Exemple

Je n'ai pas aimé ce film.

I did not like this movie.

La position des pronoms objets

Position of object pronouns

📌 Note de grammaire

Direct and indirect object pronouns (me, te, le, la, les, lui, leur, y, en) are placed immediately BEFORE the auxiliary verb avoir in the passé composé, never between the auxiliary and the past participle. ⚠️ Placing the pronoun between the auxiliary and the past participle (e.g., J'ai le acheté).

Exemple

Je l'ai acheté hier.

I bought it yesterday.

Absence d'accord avec le sujet

No agreement with the subject

📌 Note de grammaire

When a verb uses the auxiliary avoir, the past participle does NOT agree in gender and number with the subject of the sentence. It remains in its default masculine singular form regardless of who performed the action. ⚠️ Adding an extra -e or -s to the past participle when the subject is feminine or plural (e.g., Elles ont finies).

Exemple

Marie a mangé une pomme.

Marie ate an apple.

Exception : Accord avec le COD antéposé

Exception: Agreement with a preceding direct object

📌 Note de grammaire

Although verbs with avoir usually do not show agreement, there is one major exception: if a direct object pronoun (le, la, les, que/qu') comes BEFORE the verb, the past participle must agree in gender and number with that direct object. ⚠️ Forgetting to add the feminine -e or plural -s to the participle even when a direct object pronoun clearly precedes the verb.

Exemple

La pomme ? Je l'ai mangée.

The apple? I ate it.

Règle de contraste et erreurs fréquentes: Le passé composé avec avoir

Contrast rule and common errors: Passé composé with avoir

📌 Note de grammaire

Key contrast in Passé composé with avoir: Pay special attention to distinguishing core forms (Structure générale du passé composé, Verbes en -er : participe passé en -é, Verbes en -ir : participe passé en -i, Verbes en -re : participe passé en -u) from their false friends or overlapping structures. Primary pitfall: Learners often forget to include the auxiliary verb and only use the past participle (e.g., Je mangé instead of J'ai mangé). ⚠️ Avoid the common error: Learners often forget to include the auxiliary verb and only use the past participle (e.g., Je mangé instead of J'ai mangé).

Exemple

J'ai mangé une pomme.

I ate an apple.

Ordre des mots et position syntaxique: Le passé composé avec avoir

Word order and sentence structure: Passé composé with avoir

📌 Note de grammaire

Word order and sentence structure rules for Passé composé with avoir (A2): In main clauses, subordinate clauses, and with negation or adverbial phrases, ensure Structure générale du passé composé, Verbes en -er : participe passé en -é, Verbes en -ir : participe passé en -i, Verbes en -re : participe passé en -u occupy their strict grammatical position. ⚠️ Misplacing elements relative to the conjugated verb, negation particle, or clause boundary.

Exemple

Nous avons parlé français hier.

We spoke French yesterday.

Registre formel vs usage courant: Le passé composé avec avoir

Formal register vs. colloquial spoken usage: Passé composé with avoir

📌 Note de grammaire

Register and usage nuance (A2): While everyday spoken communication frequently uses conversational shortcuts, formal written texts and CEFR A2 exams require precise adherence to Passé composé with avoir. ⚠️ Using overly colloquial shortcuts in formal written contexts or stiff bookish phrasing where natural flow is required.

Exemple

J'ai choisi une belle robe.

I chose a nice dress.

Règle de décision et synthèse pratique: Le passé composé avec avoir

Decision framework and practical summary: Passé composé with avoir

📌 Note de grammaire

Decision framework for Passé composé with avoir (A2): Before producing a sentence, verify 1) grammatical agreement (gender, number, person), 2) tense/mood triggers, and 3) exact word order. ⚠️ Producing clauses without checking case, mood triggers, or subject-verb agreement.

Exemple

Il a attendu le bus longtemps.

He waited for the bus a long time.
