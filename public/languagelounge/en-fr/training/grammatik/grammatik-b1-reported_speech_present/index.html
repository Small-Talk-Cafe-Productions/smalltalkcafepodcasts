L'introduction du discours rapporté avec 'que'

Introduction of reported statements using 'que'

📌 Note de grammaire

When reporting a statement (discours indirect), use the conjunction 'que' (or 'qu'' before a vowel) to connect the reporting clause to the reported clause. Unlike in English, the conjunction 'que' can never be omitted in French.

Exemple

L'absence de changement de temps (Présent/Futur)

No tense shift when the reporting verb is in the present

📌 Note de grammaire

When the introductory verb (e.g., il dit, elle pense, tu demandes) is in a present or future tense, the verb tense in the reported clause does not change. It remains identical to the original direct speech.

Exemple

Le rapport des questions par 'si'

Reporting yes/no questions using 'si'

📌 Note de grammaire

When reporting a direct question that requires a 'yes' or 'no' answer (est-ce que / inversion), use the conjunction 'si' (which becomes 's'' before 'il' or 'ils').

Exemple

Le rapport des questions en 'est-ce que'

Reporting questions starting with 'est-ce que'

📌 Note de grammaire

When converting a direct question introduced by 'est-ce que' into reported speech, the phrase 'est-ce que' transforms into 'si' followed by standard subject-verb word order.

Exemple

Le maintien des mots interrogatifs (où, quand, comment)

Retaining question words (where, when, how)

📌 Note de grammaire

When reporting information questions (wh- questions) starting with words like où, quand, comment, pourquoi, or combien, the interrogative word is kept in the reported clause, followed by normal subject-verb order.

Exemple

Le cas particulier de 'qu'est-ce que' (objet direct)

Reporting 'qu'est-ce que' (what as a direct object)

📌 Note de grammaire

When direct speech uses 'qu'est-ce que' (asking about an object), it changes to 'ce que' in reported speech, followed by the subject and verb.

Exemple

Le cas particulier de 'qu'est-ce qui' (sujet)

Reporting 'qu'est-ce qui' (what as a subject)

📌 Note de grammaire

When direct speech uses 'qu'est-ce qui' (asking about a subject), it changes to 'ce qui' in reported speech, maintaining the subject function before the verb.

Exemple

L'impératif rapporté avec 'de' + infinitif

Reporting imperatives using 'de' + infinitive

📌 Note de grammaire

Direct commands or requests (imperative mood) are reported using verbs of command or advice (dire de, demander de, conseiller de) followed by the preposition 'de' and an infinitive verb.

Exemple

L'ajustement des pronoms sujets et objets

Adjustment of subject and object pronouns

📌 Note de grammaire

When converting direct speech to reported speech in a present context, pronouns often need to shift to reflect the new perspective of the speaker (e.g., changing first-person 'je' to third-person 'il/elle' or second-person 'tu' depending on context).

Exemple

L'ajustement des adjectifs possessifs

Adjustment of possessive adjectives

📌 Note de grammaire

Just like pronouns, possessive adjectives (mon, ton, son, etc.) must change in reported speech to match the owner in the reported context, preventing logical contradictions.

Exemple

Les verbes introductifs courants au présent

Common introductory verbs in the present

📌 Note de grammaire

In a present context, reported speech relies heavily on everyday communicative verbs in the present tense: dire (to say), expliquer (to explain), répondre (to answer), demander (to ask), affirmer (to state), and penser (to think).

Exemple

L'ordre des mots : Sujet + Verbe dans la subordonnée

Word order: Subject + Verb in the subordinate clause

📌 Note de grammaire

In all types of indirect questions (whether introduced by si, an interrogative word, ce que, or ce qui), the word order inside the reported clause strictly follows standard declarative structure: Subject + Verb, never inversion.
