Formation générale à partir des adjectifs féminins

General formation of adverbs from feminine adjectives

📌 Note de grammaire

To form most regular French adverbs, take the feminine singular form of the adjective and add '-ment' to it. For example, 'lent' becomes 'lente' in the feminine, and 'lentement' (slowly) as an adverb.

Exemple

Adverbes en -amment et -emment

Adverbs ending in -amment and -emment

📌 Note de grammaire

Adjectives ending in '-ant' form adverbs with '-amment', while adjectives ending in '-ent' form adverbs with '-emment'. Both are pronounced identically as '-amment' (/am/).

Exemple

Adverbes irréguliers courants

Common irregular adverbs

📌 Note de grammaire

Some common adverbs do not follow the standard rules. For example, 'bon' becomes 'bien' (well), 'mauvais' becomes 'mal' (badly), and 'gentil' becomes 'gentiment' (nicely).

Exemple

Position avec les temps simples

Position with simple tenses

📌 Note de grammaire

Short and common adverbs (like 'bien', 'mal', 'trop', 'beaucoup', 'toujours') are placed immediately after the conjugated verb in simple tenses.

Exemple

Position avec les temps composés

Position with compound tenses

📌 Note de grammaire

Short or common adverbs (like 'bien', 'mal', 'trop', 'déjà', 'beaucoup') are placed between the auxiliary verb ('avoir' or 'être') and the past participle in compound tenses.

Exemple

Place des longs adverbes en -ment

Position of long adverbs in -ment

📌 Note de grammaire

Longer adverbs ending in '-ment' usually follow the past participle in compound tenses, unlike short adverbs which go between the auxiliary and the participle.

Exemple

Adverbes de temps : hier, aujourd'hui, demain

Adverbs of time: hier, aujourd'hui, demain

📌 Note de grammaire

Adverbs of time that refer to specific days usually go either at the very beginning of the sentence for emphasis or at the very end.

Exemple

Adverbes de lieu : ici, là, partout

Adverbs of place: ici, là, partout

📌 Note de grammaire

Adverbs of place like 'ici' (here), 'là' (there), and 'partout' (everywhere) generally come after the direct object or right after the verb if there is no object.

Exemple

Adverbes de quantité : trop, beaucoup, un peu

Adverbs of quantity: trop, beaucoup, un peu

📌 Note de grammaire

Adverbs of quantity modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. When modifying a noun, they take 'de' (e.g., 'beaucoup de pain'), but when modifying a verb, they follow it directly.

Exemple

Adverbes modifiant les adjectifs

Adverbs modifying adjectives

📌 Note de grammaire

Adverbs of intensity (like 'très', 'trop', 'assez', 'plus', 'moins') are placed directly before the adjective they modify.

Exemple

Négation avec les adverbes de temps (ne... jamais, ne... plus)

Negation with time adverbs (ne... jamais, ne... plus)

📌 Note de grammaire

Adverbs like 'jamais' (never) and 'plus' (no longer) replace 'pas' in the standard negative structure 'ne... pas'. They are placed where 'pas' would normally go.

Exemple

Adverbes modifiant d'autres adverbes

Adverbs modifying other adverbs

📌 Note de grammaire

An adverb can modify another adverb to increase or decrease its intensity (e.g., 'très bien', 'trop lentement'). The modifying adverb always comes first.
