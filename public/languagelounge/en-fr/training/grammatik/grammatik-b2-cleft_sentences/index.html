Cleft Sentences & Emphasis (c'est ... qui/que)

C'est ... qui (Subject Emphasis)

It is ... who/that (emphasizing the subject)

📌 Note de grammaire

To emphasize the subject of a sentence, use 'C'est [subject] qui [verb]'. The pronoun 'qui' acts as the subject of the following verb and cannot be replaced by 'que'. The verb that follows agrees in person and number with the emphasized element if it is a personal pronoun (e.g., C'est moi qui suis...).

Exemple

C'est ... que (Direct Object Emphasis)

It is ... that (emphasizing the direct object)

📌 Note de grammaire

To emphasize a direct object, use the structure 'C'est [direct object] que [subject] [verb]'. Note that 'que' contracts to qu' before a vowel. Past participles placed after the cleft structure still follow normal agreement rules with the direct object if it precedes the auxiliary.

Exemple

C'était / Ce sera ... qui/que (Past and Future Tenses)

It was / It will be ... who/that

📌 Note de grammaire

The introductory phrase 'c'est' can be conjugated into other tenses to shift the time frame of the emphasis. Use 'c'était' for the past (imperfect) and 'ce sera' or 'ce fut' for the future or literary past. The relative pronoun 'qui' or 'que' is still chosen based on the grammatical function of the emphasized element.

Exemple

Ce sont ... qui (Plural Subject Emphasis)

It is ... who (emphasizing a plural subject)

📌 Note de grammaire

When the emphasized subject is plural, 'c'est' changes to 'ce sont'. Similarly, in negative or interrogative forms, use 'ce n'est pas' or 'est-ce que c'est'. Note that in spoken French, native speakers sometimes incorrectly use 'c'est' with plural nouns, but standard written B2 French requires 'ce sont'.

Exemple

C'est ... que (Adverbial / Place / Time Emphasis)

It is ... that (emphasizing time or place)

📌 Note de grammaire

To emphasize a prepositional phrase expressing time, place, or circumstance, use 'c'est ... que'. Unlike English (which sometimes uses 'where' or 'when'), French consistently uses 'que' for these adverbial expressions within the cleft structure.

Exemple

C'est ... que (Prepositional Objects with 'de')

It is ... that (emphasizing prepositional objects with 'de')

📌 Note de grammaire

When emphasizing a noun phrase introduced by the preposition 'de' (such as an indirect object or complement), the preposition is retained right before the emphasized noun phrase, followed by 'que'.

Exemple

C'est ... que (Cause and Explanation Emphasis)

It is ... because/that (emphasizing reason)

📌 Note de grammaire

To emphasize a reason introduced by 'parce que' or 'à cause de', you extract the cause and place it inside the 'c'est ... que' frame. The conjunction 'parce que' changes to 'que' within the cleft structure.

Exemple

C'est ... que (Emphasizing Pronouns)

It is ... who/that (emphasizing disjunctive pronouns)

📌 Note de grammaire

When emphasizing a personal pronoun (moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles), the pronoun takes its disjunctive form immediately after 'c'est', followed by 'qui' if it's the subject, or 'que' if it's the object. The verb following 'qui' matches the person of the pronoun.

Exemple

Ce qui / Ce que (Subject/Object Pseudo-Clefts)

What (introducing a pseudo-cleft sentence for emphasis)

📌 Note de grammaire

Pseudo-cleft sentences use 'ce qui' (subject) or 'ce que' (direct object) at the beginning of the sentence to focus attention on information placed at the end, often summarized with 'c'est'. 'Ce qui' takes a subject role in the first clause, while 'ce que' takes a direct object role.

Exemple

C'est ... que (Negative Cleft Sentences)

It is not ... that (negative emphasis)

📌 Note de grammaire

To negate a cleft sentence, apply the negation 'ne... pas' to the verb 'être' (making it 'ce n'est pas' or 'ce ne sont pas'). This structure is extremely common in arguments and clarifications to correct a misconception.

Exemple

C'est ... que (Interrogative Cleft Sentences)

Is it ... that ... ? (asking with cleft structures)

📌 Note de grammaire

Cleft sentences can be turned into questions using standard interrogation patterns ('Est-ce que', inversion, or intonation). They are used when asking for specific confirmation regarding who, what, where, or when an action occurred.

Exemple

C'est vrai que / C'est sûr que (Introductory Emphasis Expressions)

It's true that / It's certain that (conversational emphasis)

📌 Note de grammaire

At the B2 level, conversational emphasis often relies on fixed expressions like 'c'est vrai que', 'c'est sûr que', or 'c'est évident que' followed by a standard clause. These act as soft cleft structures to validate a point before adding nuance or agreement.
