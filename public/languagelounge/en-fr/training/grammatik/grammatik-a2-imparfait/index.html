Formation de base de l'imparfait

Basic formation of the imparfait using the 'nous' stem

📌 Note de grammaire

To form the imparfait, take the present tense 'nous' form of the verb, drop the '-ons' ending, and add the imparfait endings: -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient. This applies to almost all regular and irregular verbs.

Exemple

Les terminaisons de l'imparfait

The six endings of the imparfait

📌 Note de grammaire

The imparfait uses the exact same endings for all verbs (regular and irregular): -ais (je), -ais (tu), -ait (il/elle/on), -ions (nous), -iez (vous), -aient (ils/elles). Note that -ais, -ais, -ait, and -aient are all pronounced identically.

Exemple

L'exception du verbe être

The irregular verb 'être' (to be)

📌 Note de grammaire

'Être' is the only verb in French that does not use the 'nous' present stem for the imparfait. Instead, its stem is 'ét-', to which the standard imparfait endings are attached: ét-ais, ét-ais, ét-ait, ét-ions, ét-iez, ét-aient.

Exemple

Les verbes en -ger

Spelling changes in verbs ending in -ger

📌 Note de grammaire

For verbs ending in -ger (like manger, nager, voyager), a silent 'e' must be inserted between the stem and the endings -ais, -ais, -ait, and -aient. This keeps the 'g' soft (sounding like the 's' in 'pleasure'), rather than hard like a 'g' in 'game'.

Exemple

Les verbes en -cer

Spelling changes in verbs ending in -cer

📌 Note de grammaire

For verbs ending in -cer (like commencer, placer, lancer), add a cedilla (ç) to the 'c' before endings that start with 'a' (-ais, -ais, -ait, -aient). This preserves the soft 's' sound instead of a hard 'k' sound.

Exemple

Les verbes en -ier, -yer, -uier

Verbs with double 'i' for nous and vous

📌 Note de grammaire

Verbs whose stems end in 'i' (like étudier, rire, crier) naturally result in a double 'i' in the 'nous' and 'vous' forms of the imparfait (-iians, -iiez). Do not drop one of the i's; both belong to the grammar pattern.

Exemple

Habitudes et actions répétées dans le passé

Expressing past habits and repeated actions

📌 Note de grammaire

The imparfait is used to describe past habits, routines, or things you 'used to' do regularly. It paints a picture of ongoing or habitual lifestyle patterns without specifying a strict beginning or end.

Exemple

Descriptions physiques et psychologiques

Describing past states, feelings, and conditions

📌 Note de grammaire

Use the imparfait for background descriptions in the past, including weather, age, feelings, physical appearance, and mental states (e.g., wanting, knowing, thinking).

Exemple

Contexte et action en cours (Imparfait + Passé Composé)

Setting the scene while a sudden action happens

📌 Note de grammaire

A very common pattern uses the imparfait for the background scene (ongoing state or action) interrupted by the passé composé (a sudden, specific action).

Exemple

Expressions temporelles typiques

Common time expressions used with the imparfait

📌 Note de grammaire

Certain phrases naturally trigger the imparfait because they signal habit or background timeframes: 'autrefois' (formerly), 'd'habitude' (usually), 'souvent' (often), 'toujours' (always), 'le lundi' (on Mondays / every Monday), and 'quand j'étais petit' (when I was little).

Exemple

Verbes de sentiment et d'état mental

Mental state verbs ('vouloir', 'savoir', 'pouvoir', 'aimer')

📌 Note de grammaire

Verbs expressing mental states, wishes, knowledge, or opinions (vouloir, pouvoir, savoir, croire, penser, aimer) are almost always in the imparfait in past narratives because they describe ongoing internal conditions rather than completed events.

Exemple

Comparaison avec le Passé Composé

Contrast between Imparfait (background) and Passé Composé (foreground)

📌 Note de grammaire

Think of the imparfait as the background canvas of a painting (ongoing, habitual, descriptive) and the passé composé as the specific events painted on top of the canvas (completed, sudden, sequential actions).
