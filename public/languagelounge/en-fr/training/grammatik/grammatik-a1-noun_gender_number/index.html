Masculine Nouns and Definite Article (le)

Identifying and using masculine singular nouns with 'le'.

📌 Note de grammaire

In French, all nouns have a gender. Masculine singular nouns take the definite article 'le' (or 'l'' before a vowel). Learning the article with the noun is essential since English does not grammaticalize noun gender.

Exemple

Feminine Nouns and Definite Article (la)

Identifying and using feminine singular nouns with 'la'.

📌 Note de grammaire

Feminine singular nouns take the definite article 'la' (which contracts to 'l'' before a vowel or mute h). Many nouns referring to female beings or things ending in specific suffixes (like -tion, -té) are feminine.

Exemple

Indefinite Articles (un / une)

Using 'un' for masculine and 'une' for feminine 'a/an'.

📌 Note de grammaire

To introduce an unspecified singular noun, use 'un' for masculine and 'une' for feminine. These correspond to 'a' or 'an' in English and carry the gender of the noun they precede.

Exemple

Standard Plural Formation (-s)

Forming the plural of French nouns by adding an '-s'.

📌 Note de grammaire

The vast majority of French nouns form their plural by adding an unpronounced '-s' to the singular form. The article changes too: 'le/la/l'' becomes 'les', and 'un/une' becomes 'des'.

Exemple

Nouns Ending in -s, -x, -z in the Plural

Nouns that do not change their spelling in the plural.

📌 Note de grammaire

Nouns that already end in '-s', '-x', or '-z' in their singular form remain completely unchanged in the plural. Only the preceding article shows that the noun is plural.

Exemple

Plural of Nouns Ending in -eau

Adding '-x' to singular nouns ending in '-eau'.

📌 Note de grammaire

Nouns ending in the diphthong '-eau' form their plural by adding '-x' instead of '-s' (e.g., un bateau -> des bateaux). The pronunciation remains identical to the singular form.

Exemple

Plural of Nouns Ending in -al

Changing '-al' to '-aux' in the plural.

📌 Note de grammaire

Most masculine nouns ending in '-al' drop the '-l' and add '-aux' to form the plural (e.g., un animal -> des animaux). Note: a few common exceptions like 'le bal' just take an '-s'.

Exemple

Feminine Forms of Professions (-e)

Adding '-e' to masculine nouns to indicate female roles.

📌 Note de grammaire

For many nouns indicating professions or nationalities, the feminine form is created by adding an '-e' to the masculine singular. If the masculine already ends in '-e', the spelling does not change.

Exemple

Partitive Articles (du / de la)

Expressing an unspecified quantity of an uncountable noun.

📌 Note de grammaire

When talking about mass nouns or food items in A1, use 'du' for masculine, 'de la' for feminine, and 'de l'' before vowels. These combine the preposition 'de' with the definite article.

Exemple

Elision with Definite Articles (l')

Dropping the vowel of 'le' or 'la' before a vowel or mute h.

📌 Note de grammaire

The definite articles 'le' and 'la' drop their terminal vowel and add an apostrophe (l') when they precede a noun starting with a vowel sound or a mute 'h' to avoid two consecutive vowel sounds.

Exemple

Plural Article Contraction: de + les = des

Merging preposition 'de' with plural definite article 'les'.

📌 Note de grammaire

In French, when the preposition 'de' is followed by the plural definite article 'les', they contract into 'des'. This 'des' functions as an indefinite plural article or 'some/of the'.

Exemple

Plural Article Contraction: à + les = aux

Merging preposition 'à' with plural definite article 'les'.

📌 Note de grammaire

When the preposition 'à' (to/at) is followed by the plural definite article 'les', they combine into 'aux'. This is mandatory in French grammar.
