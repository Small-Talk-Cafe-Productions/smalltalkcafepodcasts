Formation générale du conditionnel présent

General formation of the conditional present

📌 Note de grammaire

To form the conditionnel présent, take the full infinitive verb (for -re verbs, drop the final 'e') and add the imperfect endings: -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient. The stem is identical to the future simple stem.

Exemple

Radicaux irréguliers au conditionnel

Irregular stems in the conditional

📌 Note de grammaire

Verbs with irregular future stems use the exact same irregular stem for the conditional, followed by the standard -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient endings. Examples include avoir (aur-), être (ser-), aller (ir-), and faire (fer-).

Exemple

Exprimer un souhait ou un désir

Expressing a wish or a desire

📌 Note de grammaire

The conditionnel présent is frequently used with verbs like vouloir (voudrais), aimerais, or préférerais to express polite desires, soften requests, or state wishes without sounding too demanding.

Exemple

Demander poliment avec 'pourriez' et 'voudriez'

Making polite requests with 'could' and 'would'

📌 Note de grammaire

When asking someone for a favor or information, using 'pourriez-vous' (could you) or 'voudriez-vous' (would you) is the standard polite register in French, equivalent to English modal constructions.

Exemple

Donner des conseils avec 'tu devrais'

Giving advice with 'you should'

📌 Note de grammaire

To offer advice or suggestions, French speakers use the verb devoir in the conditional (tu devrais / vous devriez), directly translating to 'you should' in English.

Exemple

Propositions conditionnelles : Si + imparfait, conditionnel

Conditional clauses: If + imperfect, conditional

📌 Note de grammaire

To talk about hypothetical situations in the present or future, use 'si' + imperfect in the conditional clause, followed by the conditionnel présent in the main clause.

Exemple

Exprimer une information incertaine ou un rumor

Expressing uncertain information or a rumor

📌 Note de grammaire

Journalists and speakers use the conditionnel présent to report unverified news, rumors, or allegations when they cannot guarantee the absolute truth of the statement.

Exemple

Atténuer une opinion ou un reproche

Softening an opinion or a criticism

📌 Note de grammaire

To express a critique or a personal opinion in a polite, less confrontational way, French speakers use verbs like penser, trouver, or aimer in the conditional.

Exemple

Le verbe 'falloir' au conditionnel

The impersonal verb 'falloir' in the conditional

📌 Note de grammaire

The impersonal verb 'falloir' (to be necessary) becomes 'il faudrait' in the conditional, translating to 'it would be necessary' or 'one should'.

Exemple

Le verbe 'pouvoir' pour exprimer une possibilité

The verb 'pouvoir' to express possibility

📌 Note de grammaire

Using 'pourrait' or 'pourraient' allows speakers to suggest a possibility or potential scenario without stating it as a definitive fact.

Exemple

Le conditionnel avec 'aimer autant' ou 'aimer mieux'

The conditional with preferences ('would just as soon')

📌 Note de grammaire

Expressions like 'j'aimerais autant' or 'j'aimerais mieux' are used to express a strong personal preference in a polite or nuanced manner.

Exemple

Les verbes en -eler et -eter au conditionnel

Verbs ending in -eler and -eter in the conditional

📌 Note de grammaire

Verbs like appeler and jeter double their consonant (l/t) before a mute 'e'. Since the conditional stem uses a mute 'e' before the endings, this spelling change applies to the conditionnel présent.
