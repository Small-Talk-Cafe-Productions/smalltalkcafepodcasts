commencer à

to start to / to begin to

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Requires the preposition 'à' before an infinitive.

Exemple

Je commence à manger.

I am starting to eat.

continuer à

to continue to

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Requires the preposition 'à' before an infinitive.

Exemple

Il continue à travailler.

He continues to work.

penser à

to think about

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Uses preposition 'à' to introduce objects/topics.

Exemple

Je pense à toi.

I am thinking about you.

arriver à

to manage to / to succeed in

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary être when meaning to arrive, but here takes avoir for figurative success, or être. Standard auxiliary is être.

Exemple

J'arrive à comprendre.

I manage to understand.

apprendre à

to learn to

📌 Note de grammaire

Third group irregular verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Followed by 'à' before an infinitive.

Exemple

J'apprends à parler.

I am learning to speak.

aider à

to help to

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Connects with 'à' before a following action.

Exemple

J'aide à cuisiner.

I help to cook.

chercher à

to try to / to seek to

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Takes 'à' before an infinitive.

Exemple

Je cherche à comprendre.

I am trying to understand.

réussir à

to succeed in

📌 Note de grammaire

Second group (-ir) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Uses 'à' before infinitives.

Exemple

Je réussis à passer.

I succeed in passing.

jouer à

to play (a sport/game)

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Followed by 'à' + definite article for games/sports.

Exemple

Je joue au football.

I play soccer.

s'intéresser à

to be interested in

📌 Note de grammaire

Reflexive first group verb, takes auxiliary être. Followed by 'à'.

Exemple

Je m'intéresse à l'art.

I am interested in art.

décider de

to decide to

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Uses preposition 'de' before an infinitive.

Exemple

Je décide de partir.

I decide to leave.

finir de

to finish doing / to finish

📌 Note de grammaire

Second group (-ir) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Takes 'de' before an infinitive.

Exemple

Je finis de manger.

I finish eating.

essayer de

to try to

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Requires 'de' before an infinitive.

Exemple

J'essaie de dormir.

I try to sleep.

oublier de

to forget to

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Uses preposition 'de' before an infinitive.

Exemple

J'oublie de fermer.

I forget to close.

refuser de

to refuse to

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Uses preposition 'de' before an infinitive.

Exemple

Je refuse de manger.

I refuse to eat.

rêver de

to dream about / to dream of

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Followed by 'de'.

Exemple

Je rêve de voyager.

I dream of traveling.

parler de

to talk about

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes auxiliary avoir. Uses 'de' to introduce the topic.

Exemple

Je parle de toi.

I am talking about you.

avoir besoin de

to need

📌 Note de grammaire

Idiomatic expression with 'avoir' (third group auxiliary). Uses 'de' before nouns or infinitives.

Exemple

J'ai besoin d'eau.

I need water.

avoir peur de

to be afraid of

📌 Note de grammaire

Idiomatic expression with 'avoir' (third group auxiliary). Uses 'de' before nouns or verbs.

Exemple

J'ai peur des chiens.

I am afraid of dogs.

avoir envie de

to feel like / to want

📌 Note de grammaire

Idiomatic expression with 'avoir' (third group auxiliary). Uses 'de' before nouns or verbs.

Exemple

J'ai envie de dormir.

I feel like sleeping.
