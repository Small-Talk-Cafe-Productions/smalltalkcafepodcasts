investir

to invest

📌 Note de grammaire

Second group (-ir), auxiliary 'avoir'. Regular conjugation model for verbs like finir.

Exemple

Il est conseillé d'investir dans des projets durables pour l'avenir.

It is advised to invest in sustainable projects for the future.

emprunter

to borrow

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Takes a direct object or preposition 'à'.

Exemple

Beaucoup de jeunes ménages empruntent pour acheter leur premier logement.

Many young households borrow to buy their first home.

prêter

to lend

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Often used with 'à' for the recipient.

Exemple

La banque refuse de prêter de l'argent sans garanties solides.

The bank refuses to lend money without solid guarantees.

gérer

to manage / to run

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) with a spelling change (è instead of é before a mute e), auxiliary 'avoir'.

Exemple

Elle gère un portefeuille d'actifs de plusieurs millions d'euros.

She manages an asset portfolio worth several million euros.

épargner

to save (money)

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Reflexive form 's'épargner' means to spare oneself.

Exemple

Les ménages français ont tendance à épargner davantage en cas d'incertitude.

French households tend to save more in case of uncertainty.

rentabiliser

to make profitable / to pay for itself

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Often used in corporate strategy contexts.

Exemple

Cette nouvelle machine permettra de rentabiliser la production plus rapidement.

This new machine will make production profitable more quickly.

bénéficier

to benefit / to profit

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Takes the preposition 'de' (bénéficier de).

Exemple

Les petites entreprises peuvent bénéficier d'aides financières exceptionnelles.

Small businesses can benefit from exceptional financial aid.

financer

to finance / to fund

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) with a cedilla (ç) before 'a' and 'o' to maintain soft c sound, auxiliary 'avoir'.

Exemple

La banque a refusé de financer leur projet de développement.

The bank refused to fund their development project.

subventionner

to subsidize

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Common in public economics and state aid contexts.

Exemple

L'État cherche à subventionner la transition écologique des entreprises.

The state seeks to subsidize the ecological transition of businesses.

fluctuer

to fluctuate

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir' (sometimes 'être' in literary contexts, but 'avoir' is standard for business).

Exemple

Les prix du pétrole ne cessent de fluctuer sur les marchés mondiaux.

Oil prices keep fluctuating on world markets.

thésauriser

to hoard / to stash away money

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Refers to keeping money out of circulation.

Exemple

En temps de crise, certains particuliers préfèrent thésauriser plutôt que consommer.

In times of crisis, some individuals prefer to hoard money rather than consume.

déprécier

to depreciate / to lose value

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er). Can be used transitively ('déprécier une monnaie') or pronominally ('se déprécier'). Auxiliary 'avoir'.

Exemple

La monnaie locale s'est dépréciée face au dollar au cours du trimestre.

The local currency depreciated against the dollar during the quarter.

taxer

to tax

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Frequent in fiscal policy and taxation discussions.

Exemple

Le gouvernement envisage de taxer davantage les transactions financières.

The government is considering taxing financial transactions more heavily.

solvabiliser

to make solvent / to provide purchasing power to

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Specialized economic term referring to aiding consumers/borrowers.

Exemple

Des aides publiques visent à solvabiliser la demande sur le marché immobilier.

Public aid aims to make demand solvent in the real estate market.

externaliser

to outsource

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Common in corporate business models.

Exemple

Pour réduire leurs coûts, plusieurs grandes entreprises choisissent d'externaliser leur service client.

To reduce their costs, several large companies choose to outsource their customer service.

spéculer

to speculate

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Often used with 'sur' (spéculer sur).

Exemple

Certains investisseurs n'hésitent pas à spéculer sur la baisse des marchés.

Some investors do not hesitate to speculate on the decline of the markets.

redresser

to turn around / to recover (an economy or company)

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Often used reflexively 'se redresser' for recovery.

Exemple

Le nouveau directeur général a réussi à redresser les comptes de la société.

The new CEO successfully turned around the company's accounts.

comptabiliser

to record / to log / to account for

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Standard bookkeeping term.

Exemple

Le service financier doit comptabiliser toutes les factures avant la fin du mois.

The finance department must record all invoices before the end of the month.

croître

to grow / to increase

📌 Note de grammaire

Third group irregular (-re with circumflex on î in certain forms), auxiliary 'avoir' or 'être' (usually 'avoir' for economic growth, 'être' for physical growth). Past participle: croît / crû.

Exemple

Le chiffre d'affaires de l'entreprise a su croître malgré un marché difficile.

The company's turnover managed to grow despite a difficult market.

restructurer

to restructure

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er), auxiliary 'avoir'. Often used in corporate restructuring contexts.

Exemple

Le groupe a dû restructurer sa dette pour éviter la faillite.

The group had to restructure its debt to avoid bankruptcy.
