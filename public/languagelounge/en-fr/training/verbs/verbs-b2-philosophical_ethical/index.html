douter

to doubt

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Often followed by 'de' before a noun or 'que' with the subjunctive.

Exemple

Il est normal de douter de nos propres convictions face à la complexité du monde.

It is normal to doubt our own convictions when facing the complexity of the world.

réfléchir

to reflect / to think deeply

📌 Note de grammaire

Second group (-ir) verb, takes avoir. Uses the preposition 'à' before an object.

Exemple

Nous devons réfléchir aux conséquences morales de nos choix technologiques.

We must reflect on the moral consequences of our technological choices.

prétendre

to claim / to assert

📌 Note de grammaire

Third group (-re) verb, takes avoir. Followed directly by an infinitive or 'que' for assertions.

Exemple

Il ne faut pas prétendre détenir la vérité absolue dans un débat éthique.

One must not claim to hold absolute truth in an ethical debate.

supposer

to suppose / to assume

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Often introduces a hypothesis with 'que'.

Exemple

On peut supposer que la justice sociale est le fondement d'une société stable.

One can assume that social justice is the foundation of a stable society.

justifier

to justify

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Used when examining the moral validity of an action.

Exemple

La fin peut-elle réellement justifier les moyens dans une perspective morale ?

Can the end really justify the means from a moral perspective?

condamner

to condemn

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Frequently used in ethical and legal contexts.

Exemple

La conscience collective condamne fermement toute forme de discrimination.

The collective conscience firmly condemns any form of discrimination.

admettre

to admit / to acknowledge

📌 Note de grammaire

Third group irregular verb, conjugated like 'mettre', takes avoir. Takes subjunctive or indicative after 'que'.

Exemple

Il est difficile d'admettre que nos propres croyances puissent être erronées.

It is difficult to admit that our own beliefs might be erroneous.

rejeter

to reject

📌 Note de grammaire

First group verb with stem change (è instead of é in most forms except nous/vous), takes avoir.

Exemple

Il ne faut pas rejeter d'emblée les idées qui bousculent nos certitudes.

One must not immediately reject ideas that shake our certainties.

valoir

to be worth

📌 Note de grammaire

Third group irregular verb, takes avoir. Often used impersonally ('il vaut mieux').

Exemple

Il vaut mieux méditer longuement avant de porter un jugement moral.

It is better to meditate for a long time before making a moral judgment.

promouvoir

to promote

📌 Note de grammaire

Third group verb conjugated like 'mouvoir', takes avoir. Used for ideals and values.

Exemple

Les institutions internationales doivent promouvoir l'égalité et la dignité humaine.

International institutions must promote equality and human dignity.

transgresser

to transgress / to violate

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Used when breaking moral or social rules.

Exemple

Le rebelle cherche constamment à transgresser les interdits imposés par la tradition.

The rebel constantly seeks to transgress the prohibitions imposed by tradition.

redouter

to dread / to fear

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Expresses intellectual or existential fear.

Exemple

Beaucoup de penseurs redoutent les dérives autoritaires de l'intelligence artificielle.

Many thinkers dread the authoritarian drifts of artificial intelligence.

dissuader

to dissuade

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Takes 'de' before an infinitive ('dissuader quelqu'un de faire').

Exemple

L'éthique de la discussion vise à persuader plutôt qu'à dissuader par la force.

The ethics of discussion aims to persuade rather than dissuade by force.

soutenir

to support / to maintain (an argument)

📌 Note de grammaire

Third group irregular verb, conjugated like 'tenir', takes avoir. Used for defending a thesis or position.

Exemple

Il est difficile de soutenir une telle théorie sans preuves scientifiques solides.

It is difficult to maintain such a theory without solid scientific evidence.

interroger

to question / to interrogate

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Essential for philosophical inquiry and critical thinking.

Exemple

La philosophie commence dès que l'on commence à interroger l'évidence du quotidien.

Philosophy begins as soon as one starts to question the obviousness of everyday life.

nuancer

to nuance / to qualify (a statement)

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb with cedilla (ç) before 'a' and 'o' to keep soft /s/ sound, takes avoir.

Exemple

Il est indispensable de nuancer ce jugement trop hâtif sur la nature humaine.

It is essential to qualify this overly hasty judgment on human nature.

façonner

to shape / to forge

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Used metaphorically for minds, values, and cultures.

Exemple

Nos expériences de vie et nos lectures façonnent notre vision du monde.

Our life experiences and our readings shape our worldview.

aliéner

to alienate

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Frequently used in sociological and philosophical critiques.

Exemple

Certaines formes de travail moderne peuvent aliéner l'esprit des individus.

Certain forms of modern work can alienate the minds of individuals.

toiser

to look down on / to measure up

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Can be used literally or metaphorically regarding intellectual arrogance.

Exemple

Il ne faut jamais toiser autrui avec mépris sous prétexte qu'on a raison.

One must never look down on others with contempt under the pretext of being right.

primer

to take precedence / to override

📌 Note de grammaire

First group (-er) verb, takes avoir. Used when one ethical or legal principle supersedes another.

Exemple

Dans un État de droit, les libertés fondamentales doivent toujours primer sur la sécurité.

In a state governed by the rule of law, fundamental freedoms must always take precedence over security.
