Het voltooid deelwoord (Basis)

The Past Participle (Basics)

📌 Grammaticanotitie

In Dutch, the past participle (voltooid deelwoord) is used with an auxiliary verb (hebben or zijn) to form the perfect tense. For weak verbs, the general formula is: ge- + stem + -t or -d. The stem is the infinitive minus the '-en' ending (e.g., 'wandelen' -> stem 'wandel').

Voorbeeld

't Kofschip-x (De T-regel)

The 't Kofschip-x Rule (The T-rule)

📌 Grammaticanotitie

To decide if a weak verb ends in -t or -d, look at the last letter of the stem (Infinitive minus -en). If the stem ends in T, K, F, S, CH, or P (found in 't kofschip), the participle ends in -t. The letter 'x' also follows this rule.

Voorbeeld

De D-regel (Zwakke werkwoorden)

The D-rule (Weak verbs)

📌 Grammaticanotitie

If the last letter of the verb stem is NOT in 't kofschip (T, K, F, S, CH, P), the past participle ends in -d. This applies to stems ending in vowels or consonants like L, M, N, R, B, G, V, Z.

Voorbeeld

Spelling: De V en de Z

Spelling: The V and the Z

📌 Grammaticanotitie

When determining the ending (-t or -d), always look at the stem of the infinitive. Verbs like 'reizen' (to travel) or 'leven' (to live) have stems ending in Z and V. Even though the 'ik-form' changes to S or F (ik reis, ik leef), the rule uses the original Z/V. Therefore, they end in -d.

Voorbeeld

Onscheidbare voorvoegsels

Inseparable Prefixes

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Verbs starting with be-, ge-, her-, ont-, or ver- do NOT get an extra 'ge-' in the past participle. You simply take the stem and add -t or -d based on 't kofschip.

Voorbeeld

Scheidbare werkwoorden

Separable Verbs

📌 Grammaticanotitie

For separable verbs (like 'opbellen'), the 'ge-' prefix is placed between the separable part and the main verb: [prefix] + ge + [stem] + [t/d/en].

Voorbeeld

Sterke werkwoorden (Introductie)

Strong Verbs (Introduction)

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Strong verbs are irregular. They often change their vowel in the past participle and almost always end in -en. The formula is: ge- + (changed) stem + -en.

Voorbeeld

Sterke werkwoorden: ij -> ie

Strong Verbs: ij -> ie

📌 Grammaticanotitie

A very common pattern for strong verbs is the shift from 'ij' in the infinitive to 'ie' in the past participle. These verbs always end in -en.

Voorbeeld

Sterke werkwoorden: ie -> o

Strong Verbs: ie -> o

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Another common pattern for strong verbs is the shift from 'ie' (or sometimes 'ij') in the infinitive to 'o' in the past participle.

Voorbeeld

Sterke werkwoorden: e -> o

Strong Verbs: e -> o

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Many strong verbs with an 'e' in the stem change to an 'o' in the past participle. These often involve verbs of breaking, speaking, or stealing.

Voorbeeld

Sterke werkwoorden: Geen klinkerwissel

Strong Verbs: No Vowel Change

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Some strong verbs do not change their vowel at all in the past participle, but they still end in -en (unlike weak verbs).

Voorbeeld

Onregelmatige & Gemengde werkwoorden

Irregular & Mixed Verbs

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Some verbs are 'mixed': they have a vowel change (strong) but end in -t or -d (weak). Common examples include 'kopen' (gekocht), 'zoeken' (gezocht), and 'brengen' (gebracht).
