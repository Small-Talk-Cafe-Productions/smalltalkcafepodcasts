Er wordt + past participle (Impersonal Passive)

Intransitive passive with 'er' for actions without a direct object

📌 Grammaticanotitie

When an intransitive verb (a verb that cannot take a direct object, such as 'lachen', 'werken', 'dansen') is used in the passive voice, Dutch uses the dummy subject 'er' combined with 'wordt' and the past participle. This construction is strictly impersonal and does not change for plural subjects because there is no grammatical subject.

Voorbeeld

Er wordt + past participle in subordinate clauses

Position of 'er wordt' in dependent clauses

📌 Grammaticanotitie

In subordinate clauses, verbs move to the end. For the impersonal passive, 'er' remains near the beginning of the clause (often right after the conjunction), followed by the past participle and finally 'wordt', observing the standard verbal cluster rule (participle before auxiliary in subordinate clauses).

Voorbeeld

Impersonal passive with prepositional phrases

Passives derived from verbs taking prepositional objects

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Verbs that require a specific preposition (e.g., 'wachten op', 'reageren op', 'denken aan') can also form an impersonal passive with 'er'. The preposition remains intact immediately following 'er' or further in the sentence before the agent.

Voorbeeld

Zijn + past participle (Stative Passive / Toestandspassief)

Expressing a resulting state rather than an ongoing action

📌 Grammaticanotitie

While 'worden' indicates an action (process passive), 'zijn' combined with a past participle expresses a completed state or condition resulting from a previous action. This is called the stative passive ('toestandspassief'). It functions much like an adjective.

Voorbeeld

Stative Passive in past tenses (Was/Waren + past participle)

Expressing past states resulting from prior actions

📌 Grammaticanotitie

To express a stative condition in the past, use the past tense of 'zijn' ('was' / 'waren') plus the past participle. This highlights that a certain state existed at a specific moment in the past, distinct from the past dynamic passive ('werd / werden').

Voorbeeld

Distinguishing Worden (Action) vs. Zijn (State)

Semantic contrast between process and stative passives

📌 Grammaticanotitie

The choice between 'worden' and 'zijn' changes the meaning completely. 'Het boek wordt gedrukt' means the printing process is currently underway. 'Het boek is gedrukt' means the printing is finished and the book exists in a printed state.

Voorbeeld

Agent expression with 'door' vs. 'van'

Indicating who performs the action in passive constructions

📌 Grammaticanotitie

In Dutch passives, the agent (the doer of the action) is typically introduced by the preposition 'door' (by). However, when the action involves emotions, mental states, or specific verbs like 'houden van', 'bekend zijn', or non-agentive causes, other prepositions like 'van' may be encountered.

Voorbeeld

Passive with modal verbs (Modal + participle + worden)

Combining modal verbs with the passive voice

📌 Grammaticanotitie

When expressing necessity, possibility, or permission in the passive voice, use a modal verb ('moeten', 'kunnen', 'mogen', 'willen') in conjugated form, place the past participle near the end, and end the sentence with the infinitive 'worden'.

Voorbeeld

Passive in the present perfect (Is + participle + zijn)

Forming the passive voice in compound past tenses

📌 Grammaticanotitie

The compound past tense of the dynamic passive uses the auxiliary verb 'zijn' (not 'hebben') combined with the past participle of the main verb and ending with the special passive participle 'geweest'. Structure: [subject] + is/zijn + [past participle] + geweest.

Voorbeeld

Er wordt + passive with indefinite or omitted subjects

Generalizing actions and omitting the specific actor using 'er'

📌 Grammaticanotitie

In professional and journalistic Dutch, 'er wordt' is frequently used to report general actions, trends, or institutional procedures without pointing fingers at specific individuals, similar to English 'it is said' or impersonal 'they'.

Voorbeeld

Advanced Passive with indirect objects (Dativpassiv)

Converting indirect objects into subjects of passive sentences

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Unlike English, Dutch allows certain verbs with an indirect object (dative) to become the subject of a passive sentence. Verbs like 'geven', 'vertellen', 'beloven' permit the person receiving something to become the subject of the passive construction.

Voorbeeld

Passive infinitive constructions (te + worden + participle)

Passive voice inside infinitive clauses with 'te'

📌 Grammaticanotitie

When a passive clause functions as an infinitive phrase governed by another verb or adjective (e.g., 'schijnen te', 'hoeven te', 'beloven te'), the construction requires the past participle followed immediately by 'te worden'.
