Ja/nee-vragen (Inversie)

Yes/No Questions (Inversion)

📌 Grammaticanotitie

To form a basic yes/no question in Dutch, you use 'inversion'. This means you swap the position of the subject and the conjugated verb. The verb moves to the first position, and the subject follows immediately. ⚠️ Learners often say 'Je werkt vandaag?' with rising intonation. While understood, the correct grammatical way is to swap the verb and subject: 'Werk je vandaag?'

Voorbeeld

Werk je vandaag?

Are you working today?

Wie (Who)

Who

📌 Grammaticanotitie

'Wie' is used to ask about the identity of a person or people. It functions as the subject or object of the sentence. In a question, 'Wie' usually comes first, followed by the verb. ⚠️ Confusing 'Wie' (Who) with the English 'Who' is easy, but don't confuse it with 'Wie' in German (which means How). In Dutch, 'Wie' is always 'Who'.

Voorbeeld

Wie is dat?

Who is that?

Wat (What)

What

📌 Grammaticanotitie

'Wat' is used to ask about things, objects, or actions. It is placed at the beginning of the sentence, followed by the verb and then the subject. ⚠️ Using 'Wat' to ask 'Which one'. For specific choices, Dutch uses 'Welke' instead of 'Wat'.

Voorbeeld

Wat eet je?

What are you eating?

Waar (Where)

Where

📌 Grammaticanotitie

'Waar' asks about a location or position. Like other question words, it triggers inversion: Question Word + Verb + Subject. ⚠️ Forgetting the 's' in 'foto's' or using 'Waar' when you mean 'Where to' (which often requires 'heen' at the end).

Voorbeeld

Waar woon je?

Where do you live?

Wanneer (When)

When

📌 Grammaticanotitie

'Wanneer' is used to ask about time, days, or dates. It follows the standard question word word-order: Wanneer + Verb + Subject. ⚠️ Using 'Wanneer' to mean 'If'. In Dutch, 'Wanneer' is only for time questions; 'Als' is used for 'if/when' in conditional statements.

Voorbeeld

Wanneer kom je?

When are you coming?

Waarom (Why)

Why

📌 Grammaticanotitie

'Waarom' asks for a reason or cause. The answer usually begins with 'Omdat' (Because), though at A1 level, short direct answers are common. ⚠️ Misspelling 'Waarom' as 'Waroem'. Remember it ends in '-om'.

Voorbeeld

Waarom lach je?

Why are you laughing?

Hoe (How)

How

📌 Grammaticanotitie

'Hoe' asks about the manner, method, or state of something. It is also used in common greetings. ⚠️ Using 'Hoe' for 'What' in 'What is your name?'. In Dutch, we say 'Hoe heet je?' (How are you called?) rather than 'Wat is je naam?' (though both are possible, 'Hoe' is more common).

Voorbeeld

Hoe gaat het?

How are things? / How is it going?

Welke / Welk (Which)

Which

📌 Grammaticanotitie

'Welke' is used for 'de-words' and all plurals. 'Welk' is used for 'het-words' in the singular. They are used to ask for a specific choice among a group. ⚠️ Using 'Welke' for everything. Remember: 'Welk' for 'het' words (Welk huis?), 'Welke' for 'de' words (Welke auto?).

Voorbeeld

Welke krant lees je?

Which newspaper do you read?

Hoeveel (How much/many)

How much / How many

📌 Grammaticanotitie

'Hoeveel' is used for both countable and uncountable quantities. It is followed by the noun being counted, then the verb, then the subject. ⚠️ Trying to use two different words for 'how much' and 'how many'. Dutch uses 'Hoeveel' for both.

Voorbeeld

Hoeveel kost dat?

How much does that cost?

Inversie met 'jij' (The -t drop)

Inversion with 'you' (jij/je)

📌 Grammaticanotitie

This is a crucial rule: When the subject 'jij' or 'je' (you) follows the verb in a question, the verb loses its '-t' ending. This only happens with 'jij/je' in the second person singular. ⚠️ Saying 'Loopt je?' or 'Werkt je?'. You must drop the -t: 'Loop je?', 'Werk je?'.

Voorbeeld

Loop je naar huis?

Are you walking home?

Waarheen / Waar... heen

Where to

📌 Grammaticanotitie

To ask about direction (where to), Dutch often uses 'Waarheen' or splits it by putting 'heen' at the end of the sentence. 'Waar ga je heen?' is very common. ⚠️ Just saying 'Waar ga je?' which sounds incomplete. Adding 'heen' clarifies that you are asking about the destination.

Voorbeeld

Waar ga je heen?

Where are you going?

Beleefde vragen met 'u'

Polite questions with 'u'

📌 Grammaticanotitie

When asking questions to strangers, elders, or in formal settings, use 'u'. Unlike 'jij', the verb keeps its '-t' ending when 'u' follows it in inversion. ⚠️ Dropping the -t with 'u' because you learned to drop it with 'jij'. Correct: 'Werkt u?' (with -t) vs 'Werk je?' (without -t).

Voorbeeld

Spreekt u Engels?

Do you speak English?

Word order and sentence structure: Asking Questions & Question Words

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Word order and sentence structure rules for Asking Questions & Question Words (A1): In main clauses, subordinate clauses, and with negation or adverbial phrases, ensure Ja/nee-vragen, Wie, Wat, Waar occupy their strict grammatical position. ⚠️ Misplacing elements relative to the conjugated verb, negation particle, or clause boundary.

Formal register vs. colloquial spoken usage: Asking Questions & Question Words

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Register and usage nuance (A1): While everyday spoken communication frequently uses conversational shortcuts, formal written texts and CEFR A1 exams require precise adherence to Asking Questions & Question Words. ⚠️ Using overly colloquial shortcuts in formal written contexts or stiff bookish phrasing where natural flow is required.

Decision framework and practical summary: Asking Questions & Question Words

Decision framework for Asking Questions & Question Words (A1): Before producing a sentence, verify 1) grammatical agreement (gender, number, person), 2) tense/mood triggers, and 3) exact word order. ⚠️ Producing clauses without checking case, mood triggers, or subject-verb agreement.
