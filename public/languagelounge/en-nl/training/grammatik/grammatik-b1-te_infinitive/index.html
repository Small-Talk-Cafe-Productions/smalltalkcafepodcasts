De basisregel: te + infinitief

The basic rule: te + infinitive

📌 Grammaticanotitie

When a second verb follows a primary verb in a sentence, it often takes the particle 'te' and is placed at the very end of the clause in its infinitive form. This is structurally similar to 'to + verb' in English. In main clauses, the conjugated verb takes the second position, and the 'te + infinitive' phrase goes to the end.

Voorbeeld

Voornemens en plannen: beginnen, plannen, proberen

Intentions and plans: beginnen, plannen, proberen

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Verbs expressing the start, planning, or attempt of an action routinely take 'te + infinitive'. Common B1 examples include 'beginnen' (to begin), 'plannen' (to plan), 'proberen' (to try), and 'besluiten' (to decide). Note that 'beginnen aan' can also take a noun, but with an action, it uses 'te'.

Voorbeeld

Mentale processen en meningen: hopen, vergeten, schijnen

Mental processes and opinions: hopen, vergeten, schijnen

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Verbs dealing with cognitive states, expectations, or perceptions—such as 'hopen' (to hope), 'vergeten' (to forget), 'schijnen' (to seem/appear), and 'wensen' (to wish)—frequently govern 'te + infinitive'. They connect a subject's mental stance to a subsequent action.

Voorbeeld

Verbuiging met 'om... te + infinitief'

Construction with 'om... te + infinitive' (purpose)

📌 Grammaticanotitie

When expressing purpose ('in order to'), Dutch uses the construction 'om' followed by the clause, ending with 'te + infinitive'. While simple 'te' indicates a direct complement, 'om... te' explicitly highlights the objective or reason behind the main clause's action.

Voorbeeld

Adjectieven met 'te': het is belangrijk om te...

Adjectives with 'te': it is important to...

📌 Grammaticanotitie

When an impersonal phrase starts with 'het is' + adjective (e.g., 'belangrijk', 'moeilijk', 'leuk', 'duur'), the accompanying action clause must be introduced by 'om... te + infinitive'. This structure standardly evaluates an activity or state.

Voorbeeld

Scheidbare werkwoorden met 'te'

Separable verbs with 'te'

📌 Grammaticanotitie

When a separable verb (like 'meenemen', 'opruimen', 'uitnodigen') takes 'te', the particle 'te' inserts itself between the separable prefix and the base verb: prefix + te + infinitive. The entire cluster stays at the very end of the clause as one single orthographic unit.

Voorbeeld

Uitzondering: Modal verbs (geen 'te')

Exception: Modal verbs (no 'te')

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Modal verbs (kunnen, moeten, willen, mogen, zullen) never take 'te' before their infinitive. The main verb goes straight to the end of the clause in its bare infinitive form. This is a crucial distinction from English, where modal equivalents sometimes require 'to'.

Voorbeeld

Uitzondering: Perceptiewerkwoorden en 'gaan/komen'

Exception: Perception verbs and 'gaan/komen'

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Verbs of perception (zien, horen, voelen) as well as verbs of movement like gaan and komen combine with a bare infinitive, omitting 'te'. The action verb simply rests at the end without any particle.

Voorbeeld

Verba van advies en dwang: durven, raden, weigeren

Verbs of advice and compulsion: durven, raden, weigeren

📌 Grammaticanotitie

Verbs indicating personal stance against risk or social guidance—such as durven (to dare), raden (to advise), weigeren (to refuse), and beloven (to promise)—govern the 'te + infinitive' construction in subordinate and main clauses.

Voorbeeld

Te + infinitief inbijzinnen (Subordinate Clauses)

Te + infinitive in subordinate clauses

📌 Grammaticanotitie

In subordinate clauses introduced by conjunctions like omdat, dat, or als, the conjugated verb moves to the very end. However, if a 'te + infinitive' structure is present, the infinitival phrase comes after the conjugated verb in that final cluster.

Voorbeeld

Vaste combinaties: 'hebben te' en 'zijn te'

Fixed combinations: 'hebben te' and 'zijn te'

📌 Grammaticanotitie

The auxiliary verbs hebben and zijn can combine with 'te + infinitive' to express necessity or possibility. Hebben te means 'have to / must' (formal), while zijn te combined with an infinitive often implies possibility ('can be done').

Voorbeeld

Te + infinitief als substantief (Zelfstandig gebruik)

Te + infinitive as a noun (Substantive usage)

📌 Grammaticanotitie

At the B1 level, you will encounter 'te + infinitive' used notionally with neutral nouns or pronouns like iets, niets, veel, or weinig, acting like an adjective modifying the pronoun. It translates roughly to 'something to do', 'nothing to eat'.
