W-Fragen: Grundstruktur

Basic Structure of W-Questions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

W-questions (W-Fragen) begin with a question word (Fragewort) starting with 'W'. The conjugated verb always stands in the second position, followed by the subject. This structure is fundamental for forming most information-seeking questions in German.

Beispielsatz

Wo wohnst du?

Where do you live?

Wer? (Nominativ)

Who? (Nominative case)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Wer?' asks for the subject of the sentence, specifically a person or people. Since it asks for the subject, it is always in the nominative case. The verb agrees with 'wer' as if it were a third-person singular pronoun (er/sie/es).

Beispielsatz

Wer kommt heute Abend?

Who is coming tonight?

Wen? (Akkusativ)

Whom? (Accusative case)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Wen?' asks for the direct object of the sentence, specifically a person or people. It is always in the accusative case. This question word is used when the person receives the action of the verb directly.

Beispielsatz

Wen siehst du?

Whom do you see? / Who do you see?

Wem? (Dativ)

To whom? (Dative case)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Wem?' asks for the indirect object of the sentence, specifically a person or people. It is always in the dative case. This question word is used when the person is the recipient of something or benefits from an action, often with dative verbs or prepositions.

Beispielsatz

Wem gibst du das Buch?

To whom are you giving the book?

Was? (Nominativ/Akkusativ)

What? (Nominative/Accusative for things)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Was?' asks for things, concepts, or actions. It can function as either the subject (nominative) or the direct object (accusative) in a sentence, depending on its role. Unlike 'wer/wen/wem', 'was' does not change its form for different cases.

Beispielsatz

Was ist das? / Was machst du?

What is that? / What are you doing?

Wo? (Ort, statisch)

Where? (Location, static)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Wo?' asks about a static location, meaning 'at what place'. It is used when there is no movement involved, only a position. Questions with 'wo' often require an answer with a dative preposition (e.g., in der Stadt, auf dem Tisch).

Beispielsatz

Wo bist du?

Where are you?

Wohin? (Richtung, Bewegung)

Where to? (Direction, movement)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Wohin?' asks about a destination or direction of movement, meaning 'to what place'. It implies an action of moving from one point to another. Answers often involve accusative prepositions (e.g., in die Stadt, auf den Tisch).

Beispielsatz

Wohin gehst du?

Where are you going (to)?

Woher? (Herkunft)

Where from? (Origin)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Woher?' asks about the place of origin, meaning 'from what place'. It is used to inquire about where someone or something comes from. Answers typically include prepositions like 'aus' or 'von'.

Beispielsatz

Woher kommst du?

Where do you come from?

Wann? (Zeitpunkt)

When? (Time)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Wann?' asks about a specific point in time or a period. It is used to inquire about the timing of an event or action. Answers can include specific dates, times, or temporal adverbs.

Beispielsatz

Wann beginnt der Film?

When does the movie start?

Wie? (Art und Weise, Qualität, Alter, Preis)

How? / What... like? (Manner, quality, age, price)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Wie?' is a highly versatile question word used to inquire about manner, quality, condition, age, price, size, and more. It can stand alone or combine with adjectives or adverbs to form specific questions. This makes it one of the most common W-words.

Beispielsatz

Wie geht es dir? / Wie alt bist du?

How are you? / How old are you?

Warum? / Wieso? / Weshalb? (Grund)

Why? (Reason)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

These three question words all ask for a reason or cause. 'Warum' is the most common and generally interchangeable with 'wieso' and 'weshalb'. Answers often begin with 'weil' (because) or 'darum/deshalb/deswegen' (therefore).

Beispielsatz

Warum lernst du Deutsch?

Why are you learning German?

Welche(r/s)? (Auswahl)

Which one? (Choice)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Welche(r/s)?' is used when asking to choose from a specific group or selection. It functions like a definite article (der/die/das) and must agree in gender, number, and case with the noun it refers to. Its endings change accordingly (e.g., welcher Mann, welche Frau, welches Kind).

Beispielsatz

Welches Buch möchtest du?

Which book would you like?

Was für ein(e/s)? (Art, Typ)

What kind of? (Kind/Type)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Was für ein(e/s)?' asks about the nature, type, or quality of something. It is used when you want to know 'what kind of' something it is. The 'ein' part declines like an indefinite article (ein/eine/ein), agreeing in gender and case with the noun.

Beispielsatz

Was für ein Auto hast du?

What kind of car do you have?

Wie viel? / Wie viele? (Menge)

How much? / How many? (Quantity)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

'Wie viel?' is used for uncountable nouns (e.g., water, money) to ask about quantity. 'Wie viele?' is used for countable nouns (e.g., books, people) to ask about number. The choice depends on whether the noun can be counted individually.

Beispielsatz

Wie viel Wasser trinkst du? / Wie viele Bücher hast du?

How much water do you drink? / How many books do you have?

W-Fragen mit Präpositionen (Wo- + Präposition für Dinge)

W-Questions with Prepositions (Wo- + Preposition for things/concepts)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When asking about things or concepts that require a preposition, German often combines 'wo-' with the preposition. If the preposition starts with a vowel, an 'r' is inserted between 'wo' and the preposition (e.g., worüber, woran, worauf). This structure is used when the object of the preposition is not a person.

Beispielsatz

Worüber sprecht ihr?

What are you talking about?

W-Fragen mit Präpositionen (Präposition + Wen/Wem für Personen)

W-Questions with Prepositions (Preposition + Whom/To whom for people)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When asking about people that require a preposition, the preposition comes before the question word 'wen' (accusative) or 'wem' (dative). The choice between 'wen' and 'wem' depends on the case required by the specific preposition. This structure is exclusively for people.

Beispielsatz

Mit wem gehst du ins Kino? / An wen denkst du?

With whom are you going to the cinema? / Whom are you thinking of?
