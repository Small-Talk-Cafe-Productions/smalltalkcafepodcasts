Das Präteritum (Simple Past)

The Simple Past tense

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Präteritum is one of two past tenses in German. While the Perfekt (Present Perfect) is generally used for spoken language, the Präteritum is more common in written language, narratives, and formal contexts. However, for the verbs 'sein' (to be) and 'haben' (to have), the Präteritum is frequently used even in spoken German, making it crucial for A1 learners.

Beispielsatz

Gestern war ich im Kino.

Yesterday I was at the cinema.

Präteritum von 'sein' (ich, er/sie/es)

Simple Past of 'to be' (I, he/she/it)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'sein' (to be) is highly irregular in the Präteritum. The forms 'ich war' and 'er/sie/es war' are identical. This form is used to describe states, conditions, or locations in the past for the first and third person singular subjects.

Beispielsatz

Ich war gestern sehr müde.

I was very tired yesterday.

Präteritum von 'sein' (du, ihr)

Simple Past of 'to be' (you singular, you plural informal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the second person singular ('du') and second person plural informal ('ihr'), the Präteritum forms of 'sein' are 'warst' and 'wart' respectively. These forms are used when addressing one person informally or a group of people informally about past states or locations.

Beispielsatz

Du warst gestern nicht da.

You were not there yesterday.

Präteritum von 'sein' (wir, sie/Sie)

Simple Past of 'to be' (we, they/you formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Präteritum forms for 'wir' (we) and 'sie/Sie' (they/you formal) are both 'waren'. This is a common pattern for many verbs in German, where the first and third person plural forms are identical to the infinitive for regular verbs, but for 'sein' it's 'waren'.

Beispielsatz

Wir waren letzte Woche im Museum.

We were at the museum last week.

Zusammenfassung: Präteritum von 'sein'

Summary: Simple Past of 'to be'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Here is the complete conjugation of 'sein' in the Präteritum. Memorizing these forms is essential as 'sein' is one of the most frequently used verbs in German. It describes past states, conditions, or locations.

Beispielsatz

Ich war krank, du warst müde, er war glücklich, wir waren Freunde, ihr wart laut, sie waren zu Hause.

I was sick, you were tired, he was happy, we were friends, you all were loud, they were at home.

Präteritum von 'haben' (ich, er/sie/es)

Simple Past of 'to have' (I, he/she/it)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'haben' (to have) is also irregular in the Präteritum, but its forms are more regular than 'sein'. The forms 'ich hatte' and 'er/sie/es hatte' are identical. This form is used to express past possession or experiences for the first and third person singular subjects.

Beispielsatz

Ich hatte gestern viel Arbeit.

I had a lot of work yesterday.

Präteritum von 'haben' (du, ihr)

Simple Past of 'to have' (you singular, you plural informal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For the second person singular ('du') and second person plural informal ('ihr'), the Präteritum forms of 'haben' are 'hattest' and 'hattet' respectively. These forms are used when addressing one person informally or a group of people informally about past possessions or experiences.

Beispielsatz

Hattest du einen schönen Urlaub?

Did you have a nice vacation?

Präteritum von 'haben' (wir, sie/Sie)

Simple Past of 'to have' (we, they/you formal)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Präteritum forms for 'wir' (we) and 'sie/Sie' (they/you formal) are both 'hatten'. This form is used for expressing past possession or experiences for plural subjects, both informal and formal.

Beispielsatz

Wir hatten gestern viel Spaß.

We had a lot of fun yesterday.

Zusammenfassung: Präteritum von 'haben'

Summary: Simple Past of 'to have'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Here is the complete conjugation of 'haben' in the Präteritum. These forms are crucial for expressing past possession, experiences, or states involving 'to have'. Remember that 'haben' in Präteritum is very common even in spoken German.

Beispielsatz

Ich hatte Hunger, du hattest Durst, er hatte Fieber, wir hatten Glück, ihr hattet Pech, sie hatten Zeit.

I was hungry, you were thirsty, he had a fever, we were lucky, you all were unlucky, they had time.

Verwendung: 'sein' im Präteritum (Zustände)

Usage: 'to be' in Simple Past (States/Conditions)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Präteritum of 'sein' is used to describe how something or someone was in the past. This includes physical states, emotional conditions, or general characteristics that existed at a specific time in the past.

Beispielsatz

Ich war als Kind sehr schüchtern.

As a child, I was very shy.

Verwendung: 'sein' im Präteritum (Ort/Existenz)

Usage: 'to be' in Simple Past (Location/Existence)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Präteritum of 'sein' also describes where someone or something was located in the past, or simply that something existed. It answers questions like 'Wo war...?' (Where was...?).

Beispielsatz

Meine Eltern waren letztes Jahr in Italien.

My parents were in Italy last year.

Verwendung: 'haben' im Präteritum (Besitz)

Usage: 'to have' in Simple Past (Possession)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The Präteritum of 'haben' is used to express that someone possessed something in the past. It's a direct equivalent to 'had' in English and is very common for describing past ownership.

Beispielsatz

Früher hatte ich ein rotes Auto.

Earlier, I had a red car.

Verwendung: 'haben' im Präteritum (Erfahrungen/Ereignisse)

Usage: 'to have' in Simple Past (Experiences/Events)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Beyond possession, 'haben' in Präteritum is also used to describe past experiences or events, often in fixed expressions. This includes having fun, having time, having a good day, or having problems.

Beispielsatz

Wir hatten gestern viel Spaß auf der Party.

We had a lot of fun at the party yesterday.

Präteritum vs. Perfekt für 'sein' und 'haben'

Simple Past vs. Present Perfect for 'to be' and 'to have'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For most verbs, Perfekt is used in spoken German and Präteritum in written German. However, for 'sein' and 'haben', the Präteritum forms ('war', 'hatte') are very common and often preferred even in spoken language, especially for simple statements. While Perfekt forms ('bin gewesen', 'habe gehabt') are grammatically correct, they sound less natural for basic statements at A1.

Beispielsatz

Ich war krank. (Common) vs. Ich bin krank gewesen. (Less common in simple statements)

I was sick. vs. I have been sick.

Häufiger Fehler: Falsche Zeitform für 'sein' und 'haben'

Common Error: Incorrect Tense for 'to be' and 'to have'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A frequent mistake is to use a mix of tenses or the wrong auxiliary verb when trying to form the past for 'sein' and 'haben'. Remember that 'sein' and 'haben' are main verbs here, and their Präteritum forms are single words. Do not combine them with 'haben' or 'sein' as auxiliaries for their own past forms.

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Ich habe war. Richtig: Ich war.

Wrong: I have was. Right: I was.

Zusammenfassung: Wann Präteritum für 'sein' und 'haben'?

Summary: When to use Simple Past for 'to be' and 'to have'?

📌 Grammatiknotiz

At A1 level, always use the Präteritum forms ('war', 'hatte') for 'sein' and 'haben' when talking about past states, possessions, or experiences. These forms are simple, direct, and widely used in both written and spoken German for these two specific verbs. Focus on memorizing their irregular conjugations.

Beispielsatz

Gestern war ich im Büro und hatte viel zu tun.

Yesterday I was in the office and had a lot to do.
