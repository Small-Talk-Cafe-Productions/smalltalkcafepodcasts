Positionsverben (Wo? - Dativ)

Position Verbs (Where? - Dative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Position verbs describe a state or location where something is. They are always intransitive, meaning they do not take a direct object. They answer the question 'Wo?' (Where?) and therefore require the Dative case for the location.

Beispielsatz

Das Buch steht im Regal.

The book is standing on the shelf.

Direktionsverben (Wohin? - Akkusativ)

Direction Verbs (Whither? - Accusative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Direction verbs describe an action of moving something to a specific location. They are always transitive, meaning they require a direct object (what is being moved). They answer the question 'Wohin?' (Whither? / To where?) and therefore require the Accusative case for the destination.

Beispielsatz

Ich stelle das Buch ins Regal.

I place the book onto the shelf.

Übersicht: Position vs. Direktion

Overview: Position vs. Direction

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This table summarizes the main pairs of position and direction verbs. Notice the pattern: position verbs are often strong (vowel change in past/participle), while their corresponding direction verbs are weak (regular past/participle endings). The choice of verb dictates the case for the location.

Beispielsatz

stehen (Wo? Dativ) / stellen (Wohin? Akkusativ)

to stand/be standing (Where? Dative) / to place upright (Whither? Accusative)

stehen (Position) - Wo?

to stand, be standing (intransitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'stehen' is a strong, intransitive verb describing a vertical state or position. It means 'to be standing' or 'to be placed upright'. It always answers 'Wo?' and takes the Dative case for the location. Its principal parts are stehen, stand, gestanden.

Beispielsatz

Der Baum steht im Garten.

The tree is standing in the garden.

liegen (Position) - Wo?

to lie, be lying (intransitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'liegen' is a strong, intransitive verb describing a horizontal state or position. It means 'to be lying' or 'to be placed flat'. It always answers 'Wo?' and takes the Dative case for the location. Its principal parts are liegen, lag, gelegen.

Beispielsatz

Die Zeitung liegt auf dem Boden.

The newspaper is lying on the floor.

sitzen (Position) - Wo?

to sit, be sitting (intransitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'sitzen' is a strong, intransitive verb describing a seated state or position. It means 'to be sitting'. It always answers 'Wo?' and takes the Dative case for the location. Its principal parts are sitzen, saß, gesessen.

Beispielsatz

Wir sitzen im Café.

We are sitting in the café.

hängen (Position) - Wo?

to hang, be hanging (intransitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'hängen' (intransitive) is a strong verb describing something suspended in a state. It means 'to be hanging'. It answers 'Wo?' and takes the Dative case for the location. Its principal parts are hängen, hing, gehangen (Perfekt: hat gehangen).

Beispielsatz

Das Bild hängt an der Wand.

The picture is hanging on the wall.

stecken (Position) - Wo?

to be stuck, be in (intransitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'stecken' (intransitive) is typically a weak verb describing something being inside or stuck in a state. It means 'to be stuck' or 'to be in'. It answers 'Wo?' and takes the Dative case for the location. Its principal parts are stecken, steckte, gesteckt (Perfekt: hat gesteckt).

Beispielsatz

Der Schlüssel steckt im Schloss.

The key is stuck in the lock.

stellen (Direction) - Wohin?

to place/set upright (transitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'stellen' is a weak, transitive verb describing the action of placing something in an upright position. It requires a direct object (Accusative) and answers 'Wohin?' for the destination, which also takes the Accusative case. Its principal parts are stellen, stellte, gestellt.

Beispielsatz

Ich stelle die Vase auf den Tisch.

I place the vase onto the table.

legen (Direction) - Wohin?

to lay down (transitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'legen' is a weak, transitive verb describing the action of placing something in a horizontal position. It requires a direct object (Accusative) and answers 'Wohin?' for the destination, which also takes the Accusative case. Its principal parts are legen, legte, gelegt.

Beispielsatz

Er legt den Stift auf das Papier.

He lays the pen onto the paper.

setzen (Direction) - Wohin?

to set/place, to seat (transitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'setzen' is a weak, transitive verb describing the action of placing someone or something in a seated position. It requires a direct object (Accusative) and answers 'Wohin?' for the destination, which also takes the Accusative case. Its principal parts are setzen, setzte, gesetzt.

Beispielsatz

Die Mutter setzt das Kind auf den Stuhl.

The mother seats the child on the chair.

hängen (Direction) - Wohin?

to hang (something) (transitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'hängen' (transitive) is a weak verb describing the action of suspending something. It requires a direct object (Accusative) and answers 'Wohin?' for the destination, which also takes the Accusative case. Its principal parts are hängen, hängte, gehängt (Perfekt: hat gehängt).

Beispielsatz

Ich hänge das Bild an die Wand.

I hang the picture onto the wall.

stecken (Direction) - Wohin?

to put/stick into (transitive)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'stecken' (transitive) is typically a weak verb describing the action of putting something inside or sticking it. It requires a direct object (Accusative) and answers 'Wohin?' for the destination, which also takes the Accusative case. Its principal parts are stecken, steckte, gesteckt (Perfekt: hat gesteckt).

Beispielsatz

Er steckt den Schlüssel ins Schloss.

He sticks the key into the lock.

Reflexiv: sich setzen (Wohin? Akkusativ)

Reflexive: to sit down (oneself) (Whither? Accusative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The verb 'setzen' can be used reflexively as 'sich setzen' to mean 'to sit oneself down'. Although it describes an action of moving oneself, it functions like an intransitive verb in that the subject is also the object. The reflexive pronoun 'sich' is always in the Accusative case, and the location still answers 'Wohin?' with Accusative.

Beispielsatz

Ich setze mich auf den Stuhl.

I sit myself down on the chair.

Wechselpräpositionen: Wo? (Dativ)

Two-way Prepositions: Where? (Dative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Two-way prepositions (an, auf, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen) are used with Dative when answering 'Wo?' (describing a fixed position or state). This applies to all position verbs (stehen, liegen, sitzen, hängen, stecken).

Beispielsatz

Der Hund liegt unter dem Tisch.

The dog is lying under the table.

Wechselpräpositionen: Wohin? (Akkusativ)

Two-way Prepositions: Whither? (Accusative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Two-way prepositions (an, auf, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen) are used with Accusative when answering 'Wohin?' (describing movement to a destination). This applies to all direction verbs (stellen, legen, setzen, hängen, stecken) and reflexive 'sich setzen'.

Beispielsatz

Ich lege den Hund unter den Tisch.

I lay the dog under the table.

Perfektbildung: Alle mit 'haben'

Perfect Tense Formation: All with 'haben'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Crucially, all these position and direction verbs (stehen, liegen, sitzen, stellen, legen, setzen, hängen, stecken) form their perfect tense with the auxiliary verb 'haben'. Position verbs describe a state (not movement from A to B), and direction verbs are transitive actions, both of which typically use 'haben' in the perfect tense.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe das Buch ins Regal gestellt.

I have placed the book onto the shelf.

Kontrast: stehen vs. stellen

Contrast: to stand/be standing vs. to place/set upright

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This pair highlights the core difference: 'stehen' describes the state of being upright (intransitive, Wo? Dativ), while 'stellen' describes the action of putting something upright (transitive, Wohin? Akkusativ). Always check if something is somewhere or if something is moved somewhere.

Beispielsatz

Die Flasche steht auf dem Tisch. / Ich stelle die Flasche auf den Tisch.

The bottle is standing on the table. / I place the bottle onto the table.

Kontrast: liegen vs. legen

Contrast: to lie/be lying vs. to lay down

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This pair distinguishes between the state of being in a horizontal position ('liegen', intransitive, Wo? Dativ) and the action of putting something into a horizontal position ('legen', transitive, Wohin? Akkusativ). Remember 'liegen' has a strong past (lag, gelegen) and 'legen' is weak (legte, gelegt).

Beispielsatz

Der Teppich liegt auf dem Boden. / Ich lege den Teppich auf den Boden.

The carpet is lying on the floor. / I lay the carpet onto the floor.

Kontrast: sitzen vs. setzen

Contrast: to sit/be sitting vs. to set/place

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This pair differentiates between the state of being seated ('sitzen', intransitive, Wo? Dativ) and the action of placing someone or something into a seated position ('setzen', transitive, Wohin? Akkusativ). 'Sitzen' has a strong past (saß, gesessen), while 'setzen' is weak (setzte, gesetzt). Don't forget the reflexive 'sich setzen' for 'to sit down'.

Beispielsatz

Die Katze sitzt auf dem Stuhl. / Ich setze die Katze auf den Stuhl.

The cat is sitting on the chair. / I place the cat onto the chair.
