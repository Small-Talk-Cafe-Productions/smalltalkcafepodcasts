Personalpronomen (Nominativ)

Personal Pronouns (Nominative Case)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Personal pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition. In the nominative case, they function as the subject of a sentence, performing the action of the verb. They are essential for forming basic sentences and must agree with the verb's conjugation.

Beispielsatz

Ich bin Student.

I am a student.

ich (Nominativ)

I (Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The pronoun 'ich' is the first person singular personal pronoun, always referring to the speaker. It acts as the subject of the sentence and requires the verb to be conjugated in the first person singular form (usually ending in -e).

Beispielsatz

Ich heiße Anna und ich komme aus Deutschland.

My name is Anna and I come from Germany.

du (Nominativ)

you (informal singular, Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The pronoun 'du' is the second person singular personal pronoun, used to address one person informally. This includes friends, family, children, or people you are on a first-name basis with. It requires the verb to be conjugated in the second person singular form (usually ending in -st).

Beispielsatz

Woher kommst du, Peter?

Where do you come from, Peter?

er (Nominativ)

he (Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The pronoun 'er' is the third person singular masculine personal pronoun. It refers to a male person or a masculine noun (e.g., 'der Mann', 'der Tisch'). It acts as the subject and requires the verb to be conjugated in the third person singular form (usually ending in -t).

Beispielsatz

Das ist Herr Müller. Er ist mein Lehrer.

That is Mr. Müller. He is my teacher.

sie (Nominativ)

she (Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The pronoun 'sie' is the third person singular feminine personal pronoun. It refers to a female person or a feminine noun (e.g., 'die Frau', 'die Blume'). It acts as the subject and requires the verb to be conjugated in the third person singular form (usually ending in -t).

Beispielsatz

Das ist Frau Schmidt. Sie kommt aus Österreich.

That is Mrs. Schmidt. She comes from Austria.

es (Nominativ)

it (Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The pronoun 'es' is the third person singular neuter personal pronoun. It refers to a neuter noun (e.g., 'das Kind', 'das Buch'). It can also be used impersonally in expressions like 'Es regnet' (It's raining). It acts as the subject and requires the verb to be conjugated in the third person singular form (usually ending in -t).

Beispielsatz

Das ist das Buch. Es ist sehr interessant.

That is the book. It is very interesting.

wir (Nominativ)

we (Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The pronoun 'wir' is the first person plural personal pronoun. It refers to the speaker and at least one other person. It acts as the subject of the sentence and requires the verb to be conjugated in the first person plural form (usually ending in -en).

Beispielsatz

Wir lernen Deutsch in diesem Kurs.

We are learning German in this course.

ihr (Nominativ)

you (informal plural, Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The pronoun 'ihr' is the second person plural personal pronoun. It is used to address two or more people informally (e.g., a group of friends or family members). It acts as the subject and requires the verb to be conjugated in the second person plural form (usually ending in -t).

Beispielsatz

Was macht ihr heute Abend, Kinder?

What are you doing tonight, kids?

sie (Nominativ)

they (Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The pronoun 'sie' is the third person plural personal pronoun. It refers to two or more people or things, regardless of their gender. It acts as the subject and requires the verb to be conjugated in the third person plural form (usually ending in -en).

Beispielsatz

Das sind meine Eltern. Sie wohnen in Berlin.

Those are my parents. They live in Berlin.

Sie (Nominativ)

you (formal singular/plural, Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The pronoun 'Sie' is the formal second person pronoun, used for both singular and plural. It is always capitalized. Use 'Sie' when addressing strangers, superiors, or in professional contexts. It acts as the subject and requires the verb to be conjugated in the third person plural form (usually ending in -en).

Beispielsatz

Sprechen Sie Englisch, Herr Meier?

Do you (formal) speak English, Mr. Meier?

Personalpronomen im Nominativ (Übersicht)

Personal Pronouns in Nominative Case (Overview)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This card summarizes all personal pronouns in the nominative case. These pronouns always act as the subject of a sentence and dictate the verb's conjugation. Mastering these forms is fundamental for basic sentence construction in German.

Beispielsatz

Ich spreche Deutsch. Du sprichst Deutsch. Er/Sie/Es spricht Deutsch. Wir sprechen Deutsch. Ihr sprecht Deutsch. Sie/sie sprechen Deutsch.

I speak German. You speak German. He/She/It speaks German. We speak German. You (pl. inf.) speak German. They/You (formal) speak German.

Personalpronomen im Akkusativ (Einführung)

Personal Pronouns in Accusative Case (Introduction)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The accusative case is used for the direct object of a verb. This means the pronoun is the receiver of the action. Many common verbs, like 'haben' (to have), 'sehen' (to see), or 'lieben' (to love), require their direct object to be in the accusative case. The pronoun changes its form to reflect this.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe dich gesehen.

I saw you.

Akkusativ-Pronomen (Formen)

Accusative Pronouns (Forms)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This card lists the specific forms of personal pronouns in the accusative case. Notice that 'ich' becomes 'mich', 'du' becomes 'dich', and 'er' becomes 'ihn'. The pronouns 'sie' (she/they), 'es' (it), and 'Sie' (formal you) remain the same as in the nominative case.

Beispielsatz

mich (me), dich (you, inf. sg.), ihn (him), sie (her), es (it), uns (us), euch (you, inf. pl.), sie (them), Sie (you, formal)

me, you (inf. sg.), him, her, it, us, you (inf. pl.), them, you (formal)

Akkusativ-Pronomen (Anwendung)

Accusative Pronouns (Application)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Accusative pronouns are used as direct objects after verbs that take an accusative object. Key verbs at A1 level include 'haben' (to have), 'sehen' (to see), 'fragen' (to ask), 'lieben' (to love), 'brauchen' (to need), 'kennen' (to know), and 'besuchen' (to visit). They answer the question 'Wen oder was?' (Whom or what?).

Beispielsatz

Ich sehe dich im Park.

I see you in the park.

Personalpronomen im Dativ (Einführung)

Personal Pronouns in Dative Case (Introduction)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The dative case is used for the indirect object of a verb or after certain dative prepositions. It often indicates 'to whom' or 'for whom' an action is performed. Many verbs, like 'helfen' (to help) or 'geben' (to give), require their indirect object to be in the dative case. All pronouns change their form in the dative.

Beispielsatz

Ich helfe dir gern.

I like to help you.

Dativ-Pronomen (Formen)

Dative Pronouns (Forms)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This card lists the specific forms of personal pronouns in the dative case. All pronouns change their form in the dative. Pay close attention to 'mir' (me), 'dir' (you, inf. sg.), 'ihm' (him/it masc./neut.), 'ihr' (her), 'uns' (us), 'euch' (you, inf. pl.), 'ihnen' (them), and 'Ihnen' (you, formal).

Beispielsatz

mir (to/for me), dir (to/for you, inf. sg.), ihm (to/for him/it), ihr (to/for her), uns (to/for us), euch (to/for you, inf. pl.), ihnen (to/for them), Ihnen (to/for you, formal)

to/for me, to/for you (inf. sg.), to/for him/it, to/for her, to/for us, to/for you (inf. pl.), to/for them, to/for you (formal)

Dativ-Pronomen (Anwendung)

Dative Pronouns (Application)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Dative pronouns are used as indirect objects after verbs that take a dative object. Common A1 verbs include 'helfen' (to help), 'geben' (to give), 'gehören' (to belong to), 'schmecken' (to taste good to), 'danken' (to thank), and 'antworten' (to answer). They answer the question 'Wem?' (To whom?).

Beispielsatz

Das Essen schmeckt mir sehr gut.

The food tastes very good to me.

sie, sie, Sie (Unterscheidung)

Distinguishing 'she', 'they', 'formal you'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

German has three distinct pronouns that look similar: 'sie' (she, 3rd pers. sg. fem.), 'sie' (they, 3rd pers. pl.), and 'Sie' (formal you, always capitalized). Context and verb conjugation are crucial for distinguishing their meaning. The verb form for 'sie' (she) is singular, while for 'sie' (they) and 'Sie' (formal you) it is plural.

Beispielsatz

Sie ist meine Schwester. Sie sind meine Eltern. Sprechen Sie Deutsch?

She is my sister. They are my parents. Do you (formal) speak German?

er, sie, es und grammatisches Geschlecht

he, she, it and Grammatical Gender

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In German, all nouns have a grammatical gender (masculine, feminine, or neuter), indicated by their definite article (der, die, das). The personal pronouns 'er', 'sie', 'es' must match the grammatical gender of the noun they refer to, not necessarily the natural gender. For example, 'das Mädchen' (the girl) is neuter, so it's referred to as 'es'.

Beispielsatz

Der Tisch ist alt. Er ist alt. Die Lampe ist neu. Sie ist neu. Das Buch ist gut. Es ist gut.

The table is old. It is old. The lamp is new. It is new. The book is good. It is good.

du, ihr, Sie (Anrede)

Informal vs. Formal Address (you)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

German distinguishes between informal and formal ways of addressing people. 'du' is used for one person informally (friends, family). 'ihr' is used for multiple people informally. 'Sie' (always capitalized) is used for one or more people formally (strangers, superiors, in business). Using the wrong form can be considered rude.

Beispielsatz

Wie geht es dir? (to a friend) Wie geht es euch? (to a group of friends) Wie geht es Ihnen? (to a stranger)

How are you? (singular informal) How are you? (plural informal) How are you? (formal)
