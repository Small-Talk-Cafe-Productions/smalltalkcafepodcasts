Was sind Possessivartikel?

What are Possessive Articles?

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Possessive articles (also called possessive pronouns in some contexts) indicate possession or belonging. They answer the question 'Wessen?' (Whose?). In German, they are declined according to the gender, number, and case of the noun they refer to, similar to indefinite articles.

Beispielsatz

Das ist mein Buch.

That is my book.

Liste der Possessivartikel (Grundformen)

List of Possessive Articles (Base Forms)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

These are the base forms of the possessive articles, corresponding to personal pronouns: ich -> mein, du -> dein, er/es -> sein, sie (she) -> ihr, wir -> unser, ihr (you plural informal) -> euer, sie (they) -> ihr, Sie (you formal) -> Ihr. Remember that these forms will change based on the noun's gender, number, and case.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe mein Handy. Du suchst dein Fahrrad. Er mag sein Haus. Sie liebt ihre Katze.

I have my cell phone. You are looking for your bicycle. He likes his house. She loves her cat.

Grundregel: Angleichung an das Nomen

Basic Rule: Agreement with the Noun

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Possessive articles must agree with the noun they describe in gender (masculine, feminine, neuter), number (singular, plural), and case (nominative, accusative, dative). This means the ending of the possessive article changes to match the noun's characteristics.

Beispielsatz

Das ist mein Stuhl (m). Das ist meine Tasche (f). Das ist mein Buch (n). Das sind meine Freunde (pl).

That is my chair. That is my bag. That is my book. Those are my friends.

Nominativ: Maskulin & Neutrum (keine Endung)

Nominative: Masculine & Neuter (no ending)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the nominative case, possessive articles for masculine and neuter singular nouns do not receive an ending. They appear in their base form (e.g., mein, dein, sein). This applies when the noun is the subject of the sentence or follows 'sein' or 'werden'.

Beispielsatz

Mein Vater ist Arzt. Dein Buch ist interessant.

My father is a doctor. Your book is interesting.

Nominativ: Feminin & Plural (-e Endung)

Nominative: Feminine & Plural (-e ending)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the nominative case, possessive articles for feminine singular nouns and all plural nouns receive an '-e' ending (e.g., meine, deine, seine). This ending is consistent for both feminine singular and all plural forms in the nominative case.

Beispielsatz

Meine Mutter ist Lehrerin. Meine Kinder spielen.

My mother is a teacher. My children are playing.

Akkusativ: Maskulin (-en Endung)

Accusative: Masculine (-en ending)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a masculine singular noun is in the accusative case (often as a direct object or after an accusative preposition), its possessive article receives an '-en' ending (e.g., meinen, deinen, seinen). This is a crucial change to remember for masculine nouns.

Beispielsatz

Ich sehe meinen Bruder. Er kauft seinen neuen Computer.

I see my brother. He buys his new computer.

Akkusativ: Feminin, Neutrum & Plural (wie Nominativ)

Accusative: Feminine, Neuter & Plural (like Nominative)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For feminine singular, neuter singular, and all plural nouns in the accusative case, the possessive article forms are identical to their nominative forms. Feminine and plural take '-e', neuter takes no ending. This is because the definite articles 'die' (f/pl) and 'das' (n) do not change in accusative.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe meine Tasche (f). Ich habe mein Buch (n). Ich habe meine Schlüssel (pl).

I have my bag. I have my book. I have my keys.

Dativ: Maskulin & Neutrum (-em Endung)

Dative: Masculine & Neuter (-em ending)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the dative case, possessive articles for masculine and neuter singular nouns receive an '-em' ending (e.g., meinem, deinem, seinem). This often occurs after dative prepositions like 'mit', 'von', 'zu' or with dative verbs like 'helfen'.

Beispielsatz

Ich fahre mit meinem Auto. Er gibt seinem Freund ein Geschenk.

I drive with my car. He gives his friend a gift.

Dativ: Feminin (-er Endung)

Dative: Feminine (-er ending)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For feminine singular nouns in the dative case, the possessive article receives an '-er' ending (e.g., meiner, deiner, seiner). This is similar to the definite article 'der' for feminine dative, making it easier to remember.

Beispielsatz

Ich gehe zu meiner Schwester. Er hilft seiner Mutter.

I go to my sister. He helps his mother.

Dativ: Plural (-en Endung + Nomen -n)

Dative: Plural (-en ending + noun -n)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

For all plural nouns in the dative case, the possessive article receives an '-en' ending (e.g., meinen, deinen, seinen). Additionally, the plural noun itself often takes an extra '-n' ending, unless it already ends in -n or -s (e.g., 'Mädchen' or 'Autos').

Beispielsatz

Ich spreche mit meinen Freunden. Er gibt seinen Kindern Spielzeug.

I speak with my friends. He gives his children toys.

Sonderfall: euer / eure

Special Case: euer / eure (your, informal plural)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The possessive article 'euer' (your, informal plural) drops the 'e' before the 'r' when an ending is added. For example, 'euer' becomes 'eure' (Nom. Fem./Pl.), 'euren' (Acc. Masc.), 'eurem' (Dat. Masc./Neu.), 'eurer' (Dat. Fem.).

Beispielsatz

Das ist euer Haus. Das sind eure Kinder. Ich sehe euren Hund.

That is your house. Those are your children. I see your dog.

Possessivartikel im Überblick (mein)

Possessive Articles Overview (mein)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

This table summarizes the declension of 'mein' as an example. All other possessive articles (dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, ihr, Ihr) follow the exact same pattern of endings. Memorize these endings!

Beispielsatz

Nominativ: m: mein Vater f: meine Mutter n: mein Kind pl: meine Eltern Akkusativ: m: meinen Vater f: meine Mutter n: mein Kind pl: meine Eltern Dativ: m: meinem Vater f: meiner Mutter n: meinem Kind pl: meinen Eltern

Nominative: m: my father f: my mother n: my child pl: my parents Accusative: m: my father f: my mother n: my child pl: my parents Dative: m: my father f: my mother n: my child pl: my parents

Vergleich: Possessivartikel vs. Indefinitartikel

Comparison: Possessive Articles vs. Indefinite Articles

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Possessive articles decline exactly like indefinite articles (ein, eine, ein). They share the same endings for nominative, accusative, and dative cases. If you know how to decline 'ein', you know how to decline possessive articles!

Beispielsatz

Nominativ: ein Mann / mein Mann eine Frau / meine Frau ein Kind / mein Kind Akkusativ: einen Mann / meinen Mann eine Frau / meine Frau ein Kind / mein Kind

Nominative: a man / my man a woman / my woman a child / my child Accusative: a man / my man a woman / my woman a child / my child

Häufiger Fehler: Verwechslung von 'sein' und 'ihr'

Common Error: Confusing 'sein' (his/its) and 'ihr' (her/their)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The possessive article 'sein' refers to a masculine or neuter owner (he/it). 'Ihr' refers to a feminine owner (she) or plural owners (they). Pay close attention to the owner's gender/number, not just the owned item's gender.

Beispielsatz

Der Mann hat sein Auto. (His car) Die Frau hat ihr Auto. (Her car) Die Kinder haben ihr Auto. (Their car)

The man has his car. The woman has her car. The children have their car.

Häufiger Fehler: 'Ihr' (formal) vs. 'ihr' (informal plural)

Common Error: 'Ihr' (formal 'your') vs. 'ihr' (informal plural 'your')

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The formal 'your' (singular or plural) is always capitalized: 'Ihr', 'Ihre', 'Ihrem', etc. The informal plural 'your' (addressing multiple friends) is lowercase: 'ihr', 'ihre', 'ihrem', etc. Context and capitalization are key to distinguish them.

Beispielsatz

Herr Müller, ist das Ihr Auto? (Formal) Kinder, ist das euer Auto? (Informal plural)

Mr. Müller, is that your car? (Formal) Children, is that your car? (Informal plural)

Häufiger Fehler: Falsche Endungen

Common Error: Incorrect Endings

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A very frequent mistake is using the wrong ending or no ending at all. Always identify the noun's gender, number, and the sentence's case. Then apply the correct ending based on the declension rules. Practice makes perfect!

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Ich sehe mein Freund. Richtig: Ich sehe meinen Freund. (Accusative masculine)

Wrong: I see my friend. Correct: I see my friend.
