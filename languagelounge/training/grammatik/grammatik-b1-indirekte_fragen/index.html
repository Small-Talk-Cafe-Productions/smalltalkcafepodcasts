Was sind indirekte Fragen?

What are indirect questions?

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Indirect questions are subordinate clauses that function as the object of a main clause. They are introduced by an interrogative pronoun (W-word) or by 'ob' (if/whether) and always have the conjugated verb at the very end of the clause. They allow you to embed a question within a statement or another question.

Beispielsatz

Ich frage mich, wann der Zug ankommt.

I wonder when the train arrives.

Die Satzstellung: Verb am Ende

Sentence Structure: Verb at the End

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The most important rule for indirect questions is that the conjugated verb always moves to the very end of the subordinate clause. This applies to all types of indirect questions, regardless of whether they are introduced by 'ob' or a W-word. This structure is typical for all German subordinate clauses.

Beispielsatz

Können Sie mir sagen, wie spät es ist?

Can you tell me what time it is?

Indirekte Ja/Nein-Fragen mit 'ob'

Indirect Yes/No Questions with 'ob' (if/whether)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

If the original direct question is a yes/no question (i.e., it doesn't use a W-word), the indirect question is introduced by 'ob'. 'Ob' acts as a conjunction connecting the main clause to the subordinate clause. The verb then moves to the end of the 'ob'-clause.

Beispielsatz

Ich möchte wissen, ob das Geschäft heute geöffnet ist.

I would like to know if the shop is open today.

Indirekte W-Fragen

Indirect W-Questions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

If the original direct question uses a W-word (wer, was, wann, wo, warum, wie, etc.), the indirect question is introduced by that same W-word. The W-word acts as the conjunction, and the conjugated verb moves to the very end of the subordinate clause. The W-word always comes immediately after the main clause.

Beispielsatz

Ich frage mich, wann der nächste Zug fährt.

I wonder when the next train departs.

Häufige Einleitungen (Aussagesätze)

Common Introductory Phrases (Statements)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Indirect questions are often introduced by phrases that express curiosity, uncertainty, or a desire for information. These phrases are typically statements and include expressions like 'Ich frage mich, ...' (I wonder...), 'Ich möchte wissen, ...' (I would like to know...), 'Es ist unklar, ...' (It is unclear...), or 'Ich weiß nicht, ...' (I don't know...).

Beispielsatz

Ich frage mich, wie lange die Reise dauert.

I wonder how long the journey takes.

Häufige Einleitungen (Fragesätze)

Common Introductory Phrases (Questions)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Indirect questions can also be introduced by direct questions, asking someone else for information. Common phrases include 'Wissen Sie, ...?' (Do you know...?), 'Können Sie mir sagen, ...?' (Can you tell me...?), or 'Haben Sie eine Ahnung, ...?' (Do you have any idea...?). The main clause is a question, but the indirect question still follows subordinate clause word order.

Beispielsatz

Wissen Sie, ob der Supermarkt noch geöffnet ist?

Do you know if the supermarket is still open?

Indirekte Fragen mit Modalverben

Indirect Questions with Modal Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When an indirect question contains a modal verb, the modal verb is conjugated and moves to the very end of the subordinate clause. The infinitive verb that accompanies the modal verb precedes the modal verb, forming a verb bracket at the end of the clause. This is consistent with the general rule for subordinate clauses.

Beispielsatz

Ich möchte wissen, ob ich hier parken darf.

I would like to know if I am allowed to park here.

Indirekte Fragen mit trennbaren Verben

Indirect Questions with Separable Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In indirect questions, separable verbs are treated like any other verb in a subordinate clause. The separable prefix rejoins the verb, and the entire verb (prefix + stem) moves to the very end of the clause as a single unit. The verb is conjugated according to the subject.

Beispielsatz

Ich frage mich, wann der Film anfängt.

I wonder when the movie starts.

Indirekte Fragen im Perfekt

Indirect Questions in Perfect Tense

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When an indirect question is in the perfect tense (Perfekt), the auxiliary verb (haben or sein) is conjugated and moves to the very end of the subordinate clause. The past participle (Partizip II) precedes the auxiliary verb, forming a verb bracket at the end. This maintains the verb-final rule for subordinate clauses.

Beispielsatz

Ich weiß nicht, was er gestern gemacht hat.

I don't know what he did yesterday.

Indirekte Fragen im Passiv

Indirect Questions in Passive Voice

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In indirect questions using the passive voice, the conjugated form of 'werden' (the auxiliary for passive) moves to the very end of the subordinate clause. The past participle (Partizip II) precedes 'werden', forming a verb bracket. This structure ensures the verb-final rule is followed while maintaining the passive construction.

Beispielsatz

Er fragt, ob das Paket schon geliefert wurde.

He asks if the package has already been delivered.

Indirekte Fragen mit Präpositionen + W-Wörtern

Indirect Questions with Prepositions + W-words

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a direct question involves a preposition, the preposition often combines with the W-word (e.g., 'worüber', 'womit', 'wovon') or precedes 'wem/wen/was'. In an indirect question, this prepositional phrase (e.g., 'worüber' or 'mit wem') acts as the W-word and introduces the subordinate clause. The verb still goes to the end.

Beispielsatz

Ich frage mich, worüber sie gesprochen haben.

I wonder what they talked about.

Indirekte Fragen mit 'was für ein/eine'

Indirect Questions with 'what kind of a/an'

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The phrase 'was für ein/eine/einen/eines' acts as a single interrogative unit. When used in an indirect question, the entire phrase 'was für ein/eine...' introduces the subordinate clause. The noun following 'ein/eine' remains with the phrase, and the conjugated verb moves to the end of the clause, as always.

Beispielsatz

Ich möchte wissen, was für ein Auto er fährt.

I would like to know what kind of car he drives.

Negation in indirekten Fragen

Negation in Indirect Questions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To negate an indirect question, 'nicht' is typically placed before the part of the sentence it negates. If it negates the entire verb or clause, it usually comes before the verb at the end, or before the object if the object is indefinite. If it negates a specific element (e.g., an adjective or adverb), it precedes that element.

Beispielsatz

Ich weiß nicht, ob er heute Abend nicht kommt.

I don't know if he isn't coming tonight.

Indirekte Fragen vs. Relativsätze

Indirect Questions vs. Relative Clauses

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Both indirect questions and relative clauses are subordinate clauses with verb-final word order, which can cause confusion. However, their function differs. Indirect questions ask for information and are introduced by 'ob' or a W-word. Relative clauses provide additional information about a noun and are introduced by a relative pronoun (der, die, das, welcher, welche, welches).

Beispielsatz

Ich weiß nicht, wer das gesagt hat. (Indirekte Frage)

I don't know who said that. (Indirect Question)

Häufiger Fehler: 'ob' oder W-Wort vergessen

Common Error: Forgetting 'ob' or the W-word

📌 Grammatiknotiz

A very common mistake is to omit the introductory word ('ob' for yes/no questions or the W-word for W-questions). These words are crucial conjunctions that link the main clause to the subordinate indirect question. Without them, the sentence structure is grammatically incorrect and often unclear.

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Ich frage mich, der Zug ankommt. / Richtig: Ich frage mich, wann der Zug ankommt.

Wrong: I wonder the train arrives. / Right: I wonder when the train arrives.

Häufiger Fehler: Falsche Verbposition

Common Error: Incorrect Verb Position

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The most frequent error in indirect questions is failing to place the conjugated verb at the very end of the subordinate clause. Learners often apply the main clause word order (verb in second position) or place it directly after the subject. Always remember: in indirect questions, the verb is always last.

Beispielsatz

Falsch: Ich weiß nicht, wann kommt der Bus. / Richtig: Ich weiß nicht, wann der Bus kommt.

Wrong: I don't know when comes the bus. / Right: I don't know when the bus comes.

Indirekte Fragen mit Konjunktiv I/II (optional)

Indirect Questions with Subjunctive I/II (optional)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

While B1 typically uses the indicative mood in indirect questions, especially when the information is presented as fact, Konjunktiv I or II can be used to indicate reported speech, doubt, or distance from the statement. Konjunktiv I is for reported speech, Konjunktiv II for doubt or hypothetical situations. For B1, using the indicative is generally sufficient and correct.

Beispielsatz

Er fragt, ob sie mitkäme. (Konjunktiv II, indicating doubt)

He asks if she would come along. (Subjunctive II, indicating doubt)

Indirekte Fragen mit doppelten Konjunktionen

Indirect Questions with Double Conjunctions

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Sometimes, an introductory phrase might itself contain a conjunction, but this does not replace the 'ob' or W-word of the indirect question. For example, 'Ich bin nicht sicher, ob...' (I am not sure if...). Here, 'ob' is the conjunction for the indirect question, and 'nicht sicher' is part of the main clause. The structure remains consistent: Main clause + comma + 'ob'/W-word + subject + ... + verb.

Beispielsatz

Ich bin nicht sicher, ob er heute kommt.

I am not sure if he is coming today.
