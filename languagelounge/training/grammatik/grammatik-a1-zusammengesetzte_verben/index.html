Zusammengesetzte Verben (Compound Verbs)

Introduction to Compound Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Compound verbs in German are formed by combining a base verb with a prefix (Vorsilbe). These prefixes often change the meaning of the base verb significantly, creating new verbs. Understanding them is crucial for building vocabulary and forming correct sentences.

Beispielsatz

aufstehen (to stand up), weggehen (to go away), anrufen (to call)

These are examples of verbs formed by a prefix and a base verb.

Trennbare Verben (Separable Verbs)

Definition and Key Feature

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Separable verbs are compound verbs where the prefix 'separates' from the verb stem in main clauses (Hauptsätze) in the present and simple past tenses. The prefix moves to the very end of the sentence, while the conjugated verb stem stays in the second position. This separation is their defining characteristic.

Beispielsatz

Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.

I stand up at 7 o'clock.

Häufige trennbare Vorsilben (Common Separable Prefixes)

List of common separable prefixes

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Many prefixes are always separable. It's important to learn them by heart, as their separability affects sentence structure. Common examples include: ab-, an-, auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, nach-, vor-, weg-, zu-, her-, hin-, da-, fest-, los-, vorbei-, zurück-.

Beispielsatz

abfahren (to depart), ankommen (to arrive), aufstehen (to stand up), ausgehen (to go out), einkaufen (to shop)

Examples of verbs with common separable prefixes.

Trennbare Verben im Hauptsatz (Präsens)

Separable Verbs in Main Clauses (Present Tense)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In a main clause in the present tense, the base verb is conjugated and placed in the second position of the sentence. The separable prefix moves to the very end of the sentence. This structure is fundamental for separable verbs and must be applied consistently.

Beispielsatz

Ich rufe meine Freundin jeden Abend an.

I call my girlfriend every evening.

Trennbare Verben mit 'zu' + Infinitiv

Separable Verbs with 'zu' + Infinitive

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a separable verb is used with 'zu' + infinitive (e.g., after verbs like 'versuchen', 'vergessen', or adjectives like 'schön'), the 'zu' is placed between the prefix and the verb stem. This forms a single, hyphenated word: prefix-zu-verb, which is then placed at the end of the clause.

Beispielsatz

Es ist wichtig, pünktlich anzukommen.

It is important to arrive on time.

Trennbare Verben im Partizip II (Perfekt)

Separable Verbs in Past Participle (Perfect Tense)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To form the past participle (Partizip II) of separable verbs, 'ge-' is inserted between the prefix and the verb stem. The auxiliary verb (haben/sein) is conjugated and placed in the second position, and the Partizip II goes to the end of the sentence. This unique formation is key.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe gestern meine Freundin angerufen.

I called my girlfriend yesterday.

Trennbare Verben mit Modalverben

Separable Verbs with Modal Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When a separable verb is used with a modal verb (können, müssen, wollen, dürfen, sollen, mögen), the separable verb remains in its infinitive form. Crucially, the prefix does not separate and stays attached to the verb stem. Both the modal verb (conjugated) and the infinitive (with prefix) are placed at the end of the sentence.

Beispielsatz

Ich muss morgen früh aufstehen.

I have to get up early tomorrow.

Trennbare Verben im Imperativ

Separable Verbs in the Imperative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the imperative (commands), the conjugated verb stem is placed at the beginning of the sentence, and the separable prefix moves to the very end, just like in a regular main clause. Remember to use the correct imperative form of the verb (du, ihr, Sie).

Beispielsatz

Steh bitte pünktlich auf!

Please get up on time!

Untrennbare Verben (Inseparable Verbs)

Definition and Key Feature

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Inseparable verbs are compound verbs where the prefix never separates from the verb stem, regardless of the tense or sentence structure. The prefix always remains attached to the verb, forming a single unit that functions like a regular verb. These prefixes are typically unstressed.

Beispielsatz

Ich verstehe die Frage nicht.

I don't understand the question.

Häufige untrennbare Vorsilben (Common Inseparable Prefixes)

List of common inseparable prefixes

📌 Grammatiknotiz

These prefixes are always inseparable and are never stressed in pronunciation. It's important to memorize them to avoid incorrect separation. Common examples include: be-, emp-, ent-, er-, ge-, miss-, ver-, zer-. These prefixes often indicate a change of state or completion.

Beispielsatz

bekommen (to receive), empfehlen (to recommend), entscheiden (to decide), erklären (to explain)

Examples of verbs with common inseparable prefixes.

Untrennbare Verben im Hauptsatz (Präsens)

Inseparable Verbs in Main Clauses (Present Tense)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In a main clause in the present tense, the inseparable verb is conjugated normally, and the prefix remains firmly attached to the verb stem. The entire verb unit (prefix + conjugated stem) is placed in the second position, just like any regular verb. There is no separation.

Beispielsatz

Ich verstehe den Text sehr gut.

I understand the text very well.

Untrennbare Verben mit 'zu' + Infinitiv

Inseparable Verbs with 'zu' + Infinitive

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When an inseparable verb is used with 'zu' + infinitive, the 'zu' is placed before the entire inseparable verb. The prefix remains attached to the verb stem, forming a single unit with 'zu' preceding it. This is different from separable verbs where 'zu' goes inside.

Beispielsatz

Es ist schwer, alles zu verstehen.

It is difficult to understand everything.

Untrennbare Verben im Partizip II (Perfekt)

Inseparable Verbs in Past Participle (Perfect Tense)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

To form the past participle (Partizip II) of inseparable verbs, no 'ge-' is added. The prefix remains attached to the verb stem, and the ending is typically '-t' or '-en'. The auxiliary verb (haben/sein) is conjugated and placed in the second position, with the Partizip II at the end.

Beispielsatz

Ich habe den Text gut verstanden.

I understood the text well.

Untrennbare Verben mit Modalverben

Inseparable Verbs with Modal Verbs

📌 Grammatiknotiz

When an inseparable verb is used with a modal verb, it remains in its infinitive form. The prefix stays attached to the verb stem, and the entire infinitive verb is placed at the end of the sentence, just like with regular verbs. The modal verb is conjugated in the second position.

Beispielsatz

Ich kann dich morgen besuchen.

I can visit you tomorrow.

Untrennbare Verben im Imperativ

Inseparable Verbs in the Imperative

📌 Grammatiknotiz

In the imperative, inseparable verbs behave like regular verbs. The conjugated verb form (with its attached prefix) is placed at the beginning of the sentence. The prefix never separates from the verb stem, maintaining its inseparable nature even in commands.

Beispielsatz

Versteh mich bitte richtig!

Please understand me correctly!

Trennbare vs. Untrennbare Verben (Zusammenfassung)

Separable vs. Inseparable Verbs (Summary)

📌 Grammatiknotiz

The key difference lies in the prefix's behavior. Separable prefixes detach in main clauses (Präsens/Präteritum) and for Partizip II ('ge-' in between). Inseparable prefixes never detach and Partizip II is formed without 'ge-'. Pay close attention to the prefix itself to identify the type and apply the correct rules.

Beispielsatz

Ich rufe an. (separable) vs. Ich verstehe. (inseparable)

I call. vs. I understand.

Bedeutungsänderung durch Vorsilben

Meaning Change through Prefixes

📌 Grammatiknotiz

Prefixes often drastically change the meaning of the base verb, creating entirely new vocabulary. For example, 'gehen' (to go) becomes 'weggehen' (to go away), 'aufgehen' (to rise/open), 'eingehen' (to enter/agree). Learning these new meanings is essential for vocabulary expansion and comprehension.

Beispielsatz

sehen (to see) -> ansehen (to look at), fernsehen (to watch TV), übersehen (to overlook)

The base verb 'sehen' gets different meanings with different prefixes.
